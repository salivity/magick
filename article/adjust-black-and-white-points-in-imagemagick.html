Adjust Black and White Points in ImageMagick

Adjusting the black and white points of an image in ImageMagick allows you to expand or compress dynamic range, correct exposure, and enhance contrast. This guide explains how to use the -level operator to set precise black, white, and midtone (gamma) levels directly from the command line across various image formats.

The -level Command Syntax

The basic syntax for adjusting levels in ImageMagick is:

magick input.jpg -level <black_point>,<white_point>,<gamma> output.jpg

Note: For ImageMagick version 6, replace magick with convert.

Parameter Breakdown

  • black_point: The pixel intensity that will be mapped to pure black (0). Any values below this threshold are clipped to black.
  • white_point: The pixel intensity that will be mapped to pure white (maximum value). Any values above this threshold are clipped to white.
  • gamma (optional): Controls the midtones. A value of 1.0 represents a linear transition. Values greater than 1.0 brighten midtones, while values less than 1.0 darken them. If omitted, gamma defaults to 1.0.

Using Percentages (Recommended)

Specifying values as percentages makes commands portable across different bit depths (8-bit, 16-bit) without needing to calculate raw quantum values.

Increasing Contrast

To cut off low-end shadows and high-end highlights, increase the black point and lower the white point:

magick input.jpg -level 10%,90% output.jpg

In this example, pixels at or below 10% brightness become 0% (pure black), and pixels at or above 90% brightness become 100% (pure white).

Darkening Shadows (Adjust Black Point Only)

To make shadows deeper without altering the highlight threshold:

magick input.jpg -level 15%,100% output.jpg

Brightening Highlights (Adjust White Point Only)

To make highlights brighter without altering the shadow threshold:

magick input.jpg -level 0%,85% output.jpg

Adjusting Black Point, White Point, and Gamma Together

To set custom black and white points while brightening the midtones:

magick input.jpg -level 10%,90%,1.4 output.jpg

Using Absolute Values

You can also use absolute pixel values based on the quantum range of your ImageMagick build (typically 0–255 for Q8 or 0–65535 for Q16).

For an 8-bit image:

magick input.jpg -level 25,230 output.jpg

Inverting the Adjustments with +level

While -level compresses dynamic range into pure black and pure white, the +level operator (using a plus sign) does the reverse. It compresses the full range into a narrower subset, preventing pure black and pure white:

magick input.jpg +level 10%,90% output.jpg

In this case, pure black becomes 10% gray, and pure white becomes 90% gray, reducing overall contrast.
