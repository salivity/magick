Adjust White Balance in ImageMagick Using Levels

This article provides a concise guide on how to adjust and correct the white balance of an image using ImageMagick's level operations. You will learn how to identify color casts, manipulate individual red, green, and blue channels using the -level operator, and apply automated level adjustments to achieve natural, balanced color tones in your photographs.

Understanding ImageMagick Level Operations

White balance issues occur when an image has an unnatural color cast caused by ambient lighting (such as yellow tungsten light or blue overcast light). ImageMagick's -level operator adjusts the black point, white point, and gamma (midtones) of an image. By applying level adjustments to individual color channels, you can increase or decrease specific colors across the shadows, midtones, and highlights to neutralize color casts.

The general syntax for channel-specific level adjustments is:

magick input.jpg -channel <Channel> -level <BlackPoint>%,<WhitePoint>%,<Gamma> +channel output.jpg
  • BlackPoint%: The value mapped to pure black (0%).
  • WhitePoint%: The value mapped to pure white (100%).
  • Gamma: Midtone adjustment value (1.0 is neutral; values greater than 1.0 lighten midtones, values less than 1.0 darken them).
  • +channel: Resets the channel selector so subsequent operations affect all channels.

Method 1: Automatic White Balance with -auto-level

The fastest way to neutralize a color cast is by stretching each RGB channel to its full dynamic range independently. This applies the "Gray World" assumption, mapping the darkest pixel in each channel to black and the brightest to white.

magick input.jpg -auto-level output.jpg

This command automatically calculates and applies the necessary level corrections per channel, eliminating strong color casts in high-contrast images.

Method 2: Manual Channel Level Correction

If an automated balance alters the image too aggressively, adjust individual channels manually using gamma shifts to balance color temperatures.

Correcting a Warm (Yellow/Orange) Tint

To cool down a warm photo, lower the red midtones and increase the blue midtones:

magick input.jpg -channel R -level 0%,100%,0.9 +channel -channel B -level 0%,100%,1.1 +channel output.jpg

Correcting a Cool (Blue) Tint

To warm up a blue-tinted photo, reduce the blue midtones and increase the red midtones:

magick input.jpg -channel B -level 0%,100%,0.9 +channel -channel R -level 0%,100%,1.1 +channel output.jpg

Correcting a Green/Magenta Tint

To correct a greenish cast, adjust the green channel gamma:

magick input.jpg -channel G -level 0%,100%,0.9 +channel output.jpg

Method 3: Targeted White Point Normalization

When you have a known white or neutral gray area in your photo that is discolored, you can balance the image by adjusting the white points of each channel to match.

  1. Sample the color cast on a target white area using the crop and txt: format: 
    magick input.jpg -crop 1x1+X+Y -format "%[fx:mean.r],%[fx:mean.g],%[fx:mean.b]" info:
  2. Apply the measured maximums as the white points for each channel. For example, if the sampled "white" pixel measures Red at 95%, Green at 90%, and Blue at 80%: 
    magick input.jpg -channel R -level 0%,95% +channel -channel G -level 0%,90% +channel -channel B -level 0%,80% +channel output.jpg

This aligns the maximum values of each channel to pure white (100%), neutralizing the overall color cast across the entire image.
