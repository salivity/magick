Auto-Crop Scanned Photos Using ImageMagick

Scanning multiple photo prints simultaneously on a flatbed scanner saves time, but separating them into individual image files can be tedious. ImageMagick can automate this process by detecting the background color of the scanner bed, isolating the distinct photo boundaries, and exporting each print into its own cropped and straightened file.

The Auto-Crop Command

In ImageMagick 7, extracting multiple individual photos from a single scan is accomplished using connected-components analysis to detect objects and crop them.

Run the following command in your terminal:

magick input_scan.jpg \
  -deskew 40% \
  \( +clone -fuzz 10% -fill white -draw "color 0,0 floodfill" \
     -fuzz 10% -fill black +opaque white \
     -define connected-components:verbose=true \
     -define connected-components:area-threshold=5000 \
     -connected-components 8 -write /dev/null \) \
  -crop "%[connected-components:output]" +repage photo_%d.jpg

(Note: On Windows systems, replace /dev/null with NUL and remove the backslashes \ used for line continuation).

How the Command Works

  • -deskew 40%: Automatically straightens slightly tilted images before segmentation.
  • +clone: Creates a temporary working copy of the image to build a segmentation mask without altering the original colors.
  • -fuzz 10% -fill white -draw "color 0,0 floodfill": Selects the scanner background color starting from the top-left corner (0,0) and unifies it into solid white. Adjust the fuzz percentage higher if your scanner bed has noticeable dust or lighting variations.
  • -fill black +opaque white: Turns everything that is not the background into solid black rectangles, generating a high-contrast binary mask.
  • -define connected-components:area-threshold=5000: Filters out dust, specks, and small artifacts by ignoring detected regions smaller than 5,000 pixels.
  • -connected-components 8: Analyzes the continuous shapes on the mask and calculates their exact bounding box dimensions.
  • -crop "%[connected-components:output]" +repage: Applies the calculated bounding boxes back to the original image and trims out each detected photograph.
  • photo_%d.jpg: Saves each extracted photograph as a sequential file (e.g., photo_0.jpg, photo_1.jpg).

Tips for Best Results

  1. Maintain Spacing: Ensure at least 0.5 inches (1–2 cm) of empty space between photos on the flatbed glass so their borders do not overlap.
  2. Keep the Lid Clean: Clean the scanner glass and the underside of the lid to prevent dust from creating false photo borders.
  3. Contrast: Ensure the background lid color contrasts sufficiently with the edges of the photo prints (e.g., a white scanner lid for dark borders, or a dark backing sheet if scanning polaroids with white borders).
