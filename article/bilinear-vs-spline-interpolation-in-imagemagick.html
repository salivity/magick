Bilinear vs Spline Interpolation in ImageMagick

When transforming or scaling images in ImageMagick, the visual difference between Bilinear and Spline interpolation modes lies in the smoothness and continuity of pixel transitions. Bilinear interpolation calculates values using simple linear formulas between the four closest neighboring pixels, often producing visible directional creases and a faceted appearance upon magnification. In contrast, Spline interpolation fits higher-order cubic curves across the pixel grid, yielding softer, curved gradients that eliminate linear grid artifacts and result in a more organic, continuous image.

Surface Smoothness and Faceting

Bilinear interpolation treats the space between pixels as flat, linear ramps along both axes. Under heavy magnification or non-affine warping, this produces distinct "tent-like" or diamond-shaped artifacts. Highlights and sharp transitions take on a polygonal, faceted appearance with noticeable seams along pixel boundaries.

Spline interpolation eliminates these polygonal seams entirely. By using cubic polynomial curves to transition between pixels, it ensures continuous first and second derivatives across the surface. Gradients appear rounded and seamless, which prevents the faceted, geometric look common to Bilinear processing.

Edge Transitions and Contours

The two methods render high-contrast boundaries differently:

  • Bilinear: Creates straight, piecewise-linear slopes across edge boundaries. While this avoids ringing (halo artifacts), it causes curved edges and diagonal lines to look slightly flattened or pinched at regular intervals.
  • Spline: Renders edges with smooth, S-shaped tonal curves. Diagonal lines and circular contours maintain their curvature without bending abruptly into linear segments, though the edge may appear slightly softer overall due to the broader blending radius of the cubic formula.

Gradient Uniformity

In continuous-tone areas such as skies, skin, or soft drop shadows, Bilinear interpolation can introduce subtle cross-hatch or diamond banding patterns where the linear slopes meet. Spline interpolation maintains continuous tone across the entire span, distributing value shifts evenly and producing photographic gradients that look significantly more natural to the human eye.
