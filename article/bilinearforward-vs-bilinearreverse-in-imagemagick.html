BilinearForward vs BilinearReverse in ImageMagick

In ImageMagick, BilinearForward and BilinearReverse are two variations of bilinear quadrilateral distortion used to map four coordinate points from a source image to four coordinate points in a destination image. The primary difference lies in the direction of the mathematical transformation equations, which dictates whether straight grid lines are preserved in the source or the destination space, as well as the computational complexity required to render the output.

The Rendering Challenge: Forward vs. Inverse Mapping

Raster graphics renderers iterate over the pixels of the destination image and look backward to sample the appropriate color from the source image. This avoids holes or overlapping pixels. However, the mathematical definition of a bilinear transformation can be written in two opposite directions.

BilinearForward Mapping

In BilinearForward distortion, the mathematical equation is defined to map source coordinates \((X_s, Y_s)\) to destination coordinates \((X_d, Y_d)\):

\[X_d = c_0 + c_1 X_s + c_2 Y_s + c_3 X_s Y_s\] \[Y_d = c_4 + c_5 X_s + c_6 Y_s + c_7 X_s Y_s\]

  • Mechanism: Because the renderer must determine the source coordinates for every destination pixel, ImageMagick must mathematically invert these equations for every pixel processed. This requires solving a system of quadratic equations involving square roots.
  • Visual Behavior: Straight lines along the source image's coordinate axes remain straight lines inside the distorted quadrilateral.
  • Performance: Computationally heavier due to the per-pixel quadratic root-finding algorithm.
  • Ideal Use Case: Mapping a standard rectangular image onto an arbitrary quadrilateral target (such as plastering a poster onto a tilted wall) where you want the interior lines of the source image to remain straight.

BilinearReverse Mapping

In BilinearReverse distortion, the equation is inverted in its definition, mapping destination coordinates \((X_d, Y_d)\) directly to source coordinates \((X_s, Y_s)\):

\[X_s = c_0 + c_1 X_d + c_2 Y_d + c_3 X_d Y_d\] \[Y_s = c_4 + c_5 X_d + c_6 Y_d + c_7 X_d Y_d\]

  • Mechanism: Because the equation directly calculates source coordinates from destination coordinates, no complex inversion is required during rendering. The renderer simply evaluates the polynomial directly for each pixel.
  • Visual Behavior: Straight lines in the destination image correspond to straight lines in the source image. As a result, straight lines originally in the source image will bend into parabolic curves when mapped onto an irregular destination quadrilateral.
  • Performance: Computationally fast and direct.
  • Ideal Use Case: Extracting and "de-skewing" an arbitrary quadrilateral region from a source photo into a flat, rectangular output image.

Summary Comparison

  • Use BilinearForward when you are pushing a rectangle into an arbitrary 4-point quadrilateral and must preserve the straightness of internal source lines.
  • Use BilinearReverse when you are pulling an arbitrary 4-point area out of an image into a rectangle, or when rendering performance is critical and curved interpolation across the quad is acceptable.
