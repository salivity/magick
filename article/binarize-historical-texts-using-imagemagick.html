Binarize Historical Texts Using ImageMagick

This article provides the most effective ImageMagick commands to binarize degraded historical documents, manuscripts, and faded texts. You will learn how to eliminate background stains, correct uneven illumination, remove ink bleed-through, and convert complex document images into clean, black-and-white outputs optimized for digital archiving and Optical Character Recognition (OCR).

The Recommended Command (Background Division & Thresholding)

Standard global thresholding fails on historical texts due to paper aging, foxing, and shadows. The most robust single command uses background division (flat-field correction) combined with thresholding:

magick input.jpg -colorspace Gray ( +clone -blur 0x25 ) -compose Divide_Src -composite -normalize -threshold 85% output.png

(Note: If using ImageMagick 6, replace magick with convert.)

How It Works

  • -colorspace Gray: Converts the image to grayscale to focus solely on luminance values.
  • ( +clone -blur 0x25 ): Clones the grayscale image and applies a large Gaussian blur to estimate the uneven background and lighting variations without capturing fine text details.
  • -compose Divide_Src -composite: Divides the original image by the blurred background, effectively neutralizing background discoloration, stains, and shadows.
  • -normalize: Spans the contrast across the full dynamic range.
  • -threshold 85%: Converts the normalized image to pure black and white. Text darker than the threshold becomes black, and everything else becomes white.

Alternative: Local Adaptive Thresholding (LAT)

For heavily degraded documents with varying ink density, Local Adaptive Thresholding evaluates a moving pixel window:

magick input.jpg -colorspace Gray -negate -lat 25x25+10% -negate output.png
  • -lat 25x25+10%: Evaluates a 25x25 pixel window around each pixel. A pixel is turned white if it is at least 10% brighter than the local average.
  • -negate: Inverts colors before and after -lat because ImageMagick's LAT implementation natively looks for light text on dark backgrounds.

Fine-Tuning Parameters

  • Adjusting blur radius (0x25): Increase the radius (e.g., 0x35 or 0x50) for larger text, or decrease it (e.g., 0x15) for very small text and fine script.
  • Adjusting threshold percentage (85%): Increase to 90% if faint text strokes are breaking apart; decrease to 75% or 80% if background noise or bleed-through remains visible.
  • Adjusting window size (25x25): The window size in LAT should roughly match twice the width of the document's stroke size.
