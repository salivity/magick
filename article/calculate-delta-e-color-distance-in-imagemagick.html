Calculate Delta E Color Distance in ImageMagick

Delta E (\(\Delta E\)) measures the perceived difference between two colors based on human vision, typically evaluated within the CIELAB color space. This guide explains how to calculate Delta E (specifically CIE76) between corresponding pixels of two images using ImageMagick. You will learn how to produce both a visual per-pixel difference map and a quantitative statistical measurement across your images.

Understanding Delta E in ImageMagick

Standard image difference algorithms (like Mean Squared Error in RGB) treat all color channels uniformly without accounting for human visual perception. Delta E resolves this by converting RGB coordinates into the perceptual CIELAB (\(L^*a^*b^*\)) space.

The standard CIE76 formula computes the Euclidean distance between two Lab colors:

\[\Delta E^*_{ab} = \sqrt{(L_2 - L_1)^2 + (a_2 - a_1)^2 + (b_2 - b_1)^2}\]

ImageMagick can perform this calculation either as a single overall value using the compare tool or on a pixel-by-pixel basis to output a Delta E map.

Method 1: Generating a Per-Pixel Delta E Map

To calculate the Euclidean color distance for every corresponding pixel and output a visual heat map or data image, use the -fx operator or composite arithmetic in ImageMagick 7.

Using the -fx Operator

The -fx command processes each pixel mathematically. When images are converted to Lab space, ImageMagick normalizes the channels internally between 0.0 and 1.0. To reflect standard Lab scaling (\(L^* \in [0, 100]\), \(a^*, b^* \in [-128, 127]\)), apply channel multipliers:

magick image1.png image2.png -colorspace Lab -fx "
  dL = (u.r - v.r) * 100;
  da = (u.g - v.g) * 255;
  db = (u.b - v.b) * 255;
  sqrt(dL*dL + da*da + db*db) / 100
" delta_e_map.png
  • u refers to image1.png and v refers to image2.png.
  • u.r, u.g, and u.b represent the \(L^*\), \(a^*\), and \(b^*\) channels, respectively.
  • Dividing by 100 scales the Delta E values into a viewable grayscale range where brighter pixels represent higher perceptual differences.

Method 2: Measuring Global Delta E Using compare

If you need a single statistical number representing the color distance across the entire image, use the compare command with the CIELAB colorspace and the RMSE (Root Mean Square Error) metric:

magick compare -metric RMSE -colorspace Lab image1.png image2.png diff_visual.png

This outputs two values to the standard error stream:

  1. The normalized distance (range 0.0 to 1.0).
  2. The raw value in parentheses (dependent on your ImageMagick build's quantum depth, such as 8-bit or 16-bit).

To interpret this as a rough average CIE76 \(\Delta E\):

  • Multiply the normalized decimal value by 100 to get an approximation of the average Delta E across all pixels.

Method 3: Fast Channel Separation with Pure Arithmetic

The -fx operator can be slow on large images. For better performance on high-resolution files, separate the channels, perform difference arithmetic, and combine them using composite operators:

magick image1.png -colorspace Lab lab1.mpc
magick image2.png -colorspace Lab lab2.mpc

magick lab1.mpc lab2.mpc -compose Difference -composite \
  -separate \
  \( -clone 0 -evaluate Multiply 100 \) \
  \( -clone 1 -evaluate Multiply 255 \) \
  \( -clone 2 -evaluate Multiply 255 \) \
  -delete 0-2 \
  \( -clone 0 -clone 0 -compose Multiply -composite \) \
  \( -clone 1 -clone 1 -compose Multiply -composite \) \
  \( -clone 2 -clone 2 -compose Multiply -composite \) \
  -delete 0-2 \
  -evaluate-sequence Add \
  -evaluate Pow 0.5 \
  delta_e_distance.tiff

rm lab1.mpc lab1.cache lab2.mpc lab2.cache

Using 32-bit floating point output (such as .tiff with HDRI enabled) ensures raw Delta E numbers are preserved without clipping differences greater than standard display limits.
