Calculate Shape Convexity and Eccentricity in ImageMagick

This guide provides a straightforward methodology for calculating the convexity and eccentricity of 2D shapes using ImageMagick. By generating binary masks, retrieving spatial image moments, and measuring geometric boundaries relative to convex hulls, you can quantify morphological properties directly through the command line or within automated shell scripts.

1. Preparing the Binary Mask

To compute shape descriptors accurately, first isolate the target object into a clean, binary (black and white) image where the shape is white (foreground) and the background is black.

magick input.png -colorspace Gray -threshold 50% -negate binary_shape.png

Ensure the object of interest is fully segmented without stray noise.

2. Calculating Eccentricity

Eccentricity measures how much a shape deviates from being circular, ranging from 0 (a perfect circle) to approaching 1 (a straight line). In digital image processing, eccentricity is calculated using central image moments.

Extracting Spatial Moments

ImageMagick provides the -moments operator to compute second-order central moments and ellipse axes:

magick binary_shape.png -moments moments.txt

Alternatively, output the moments directly to the console:

magick identify -verbose -moments binary_shape.png

Look for the Ellipse Axis values (Major Axis \(a\) and Minor Axis \(b\)) or the Centroid Moments in the output.

Formula and Calculation

Using the major axis (\(a\)) and minor axis (\(b\)), calculate eccentricity (\(e\)) with the formula:

\[e = \sqrt{1 - \frac{b^2}{a^2}}\]

You can compute this directly using ImageMagick's internal FX expression engine by querying the moment properties:

magick binary_shape.png -format "%[fx:sqrt(1 - pow(%[moments:ellipse.axis.y]/%[moments:ellipse.axis.x], 2))]\n" info:

(Note: Ensure that the denominator corresponds to the larger axis).

3. Calculating Convexity

Convexity is the measure of a shape’s topological compactness, defined as the ratio between the area of the shape itself and the area of its minimum convex hull (the smallest convex polygon that completely encloses the shape):

\[\text{Convexity} = \frac{\text{Area}_{\text{shape}}}{\text{Area}_{\text{convex\_hull}}}\]

Step A: Measure Shape Area

Calculate the pixel count of the target shape:

SHAPE_AREA=$(magick binary_shape.png -format "%[fx:mean*w*h]" info:)
echo "Shape Area: $SHAPE_AREA"

Step B: Generate the Convex Hull

While ImageMagick does not have a single-flag convex hull generator, you can produce the minimum bounding convex polygon using ImageMagick's morphological operations or connected-component analysis combined with external polygon extraction (such as potrace or a Delaunay triangulator).

Within native ImageMagick, an approximation can be obtained through iterative morphological closing or by extracting the boundary coordinates via connected components:

magick binary_shape.png -define connected-components:verbose=true \
  -connected-components 8 null:

Step C: Compute the Ratio

Once the area of the convex hull is determined (HULL_AREA), compute the final convexity index:

CONVEXITY=$(magick -format "%[fx:$SHAPE_AREA / $HULL_AREA]" info:)
echo "Convexity: $CONVEXITY"

A value of 1.0 indicates a completely convex object (e.g., a circle, square, or convex polygon), while lower values indicate indented or complex contours.
