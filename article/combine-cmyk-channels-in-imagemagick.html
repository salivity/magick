Combine CMYK Channels in ImageMagick

Merging individual Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, and Black (CMYK) channel separations into a single composite full-color image is a straightforward task in ImageMagick. This guide covers the exact command-line syntax required to combine four grayscale channel files into a unified CMYK image file or an sRGB file suitable for screen display.

Basic Command to Merge CMYK Channels

To combine four separate grayscale channel images into a single composite CMYK image, provide the separation files in the exact order of Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, and Black (K), and apply the -set colorspace CMYK and -combine operators.

For ImageMagick v7:

magick cyan.tif magenta.tif yellow.tif black.tif -set colorspace CMYK -combine output_cmyk.tif

For ImageMagick v6 (using the legacy convert tool):

convert cyan.tif magenta.tif yellow.tif black.tif -set colorspace CMYK -combine output_cmyk.tif

Merging and Converting Directly to sRGB

If your final destination is a standard screen format like JPEG or PNG, you should convert the combined CMYK color data into the sRGB color space in the same command:

magick cyan.tif magenta.tif yellow.tif black.tif -set colorspace CMYK -combine -colorspace sRGB output_rgb.jpg

Key Considerations

  • Channel Order: ImageMagick assigns channels strictly in the sequence they are passed on the command line. You must supply them in standard C-M-Y-K order.
  • Inverted Plates: Prepress separation plates sometimes store ink density where white represents 100% ink coverage and black represents 0%. If your combined output looks inverted or photographic-negative, add the -negate operator before combining:
magick cyan.tif magenta.tif yellow.tif black.tif -negate -set colorspace CMYK -combine output_cmyk.tif
  • Color Profiles: For precise color matching in commercial printing workflows, apply an official ICC/ICM profile using -profile /path/to/profile.icc after combining the channels.
