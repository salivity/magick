Compute Convexity and Eccentricity in ImageMagick

This article provides a practical guide on calculating shape convexity and eccentricity using ImageMagick's command-line analysis tools. By leveraging thresholding, connected-components labeling, morphological operators, and image moment calculations, you can extract precise geometric descriptors from binary object masks directly in your terminal or automation pipeline.

1. Preparing the Binary Mask

Before computing geometric properties, convert your target image into a clean binary mask where the shape of interest is white (foreground) and the background is black.

magick input.png -auto-threshold OTSU -negate mask.png

If the shape already has a clear foreground, adjust thresholding or color extraction accordingly to ensure an accurate silhouette.

2. Computing Shape Eccentricity

Eccentricity measures the elongation of a shape. A circle has an eccentricity of 0, while a line segment approaches 1. It is derived from the second-order central moments of the shape.

Step A: Extract Central Moments

ImageMagick 7 computes image moments using the -moments operator:

magick mask.png -moments moments.txt

Alternatively, extract raw moments directly via the format string:

magick mask.png -format "%[moments:I1] %[moments:I2]" info:

Step B: Derive Semi-Major and Semi-Minor Axes

For connected components, ImageMagick can directly report the ellipse parameters (major and minor axes) of the shape:

magick mask.png -define connected-components:verbose=true \
  -define connected-components:area-threshold=100 \
  -connected-components 8 null:

The output yields the bounding box, centroid, and major/minor axes of the fitted equivalent ellipse:

  • \(a\) = Semi-major axis length
  • \(b\) = Semi-minor axis length

Step C: Calculate Eccentricity (\(e\))

Apply the standard eccentricity formula:

\[e = \sqrt{1 - \frac{b^2}{a^2}}\]

In a bash shell script, compute the final value using bc:

# Example values: a = 50, b = 25
a=50
b=25
eccentricity=$(echo "scale=4; sqrt(1 - ($b^2 / $a^2))" | bc -l)
echo "Eccentricity: $eccentricity"

3. Computing Shape Convexity

Convexity (also known as solidity) is the ratio of the object's actual area to the area of its convex hull:

\[\text{Convexity} = \frac{\text{Area}_{\text{shape}}}{\text{Area}_{\text{ConvexHull}}}\]

Step A: Calculate the Shape Area

Count the total number of foreground pixels in the binary mask:

shape_area=$(magick mask.png -format "%[fx:int(mean*w*h)]" info:)
echo "Shape Area: $shape_area"

Step B: Generate the Convex Hull and Calculate Its Area

ImageMagick can approximate the convex hull using morphological operations or construct polygon hulls via the -morphology operator:

magick mask.png -morphology ConvexHull Square hull.png
hull_area=$(magick hull.png -format "%[fx:int(mean*w*h)]" info:)
echo "Hull Area: $hull_area"

Step C: Compute the Ratio

Divide the original shape area by the convex hull area:

convexity=$(magick identify -format "%[fx:$shape_area/$hull_area]" info:)
echo "Convexity: $convexity"

A value of 1.0 denotes a completely convex object (e.g., a circle or solid rectangle), whereas values significantly below 1.0 indicate deep concavities, indentations, or complex structural contours.
