Compute Geometric Mean in ImageMagick

Calculating the geometric mean across multiple image exposures reduces noise and blends bracketed photos by determining the \(n\)-th root of the product of \(n\) images. ImageMagick accomplishes this efficiently by combining the -evaluate-sequence multiply operation with an exponentiation step using -evaluate pow. This guide outlines the precise syntax required to execute this computation using ImageMagick's command-line tools.

The Standard Command Syntax

To compute the geometric mean, load your exposure sequence, multiply all frames together, and raise the resulting composite to the power of \(1/n\) (where \(n\) is the total number of images).

For a set of three images, the syntax is:

magick input1.tif input2.tif input3.tif -evaluate-sequence multiply -evaluate pow 0.333333 output.tif

For \(n\) images, replace 0.333333 with the decimal value of \(1/n\):

  • 2 images: -evaluate pow 0.5
  • 4 images: -evaluate pow 0.25
  • 5 images: -evaluate pow 0.2

Automatic Calculation in ImageMagick 7

ImageMagick 7 allows you to compute the exponent dynamically based on the number of input files (%n) using inline percent escapes and mathematical expressions:

magick exposure_*.tif -evaluate-sequence multiply -evaluate pow "%[fx:1/%n]" output.tif

This command automatically reads all matching files, multiplies their pixel values across identical coordinates, calculates \(1/n\), and applies the exponent to yield the correct geometric mean without requiring manual calculation.

Important Considerations

  • HDRI Support: Ensure your ImageMagick installation has High Dynamic Range Imaging (HDRI) enabled (standard in ImageMagick 7). When multiplying pixel values represented as normalized floats between 0.0 and 1.0, precision will quickly degrade under standard integer builds.
  • Zero Values: Pixels containing absolute black (0.0) in any exposure will cause the geometric mean at that coordinate to evaluate to zero. If your exposures contain clipped shadow data, normalize or lift the black level slightly prior to multiplication.
