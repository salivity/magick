Compute Image Center of Mass in ImageMagick

This guide explains how to calculate the center of mass (spatial centroid) of an isolated image subject using ImageMagick from the command line. By converting your isolated subject into a high-contrast binary mask and extracting image moments or connected-component data, you can quickly retrieve precise X and Y coordinates representing the subject's center of gravity.

1. Prepare the Image Mask

To ensure an accurate center of mass calculation, your subject should be isolated as a foreground object (white, value 1) on a solid background (black, value 0).

If your subject is already on a transparent background, extract the alpha channel to create a clean silhouette:

magick input.png -alpha extract -threshold 0 mask.png

If the subject is on a plain white background, invert it so the subject becomes the non-zero region:

magick input.png -colorspace Gray -negate -threshold 10% mask.png

2. Calculate the Centroid Using Image Moments

ImageMagick 7 provides a built-in -moments operator that computes spatial and central image moments, including the exact centroid coordinates (x, y).

Run the following command directly on your isolated mask:

magick mask.png -moments -format "Center of Mass: X=%[moments:centroid.x], Y=%[moments:centroid.y]\n" info:

This outputs the calculated pixel coordinates corresponding to the center of mass of the white pixels in the image.

3. Alternative: Using Connected Components

You can also compute the centroid using the connected components feature, which measures isolated regions and outputs their bounding geometry alongside their centroids.

magick mask.png -define connected-components:verbose=true \
  -define connected-components:auto-threshold=true \
  -connected-components 4 null:

Look for the line in the terminal output representing your foreground object (the line with the gray(255) or white label). The output includes the centroid coordinates in the format (x,y) along with the object's pixel area and bounding box.

4. Verifying the Result Visually

To verify the calculated coordinates, you can mark the calculated center of mass directly onto the original image:

X=150.4
Y=210.8

magick input.png -fill red -stroke black -strokewidth 1 \
  -draw "circle $X,$Y $(($X+4)),$Y" output_verified.png

This overlays a red dot at the calculated center of mass, confirming the accurate spatial alignment of your subject.
