Compute Image Stack Standard Deviation in ImageMagick

Computing the per-pixel standard deviation across a stack of images in ImageMagick reveals regions of high variance, motion, or sensor noise across time or exposures. Because ImageMagick does not include a direct standard deviation operator for image sequences, the calculation is performed using the mathematical variance identity: \(\sigma = \sqrt{\text{E}[X^2] - (\text{E}[X])^2}\). This guide details the exact ImageMagick commands required to calculate per-pixel standard deviation, explains the necessity of High Dynamic Range Imaging (HDRI), and covers output formatting.

Requirement: HDRI (High Dynamic Range Imaging)

To calculate per-pixel standard deviation accurately, ImageMagick must be compiled with HDRI enabled. ImageMagick 7 has HDRI enabled by default. HDRI prevents pixel values from being clamped to integers in the range \([0, \text{QuantumRange}]\) during intermediate subtraction and squaring operations.

You can verify your ImageMagick build has HDRI enabled by running:

magick -version

Look for HDRI in the Features line of the output.

The Single-Command Implementation

Using ImageMagick 7, you can load the entire sequence of images, calculate both the mean of the squares (\(\text{E}[X^2]\)) and the square of the mean (\((\text{E}[X])^2\)), subtract them to obtain the variance, and apply a square root:

magick input_*.png \
  \( -clone 0--1 -evaluate Pow 2 -evaluate-sequence Mean \) \
  \( -clone 0--2 -evaluate-sequence Mean -evaluate Pow 2 \) \
  -delete 0--3 \
  -compose Subtract -composite \
  -evaluate Pow 0.5 \
  stddev.tiff

Breakdown of the Operations

  1. magick input_*.png: Loads the stack of input images into memory (indexed from \(0\) to \(N-1\)).
  2. \( -clone 0--1 -evaluate Pow 2 -evaluate-sequence Mean \): Clones all input images, squares every pixel value in each image (\(X^2\)), and merges the stack into a single image representing the mean of the squared pixels (\(\text{E}[X^2]\)). This image is placed at index \(N\).
  3. \( -clone 0--2 -evaluate-sequence Mean -evaluate Pow 2 \): Clones the original input images (indices \(0\) through \(N-1\), referencing them up to index \(-2\) to exclude the image generated in the previous step). It computes their mean (\(\text{E}[X]\)) and squares that single result to yield \((\text{E}[X])^2\). This is placed at index \(N+1\).
  4. -delete 0--3: Deletes all original input images (\(0\) to \(N-1\)), leaving only the two intermediate results: \(\text{E}[X^2]\) and \((\text{E}[X])^2\).
  5. -compose Subtract -composite: Subtracts the square of the mean from the mean of the squares, resulting in the per-pixel variance (\(\sigma^2\)).
  6. -evaluate Pow 0.5: Calculates the square root of the variance image to produce the final per-pixel standard deviation (\(\sigma\)).

Output Considerations

Standard deviation values represent the degree of variation. For sequences with subtle variations, the raw output pixel values may be very low and appear nearly black if saved directly to standard 8-bit formats like PNG or JPEG.

  • For scientific or data-preservation needs: Save to a floating-point format such as 32-bit TIFF (-define quantum:format=floating-point stddev.tiff) or OpenEXR (stddev.exr).
  • For visual inspection: Append -auto-level immediately before the output filename to stretch the minimum and maximum standard deviation values across the full visible dynamic range:
magick input_*.png \
  \( -clone 0--1 -evaluate Pow 2 -evaluate-sequence Mean \) \
  \( -clone 0--2 -evaluate-sequence Mean -evaluate Pow 2 \) \
  -delete 0--3 \
  -compose Subtract -composite \
  -evaluate Pow 0.5 \
  -auto-level \
  stddev_visible.png
