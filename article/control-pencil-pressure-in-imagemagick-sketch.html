Control Pencil Pressure in ImageMagick Sketch

This article explains how to control simulated pencil pressure using ImageMagick's -sketch operator. It details the specific geometry parameter responsible for stroke intensity, breaks down the complete operator syntax, and demonstrates practical command-line examples for creating subtle or bold pencil effects.

The Pencil Pressure Parameter

In ImageMagick, the sigma parameter controls the simulated pencil pressure when using the -sketch operator.

The complete syntax for the operator requires a geometry string:

-sketch {radius}x{sigma}+{angle}

While radius determines the reach of the blurring effect and angle defines the direction of the pencil strokes, sigma governs the intensity and density of the lines.

How Sigma Influences the Result

  • Low Sigma Values (e.g., 0.5 to 1.0): Produce faint, light strokes, mimicking a hard pencil (such as a 2H or 4H) applied with very light pressure.
  • Higher Sigma Values (e.g., 3.0 or greater): Generate darker, thicker, and more saturated lines, simulating a soft pencil (such as a 4B or 6B) applied with heavy pressure.
  • A Sigma of 0: Disables the pressure effect, resulting in an extremely faint, baseline edge trace.

Command Examples

To apply a light-pressure sketch with strokes tilted at a 45-degree angle:

magick convert input.jpg -sketch 0x1.0+45 light_pressure.jpg

To simulate heavy pencil pressure with darker, more pronounced strokes:

magick convert input.jpg -sketch 0x5.0+45 heavy_pressure.jpg

Setting radius to 0 allows ImageMagick to automatically calculate the optimal radius based on your chosen sigma, making sigma the primary control for the visual weight and pressure of the sketch.
