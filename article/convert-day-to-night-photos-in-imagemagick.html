Convert Day to Night Photos in ImageMagick

This article provides the command-line syntax and parameter settings needed to transform daytime outdoor photographs into realistic nighttime scenes using ImageMagick. By adjusting brightness, desaturating colors, applying a cool blue color cast, and manipulating contrast, you can replicate the classic cinematic "day-for-night" effect directly from your terminal.

The Basic Day-to-Night Command

In modern ImageMagick (version 7+), the basic syntax to convert a daytime photograph into a night scene is:

magick input.jpg -modulate 45,35,100 -fill "#0e1e38" -tint 60% -gamma 0.85 output.jpg

For ImageMagick version 6, replace magick with convert:

convert input.jpg -modulate 45,35,100 -fill "#0e1e38" -tint 60% -gamma 0.85 output.jpg

Syntax and Parameter Breakdown

The transformation relies on four distinct steps combined in a single execution:

  • -modulate 45,35,100: Modifies the Brightness, Saturation, and Hue (in that order).
    • 45% Brightness: Drops the daylight exposure significantly to simulate ambient moonlight or dusk.
    • 35% Saturation: Drains the vibrant daytime colors. Human eyes perceive less color saturation in low light.
    • 100% Hue: Leaves the base color spectrum intact before tinting.
  • -fill "#0e1e38": Selects a deep, dark navy blue color to represent nighttime ambient light.
  • -tint 60%: Applies the selected blue fill across the midtones and highlights at a 60% strength, creating a cool, moonlit atmosphere.
  • -gamma 0.85: Darkens the midtones further while keeping shadow areas deep and rich.

Advanced Syntax with Dynamic Contrast

For images with bright skies or direct sunlight, use the -level operator to deepen the black levels and compress dynamic range:

magick input.jpg -modulate 50,30,100 -fill "#112244" -tint 65% -level 5%,95%,0.8 night_scene.jpg
  • -level 5%,95%,0.8: Sets the black point to 5% (turning very dark grays into pure black), the white point to 95%, and applies a gamma correction of 0.8.

Creating a Moonlit High-Contrast Look

If you need sharper, moonlit highlights alongside dark shadows, multiply the image with a blue color layer and adjust brightness-contrast:

magick input.jpg -modulate 60,40,100 ( +clone -fill "#1b2a4a" -colorize 100% ) -compose multiply -composite -brightness-contrast -15x20 moonlit_scene.jpg
  • ( +clone -fill "#1b2a4a" -colorize 100% ): Creates an in-memory solid dark-blue layer.
  • -compose multiply -composite: Blends the blue layer over the base image using a multiply blend mode to darken highlights naturally.
  • -brightness-contrast -15x20: Drops the overall brightness by 15% and increases contrast by 20% to produce distinct moonlit edges.
