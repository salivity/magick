Convert Fax to PDF with ImageMagick CCITT Compression

This article outlines how to convert black-and-white fax documents into highly compressed PDF files using ImageMagick. By utilizing CCITT Group 4 (or Group 3) fax compression, you can preserve the crisp text of scanned or received faxes while significantly reducing the final PDF file size.

The Conversion Command

For ImageMagick v7, use the magick command:

magick input_fax.tif -monochrome -compress Group4 output.pdf

For ImageMagick v6, use the convert command:

convert input_fax.tif -monochrome -compress Group4 output.pdf

Command Parameters Explained

  • input_fax.tif: The source fax file. ImageMagick natively supports single-page and multi-page TIFF, PNG, or other image formats.
  • -monochrome: Forces the image to a strictly 1-bit black-and-white format. CCITT compression requires a bi-level (1-bit) image and will fail or produce errors if the source retains grayscale or color data.
  • -compress Group4: Applies CCITT Group 4 compression, which is the most efficient and standard lossless compression scheme for bitonal fax and document PDFs. Alternatively, you can use -compress Group3 or -compress Fax for standard CCITT Group 3 compression.
  • output.pdf: The target PDF file.

Handling Multi-Page Faxes and Custom Resolutions

If your source fax files have specific non-square fax resolutions (such as standard fax resolution of 204x98 DPI or fine fax resolution of 204x196 DPI), you can preserve the proper aspect ratio by explicitly specifying the density:

magick -density 204x196 input_fax.tif -monochrome -compress Group4 output.pdf

To combine multiple single-page fax images into one multi-page PDF document:

magick page1.tif page2.tif page3.tif -monochrome -compress Group4 combined_fax.pdf

Alternatively, you can convert all TIFF files in a folder into a single PDF using wildcards:

magick *.tif -monochrome -compress Group4 combined_fax.pdf
