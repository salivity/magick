Create 3D Anaglyph Images Using ImageMagick

Creating a red-cyan 3D anaglyph image involves merging two horizontally offset photos—representing the left and right eye perspectives—into a single stereoscopic image. This guide explains how to use the command-line image processing tool ImageMagick to isolate the color channels from your left and right source photos and combine them into a 3D image viewable with standard red-cyan glasses.

Prerequisites

To follow this guide, you need:

  1. ImageMagick installed on your system (ImageMagick 7+ is recommended).
  2. Left image (left.jpg): Captured from the left eye's viewpoint.
  3. Right image (right.jpg): Captured from the right eye's viewpoint, shifted horizontally by a few centimeters. Both images must have identical dimensions.

Method 1: The Quick Stereo Composite

ImageMagick provides a built-in -stereo operator designed specifically for creating anaglyphs.

Run the following command:

magick composite -stereo +0 left.jpg right.jpg anaglyph.jpg

Note: For ImageMagick v6, replace magick composite with composite.

This command takes the red channel from left.jpg and the green and blue (cyan) channels from right.jpg, automatically merging them with zero pixel offset into anaglyph.jpg.

Method 2: Precise Channel Separation and Combination

For finer control over color management and channel processing, extract the specific color channels directly using the magick command:

magick \( left.jpg -channel R -separate \) \( right.jpg -channel G -separate \) \( right.jpg -channel B -separate \) -combine anaglyph.jpg

How this works:

  • ( left.jpg -channel R -separate ) extracts the red channel from the left photo.
  • ( right.jpg -channel G -separate ) extracts the green channel from the right photo.
  • ( right.jpg -channel B -separate ) extracts the blue channel from the right photo.
  • -combine joins the three grayscale channel masks into a single RGB output image.

Tips for Best 3D Results

  • Alignment: Ensure vertical alignment between the two source photos is precise. Any vertical drift causes eye strain.
  • Horizontal Shift: If the 3D effect feels too intense or inverted, adjust the horizontal offset in the composite command (e.g., -stereo +5 or -stereo -5), or verify that the left and right source files are not swapped.
  • Grayscale Conversion: To reduce color ghosting (retinal rivalry), convert both source images to grayscale before combining:
magick \( left.jpg -colorspace Gray \) \( right.jpg -colorspace Gray \) \( -clone 0 -clone 1 -clone 1 \) -delete 0,1 -combine anaglyph_mono.jpg
