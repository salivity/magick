Create a Cyanotype Blueprint Effect with ImageMagick

This guide explains how to transform standard digital photographs into classic Prussian blue cyanotype prints and architectural-style blueprints using ImageMagick. By converting images to grayscale, boosting contrast, and mapping tones to custom blue and off-white color values, you can achieve an authentic historical printing effect directly from your command line.

The Basic Photographic Cyanotype

A traditional cyanotype print is characterized by deep Prussian blue shadows and bright, slightly off-white highlights.

To convert an image to a photographic cyanotype using ImageMagick v7, run the following command:

magick input.jpg -colorspace Gray -auto-level -level 5%,95% +level-colors "#0d2b45","#eef4f8" cyanotype.jpg

(If you are using ImageMagick v6, replace magick with convert.)

How the Command Works

  • -colorspace Gray: Strips the original color data and converts the image to monochrome.
  • -auto-level: Stretches the tonal range so the darkest pixel becomes black and the brightest becomes white.
  • -level 5%,95%: Increases contrast by clipping extreme highlights and shadows, mimicking the high dynamic contrast of contact printing.
  • +level-colors "#0d2b45","#eef4f8": Replaces black with a deep Prussian blue (#0d2b45) and white with a soft highlight tone (#eef4f8), mapping all intermediate gray tones smoothly between them.

High-Contrast Technical Blueprint Style

If you want an architectural or schematic blueprint effect rather than a photographic print, you can invert the lines and force high contrast.

Use this command for technical drawings, line art, or graphics:

magick input.jpg -colorspace Gray -negate -lat 20x20+5% +level-colors "#003366","#ffffff" technical_blueprint.jpg

Parameter Breakdown for Technical Style

  • -negate: Inverts the image so dark lines become bright lines on a dark background.
  • -lat 20x20+5%: Applies Local Adaptive Thresholding to extract clean edge lines while suppressing uneven background lighting.
  • +level-colors "#003366","#ffffff": Maps the dark background to classic blueprint cobalt blue (#003366) and the lines to crisp white (#ffffff).

Adding Paper Texture and Aging

To give the cyanotype an authentic aged appearance, you can introduce slight grain using the noise filter:

magick input.jpg -colorspace Gray -auto-level +level-colors "#0a2239","#f0f3f4" -attenuate 0.3 +noise Gaussian vintage_cyanotype.jpg
  • -attenuate 0.3 +noise Gaussian: Adds subtle grain across the midtones, simulating the uneven chemical absorption of watercolor paper.
