Create Satellite Composites with ImageMagick

This guide explains how to combine separate grayscale multi-spectral satellite image bands into natural and false-color RGB composites using ImageMagick. Multi-spectral sensors like Landsat and Sentinel capture raw data as individual grayscale channels across distinct wavelengths. By stacking, ordering, and stretching these bands with ImageMagick’s command-line tools, you can produce clear, visually balanced composite imagery ready for analysis or publication.

1. Preparing and Ordering Your Bands

ImageMagick builds RGB images sequentially by assigning the first input file to the Red channel, the second to the Green channel, and the third to the Blue channel.

Before combining, identify the band numbers corresponding to your target satellite:

  • Landsat 8/9 True Color (Natural): Band 4 (Red), Band 3 (Green), Band 2 (Blue)
  • Landsat 8/9 False Color (NIR/Vegetation): Band 5 (NIR), Band 4 (Red), Band 3 (Green)
  • Sentinel-2 True Color (Natural): Band 4 (Red), Band 3 (Green), Band 2 (Blue)
  • Sentinel-2 False Color (NIR/Vegetation): Band 8 (NIR), Band 4 (Red), Band 3 (Green)

2. Creating a Basic True-Color Composite

Use the -combine operator to merge three single-channel grayscale GeoTIFFs into an RGB image.

magick B04.tif B03.tif B02.tif -combine true_color.tif

3. Normalizing and Enhancing Contrast

Raw multi-spectral satellite bands are typically delivered in 12-bit to 16-bit radiometric resolutions, often appearing very dark, flat, or low-contrast when directly combined. To make features visible, apply dynamic range stretching.

To automatically balance the tonal range across channels:

magick B04.tif B03.tif B02.tif -combine -auto-level -contrast-stretch 1%x1% true_color_enhanced.tif
  • -auto-level: Adjusts the full dynamic range for each color channel.
  • -contrast-stretch 1%x1%: Clips the darkest 1% to pure black and the brightest 1% to pure white, removing atmospheric haze and boosting clarity.

4. Creating False-Color Composites

False-color composites highlight features invisible to the human eye, such as healthy vegetation, moisture levels, or burn scars. Simply substitute the Near-Infrared (NIR) band into the red channel slot:

magick B08.tif B04.tif B03.tif -combine -auto-level -contrast-stretch 1%x1% false_color_nir.tif

In this output, healthy vegetation reflects strongly in NIR and will appear bright red, while water will appear dark blue or black.

5. Converting to 8-Bit Web Formats

If you need to distribute the composite as a standard JPEG or PNG for the web, reduce the color depth to 8-bit per channel using the -depth 8 option:

magick B04.tif B03.tif B02.tif -combine -auto-level -contrast-stretch 0.5%x0.5% -depth 8 output.jpg

This ensures full compatibility across standard image viewers, web browsers, and document editors.
