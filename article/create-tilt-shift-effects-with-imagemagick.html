Create Tilt-Shift Effects with ImageMagick

This guide explains how to transform standard aerial or high-angle photography into miniature-style dioramas using ImageMagick's gradient-driven variable blur. By reading this article, you will learn the mechanics behind the tilt-shift aesthetic, how to construct a custom gradient depth mask, and how to combine it with selective blurring, contrast adjustment, and color saturation in a single, efficient command-line workflow.

The Tilt-Shift Technique Explained

The miniature effect simulates the extremely shallow depth of field typically produced by physical tilt-shift lenses or macro photography. Achieving this digitally requires three core steps:

  1. Selective Gradient Blur: The focal point (typically a horizontal band across the subject) remains sharp, while areas above and below gradually increase in blur radius toward the edges.
  2. Gradient Masking: An internal grayscale mask dictates the blur intensity, where pure black represents zero blur (full sharpness) and pure white represents maximum blur.
  3. Color and Contrast Boosting: Miniatures and toys often have vibrant, slightly oversaturated paint and harsh lighting. Increasing saturation and contrast reinforces the toy-like illusion.

Step 1: Generating the Gradient Blur Mask

ImageMagick utilizes grayscale masks to control variable blur radii. The most effective mask for a horizontal focal plane is a mirrored vertical gradient: white at the top, pure black across the focal band, and white at the bottom.

To generate this mask directly in memory to match the target dimensions of an image:

magick input.jpg -alpha off \
  \( -clone 0 -size %wx%h gradient:black-white \
     -solarize 50% -level 0,50% -negate \) \
  -compose Blur -set option:compose:args 0x15 -composite \
  output.jpg
  • gradient:black-white: Generates a top-to-bottom linear gradient.
  • -solarize 50% -level 0,50% -negate: Converts the linear gradient into a mirrored gradient where the center is black (\(0\%\) blur) and both top and bottom edges are white (\(100\%\) blur).
  • -compose Blur -set option:compose:args 0x15: Applies a directional blur. The 0x15 parameter sets the horizontal blur radius to 0 and the maximum vertical/overall blur radius to 15 pixels.

Step 2: Customizing the Focal Plane Location

If the subject is not located in the exact vertical center of the frame, you can build a customized mask using coordinate points or a non-centered gradient by appending two separate gradient blocks:

magick input.jpg \
  \( -size %wx300 gradient:white-black \
     -size %wx400 xc:black \
     -size %wx300 gradient:black-white \
     -append -resize %wx%h\! \) \
  -compose Blur -set option:compose:args 0x20 -composite \
  output_custom_focus.jpg

This constructs a distinct focal zone using a 300px top blur transition, a 400px completely sharp center plane (xc:black), and a 300px bottom blur transition.

Step 3: Adding the Final Miniature Enhancements

To complete the diorama look, boost the color vibrancy and contrast after applying the blur composite:

magick input.jpg \
  \( -clone 0 -size %wx%h gradient:black-white \
     -solarize 50% -level 0,50% -negate \) \
  -compose Blur -set option:compose:args 0x18 -composite \
  -modulate 100,135,100 \
  -brightness-contrast 5x15 \
  final_tilt_shift.jpg
  • -modulate 100,135,100: Increases color saturation by 35% without shifting hue or base brightness.
  • -brightness-contrast 5x15: Adds a slight brightness increase (\(+5\)) and sharp contrast boost (\(+15\)) to mimic bright overhead lighting on miniature models.
