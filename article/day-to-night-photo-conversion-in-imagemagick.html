Day to Night Photo Conversion in ImageMagick

Converting daytime photography into a convincing twilight or night scene—often called the "day-for-night" effect—is easily accomplished using ImageMagick. This article explains the exact commands and image processing techniques required to reduce daylight exposure, introduce cool blue and indigo ambient tones, deepen shadows, and apply subtle vignette effects using the ImageMagick command-line interface.

The Core Command

To transform a daytime image into a twilight scene, you need to decrease brightness, desaturate warm daylight colors, shift the overall hue toward cool blue tones, and deepen the contrast.

Run the following command in your terminal:

magick input.jpg \
  -modulate 60,60,100 \
  -fill "#0b1d3a" -tint 55% \
  -sigmoidal-contrast 4,40% \
  -background black -vignette 0x20 \
  output_twilight.jpg

(Note: If using ImageMagick 6, replace magick with convert.)

Parameter Breakdown

Each parameter targets a specific aspect of the daylight-to-night transformation:

1. Exposure and Saturation (-modulate)

  • -modulate 60,60,100: Modifies the Brightness, Saturation, and Hue (in that order).
    • Setting brightness to 60 reduces exposure to simulate low-light conditions.
    • Setting saturation to 60 removes excess daylight vibrancy, mimicking the human eye's reduced color perception in the dark (the Purkinje effect).

2. Twilight Color Tint (-fill and -tint)

  • -fill "#0b1d3a" -tint 55%: Blends a dark navy-blue color across the midtones and highlights.
    • The hex color #0b1d3a represents deep evening twilight.
    • The 55% strength applies enough cool tone to override the sunlight without completely washing out foreground details.

3. Shadow and Contrast Adjustment (-sigmoidal-contrast)

  • -sigmoidal-contrast 4,40%: Applies an S-curve to the tonal range.
    • The first value (4) controls the strength of the contrast.
    • The second value (40%) shifts the midpoint, pushing darker midtones into deep shadows while preserving faint highlight details on reflective surfaces or skies.

4. Twilight Vignette (-background and -vignette)

  • -background black -vignette 0x20: Naturally darkens the outer edges of the frame to replicate standard low-light photography optics and draw focus to the center.

Advanced Adjustments for Golden Hour to Deep Night

You can modify the values depending on the specific time of night you want to emulate:

  • Civil Twilight (Just after sunset): Use a slightly lighter blue fill (#1a2d5a), set -modulate 75,70,100, and lower tint to 40%.
  • Deep Midnight: Use a darker tone (#050d1a), set -modulate 40,40,100, and increase tint to 70%.
