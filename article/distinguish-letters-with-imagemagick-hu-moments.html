Distinguish Letters with ImageMagick Hu Moments

This article explains how to use ImageMagick and Hu moment invariants to differentiate between characters with distinct geometric structures, specifically the letters 'O' and 'I'. You will learn how to extract shape moments using ImageMagick command-line tools and interpret the mathematical differences in rotational symmetry and elongation invariants to classify these shapes accurately.

Understanding Hu Invariant Moments

Hu moment invariants are a set of seven numbers calculated from central image moments that remain invariant under translation, scaling, and rotation. In shape recognition, they describe the spatial distribution of pixels relative to a shape's center of mass.

When distinguishing letters like 'O' and 'I', the primary factors are elongation and directional symmetry:

  • 'O' has high radial symmetry, a hollow center, and a low aspect ratio (roughly 1:1).
  • 'I' is highly elongated along a single axis, displaying extreme eccentricity.

Extracting Hu Moments Using ImageMagick

To compute these values, ensure the letter images are isolated, centered, and binarized (black text on a white background or white text on a black background). Use the -moments flag with the ImageMagick identify tool:

magick identify -verbose -moments letter_o.png
magick identify -verbose -moments letter_i.png

ImageMagick outputs several statistical values, including the seven Hu invariants labeled I1 through I7.

Key Invariants for Distinguishing 'O' and 'I'

While ImageMagick generates all seven invariants, the first two moments (\(I_1\) and \(I_2\)) provide sufficient distinction between these two letters.

1. The First Hu Moment (\(I_1\)): Spread and Compactness

The first invariant, \(I_1\), corresponds to the moment of inertia around the centroid. It reflects how compact or spread out the pixels are relative to the center:

  • For 'O', the pixels are distributed relatively close and evenly around the perimeter of the center, resulting in a lower \(I_1\) value.
  • For 'I', the length of the vertical stroke distributes mass much farther away from the center of mass along the vertical axis, producing a significantly higher \(I_1\) value.

2. The Second Hu Moment (\(I_2\)): Eccentricity

The second invariant, \(I_2\), captures directional anisotropy or elongation. It measures the difference between the principal axes of inertia:

  • For 'O', the variance along the horizontal axis (\(\eta_{20}\)) and the vertical axis (\(\eta_{02}\)) is nearly identical because the letter is roughly circular or square. As a result, the difference \((\eta_{20} - \eta_{02})\) is close to zero, yielding an \(I_2\) value close to zero.
  • For 'I', the vertical variance vastly exceeds the horizontal variance. This structural imbalance produces a distinctively large \(I_2\) value.

Implementation Strategy

To classify the characters programmatically:

  1. Extract Invariants: Run the ImageMagick -moments command and parse the floating-point values for I1 and I2.
  2. Apply a Threshold: Define a decision boundary on \(I_2\). If \(I_2\) exceeds the predefined threshold, classify the shape as elongated ('I'). If \(I_2\) falls near zero, classify it as circular/compact ('O').
  3. Verify with \(I_1\): Use \(I_1\) as a confirmation check to differentiate compact shapes from stretched geometries if the character rotation varies.
