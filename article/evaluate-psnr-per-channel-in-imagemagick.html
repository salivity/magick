Evaluate PSNR Per Channel in ImageMagick

Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) is a standard metric used to measure the reconstruction quality of lossy compression or image processing operations relative to an original image. This guide explains how to evaluate PSNR across individual and combined color channels using ImageMagick's command-line tools, interpret the resulting decibel values, and isolate specific color components such as Red, Green, Blue, and Alpha.

Understanding ImageMagick PSNR Metric

ImageMagick provides the compare utility (invoked directly in ImageMagick 6 or via magick compare in ImageMagick 7) to compute mathematical differences between two images. When using the PSNR metric, ImageMagick compares the Mean Squared Error (MSE) against the maximum possible pixel intensity and outputs the result in decibels (dB). A higher value indicates higher image fidelity, while inf (infinity) represents identical images.

Basic Syntax for Global PSNR

To compute the overall PSNR across all channels combined:

magick compare -metric PSNR original.png compressed.png null:

Note: The null: destination discards the visual difference map image and outputs only the metric to standard error (stderr).

Evaluating PSNR for Individual Color Channels

ImageMagick allows you to restrict metrics to specific channels using the -channel operator.

1. Evaluating a Specific Channel

To compute PSNR for a single channel (e.g., Red, Green, Blue, or Alpha), specify the channel name:

Red Channel:

magick compare -metric PSNR -channel R original.png compressed.png null:

Green Channel:

magick compare -metric PSNR -channel G original.png compressed.png null:

Blue Channel:

magick compare -metric PSNR -channel B original.png compressed.png null:

Alpha Channel (if transparency is present):

magick compare -metric PSNR -channel A original.png compressed.png null:

2. Evaluating Non-RGB Color Spaces

If you need to evaluate PSNR in other color spaces, such as YUV or CMYK, convert the images prior to comparison:

magick compare -metric PSNR -colorspace YUV -channel R original.png compressed.png null:

In the YUV colorspace, channel R corresponds to the Y (Luma) channel, G to U (Chroma), and B to V (Chroma).

Getting a Comprehensive Multi-Channel Breakdown

To output values for all channels simultaneously in a structured format, use the identify tool with the -format parameter and distortion calculation:

magick original.png compressed.png -metric PSNR -compare -format "%[distortion]" info:

Alternatively, running compare -verbose provides an in-depth breakdown containing the distortion values per channel along with minimum, maximum, and average error statistics:

magick compare -verbose -metric PSNR original.png compressed.png diff.png

Interpreting PSNR Output Values

  • inf (Infinity): The evaluated channel in both images is bit-for-bit identical.
  • 40 dB to 50 dB: Excellent quality; differences are typically imperceptible to the human eye.
  • 30 dB to 40 dB: Good quality; common in standard lossy compression (such as JPEG). Minor degradation may be visible upon close inspection.
  • Below 30 dB: Noticeable quality loss, severe compression artifacts, or color shifts.
