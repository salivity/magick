Extract Scanned Photos into Files Using ImageMagick

Scanning multiple photographs at once on a flatbed scanner saves time, but it results in a single large digital file containing multiple images. This guide explains how to isolate and extract those individually scanned photos into separate output files using ImageMagick commands, covering both automated boundary detection and coordinate-based cropping methods.

Method 1: Automatic Extraction Using Connected Components (ImageMagick 7)

If your scanned photos sit on a solid white or black flatbed background, ImageMagick 7 can automatically detect each distinct photo boundary using connected-component labeling and export them into separate files.

Step 1: Detect Photo Bounding Boxes

Run the following command in your terminal to analyze the scan and print the bounding boxes of distinct objects:

magick scan.jpg \
  -fuzz 15% \
  -fill white -draw "color 0,0 floodfill" \
  -threshold 90% -negate \
  -define connected-components:verbose=true \
  -define connected-components:area-threshold=10000 \
  -connected-components 4 \
  null:
  • -fuzz 15%: Accounts for slight variations in the flatbed scanner's background color.
  • -threshold 90% -negate: Converts the image to high-contrast black and white to isolate objects from the background.
  • area-threshold=10000: Filters out small dust specs or scanning artifacts, only identifying large photo regions.

Step 2: Crop Using Detected Bounding Boxes

The command output will list geometry dimensions for each identified region in standard format (e.g., 1200x800+150+200). You can extract specific regions directly:

magick scan.jpg -crop 1200x800+150+200 +repage photo_1.jpg

Method 2: Splitting Scans on a Uniform Grid

If you scanned pictures arranged in an even grid (for example, two rows of two photos), you can split the entire image into equal parts automatically.

magick scan.jpg -crop 2x2@ +repage photo_%d.jpg
  • -crop 2x2@: Divides the image into two rows and two columns (four equal segments).
  • +repage: Resets the canvas coordinates so each resulting image is treated as an independent file.
  • photo_%d.jpg: Names the resulting files incrementally (photo_0.jpg, photo_1.jpg, etc.).

Method 3: Post-Crop Trimming and Deskewing

Individual scanned photos often retain excess white border or might be slightly tilted. You can clean them up during the extraction process:

magick scan.jpg -crop 1200x800+150+200 +repage -fuzz 10% -deskew 40% -trim +repage photo_clean.jpg
  • -deskew 40%: Automatically straightens photos that were placed slightly crooked on the glass.
  • -trim: Removes any residual uniform background bordering the photo.
