Extract Shadow Areas by Luminance in ImageMagick

Extracting shadow areas based on luminance thresholds in ImageMagick involves converting an image to a grayscale luminance channel and applying threshold operations to isolate the darkest pixels. This guide provides the exact command-line syntax for generating binary shadow masks, retaining original shadow pixel values, and extracting shadows with transparency.

1. Generating a Binary Shadow Mask

To create a black-and-white mask where shadow pixels appear white (selected) and all other pixels appear black, use the following syntax:

magick input.jpg -colorspace Gray -threshold 25% -negate shadow_mask.png
  • -colorspace Gray: Converts the image to grayscale based on perceptual luminance.
  • -threshold 25%: Turns all pixels with luminance greater than 25% to white, and 25% or below to black.
  • -negate: Inverts the colors so that the shadow areas (<= 25% luminance) become white and highlights/midtones become black.

2. Extracting Shadow Pixels with Transparency

To isolate shadow regions while keeping their original colors and rendering everything above the luminance threshold transparent:

magick input.jpg \( +clone -colorspace Gray -threshold 25% -negate \) -alpha off -compose CopyOpacity -composite shadows_isolated.png
  • +clone: Creates an in-memory copy of the input image.
  • -threshold 25% -negate: Builds the luminance mask.
  • -compose CopyOpacity -composite: Applies the generated mask as the alpha (transparency) channel of the original image.

3. Preserving Shadow Pixels and Flattening Highlights to Black

If you want to keep the original image data in the shadow areas while turning all midtones and highlights black, use -white-threshold:

magick input.jpg -white-threshold 25% shadows_only.jpg
  • -white-threshold 25%: Forces any pixel with a luminance value higher than 25% to pure white or black depending on color space handling, effectively clipping out highlights and midtones.

4. Advanced Luminance Extraction Using Rec709Luma (ImageMagick 7)

For higher photometric precision based on standard video/broadcast luminance formulas, use Rec709Luma:

magick input.jpg -colorspace Rec709Luma -threshold 20% -negate shadow_mask_rec709.png

Adjust the percentage value (e.g., 10% to 30%) depending on whether you want to target deep shadows or include lower midtones.
