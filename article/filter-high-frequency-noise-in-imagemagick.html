Filter High-Frequency Noise in ImageMagick

This article provides a step-by-step guide to removing high-frequency noise from images using Fourier domain operations in ImageMagick. By converting an image into its frequency components with the Forward Fourier Transform (-fft), applying a low-pass filter to attenuate high frequencies, and reconstructing the image with the Inverse Fourier Transform (+ift), you can selectively eliminate noise patterns while preserving core visual structures.

Understanding Fourier Domain Filtering

When ImageMagick performs a Fast Fourier Transform (-fft), it converts a spatial image into two separate components:

  1. Magnitude: Represents the strength of various frequencies. Low frequencies (overall shape, smooth gradients) are centered, while high frequencies (fine details, sharp edges, and noise) spread toward the outer edges.
  2. Phase: Encodes the spatial orientation and structure of the image.

High-frequency noise resides in the outer areas of the magnitude spectrum. Filtering this noise requires creating a mask (a low-pass filter) that keeps the center bright and darkens the outer perimeter, multiplying the magnitude image by this mask, and recombining it with the phase image using +ift.

The ImageMagick Command

To filter high-frequency noise in a single command using ImageMagick v7, execute the following:

magick input.png -fft \
  \( -clone 0 -fill black -colorize 100% \
     -fill white -draw "circle %[fx:w/2],%[fx:h/2] %[fx:w/2],%[fx:h/2+40]" \
     -blur 0x15 \) \
  \( -clone 0,2 -compose Multiply -composite \) \
  -delete 0,2 +ift output.png

Breakdown of the Steps

  1. -fft (Forward Fourier Transform): Transforms input.png into two images in the current sequence: image 0 (Magnitude) and image 1 (Phase).

  2. Creating the Low-Pass Filter Mask:

    • -clone 0 -fill black -colorize 100%: Duplicates the magnitude canvas size and creates a solid black background.
    • -fill white -draw "circle ...": Draws a white circle in the center. The radius determines the cutoff frequency. Frequencies within the circle are preserved; frequencies outside will be suppressed.
    • -blur 0x15: Softens the edge of the circle (creating a Gaussian-like roll-off) to avoid "ringing" artifacts (Gibbs phenomenon) in the final output.

  3. Applying the Mask:

    • -clone 0,2 -compose Multiply -composite: Multiplies the original magnitude (image 0) by the generated low-pass mask (image 2). The outer high-frequency components are reduced to zero or near-zero.

  4. Reconstructing with +ift (Inverse Fourier Transform):

    • -delete 0,2: Removes the raw magnitude and the separate mask, leaving only the filtered magnitude and the original phase.
    • +ift: Computes the Inverse Fast Fourier Transform from the modified magnitude and phase, writing the denoised result to output.png.

Parameter Adjustments

  • Cutoff Frequency (Circle Radius): Change the offset in the draw command (e.g., %[fx:h/2+40]). A smaller circle removes more noise but softens fine details; a larger circle retains sharper details but lets more noise pass.
  • Filter Smoothness (Blur): Adjust the -blur 0x15 radius and sigma. A smoother gradient produces a cleaner transition without artificial ripple patterns.
