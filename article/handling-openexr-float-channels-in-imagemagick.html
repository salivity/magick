Handling OpenEXR Float Channels in ImageMagick

OpenEXR (.exr) files store high dynamic range (HDR) image data using 16-bit half-precision or 32-bit single-precision floating-point numbers. To manipulate, extract, or convert these floating-point channels without precision loss or data clamping, ImageMagick must be configured with High Dynamic Range Imaging (HDRI) enabled. This guide covers how to verify your build, extract specific floating-point channels, preserve float precision, and convert EXR data for standard displays.

1. Verify HDRI Support

ImageMagick must be compiled with HDRI support (typically Q16HDRI or Q32HDRI) to process non-integer pixel values. Check your installation by running:

magick -version

Look for HDRI under the Features list. If HDRI is missing, floating-point data will be clamped between 0 and integer quantum maximums, corrupting the HDR data.

2. Preserving Floating-Point Precision

By default, saving an image might convert the data into fixed-point integers. When writing or converting OpenEXR files, explicitly define the depth and floating-point format to prevent data truncation.

To save an image as a 16-bit half-float OpenEXR:

magick input.exr -depth 16 -define quantum:format=floating-point output.exr

To preserve full 32-bit single-precision floating-point channels:

magick input.exr -depth 32 -define quantum:format=floating-point output_32bit.exr

3. Extracting and Isolating Specific Channels

OpenEXR files often contain arbitrary channels beyond standard RGB, such as Depth (Z), Alpha (A), or auxiliary render passes.

To extract a specific channel (e.g., Red or a single channel pass) and export it as an individual floating-point EXR file:

magick input.exr -channel R -separate red_channel.exr

To extract all channels into separate grayscale EXR files:

magick input.exr -separate channel_%d.exr

4. Adjusting Floating-Point Data Ranges

Raw floating-point data can contain negative values or values far exceeding 1.0 (such as luminance or depth values). You can perform mathematical operations directly on the float channels using the -evaluate operator:

  • Scale data: Multiply channels by a constant factor: 
    magick input.exr -evaluate multiply 0.5 scaled.exr
  • Offset data: Add an offset to normalize negative values: 
    magick input.exr -evaluate add 1.0 offset.exr

5. Converting OpenEXR to Standard 8-bit or 16-bit Formats

Standard formats like PNG, TIFF, and JPEG do not handle unbound floating-point data correctly without tone mapping. To convert an EXR file to a standard display format, apply color space conversion, clamping, or tone adjustment:

  • Linear to sRGB conversion: 
    magick input.exr -colorspace sRGB -depth 8 output.png
  • Auto-leveling and clamping: 
    magick input.exr -auto-level -clamp -depth 8 output.png
  • Applying Gamma for non-linear display: 
    magick input.exr -gamma 2.2 -clamp -depth 8 output.jpg
