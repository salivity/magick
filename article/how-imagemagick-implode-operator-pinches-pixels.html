How ImageMagick Implode Operator Pinches Pixels

The ImageMagick -implode operator creates a pinch effect by applying a non-linear geometric distortion radially around an image’s center point. This process converts the image into a polar coordinate system, calculates a non-linear radial displacement based on a user-defined factor, and uses inverse pixel mapping combined with interpolation to sample colors from the original image into the pinched output.

Center Point and Elliptical Boundary

The distortion begins by establishing the center of the transformation, typically located at \((x_c, y_c) = (\text{width} / 2, \text{height} / 2)\). The operator then defines a maximum operational radius (\(R\)), usually determined by half the image's minimum dimension. This creates a circular or elliptical threshold outside of which pixels remain completely unaffected by the transformation.

Polar Coordinate Transformation

To evaluate displacement uniformly across all directions, the algorithm converts standard Cartesian pixel coordinates \((x, y)\) into polar coordinates \((r, \theta)\):

  • Distance (\(r\)): The Euclidean distance from the image center to the pixel, normalized relative to the radius threshold \(R\), yielding a scale from \(0.0\) at the center to \(1.0\) at the boundary.
  • Angle (\(\theta\)): The directional angle calculated via the arctangent function \(\text{atan2}(y - y_c, x - x_c)\).

Because the pinch effect is purely radial, the angular component \(\theta\) remains constant, ensuring pixels travel only directly inward or outward along a straight trajectory relative to the center.

Non-Linear Radial Displacement

The core implosion relies on modifying the normalized radius \(r\) using a non-linear curve. ImageMagick applies a scaling factor to determine how drastically the distance changes:

\[r_{\text{source}} = r \times (1.0 + \text{factor} \times (1.0 - r))\]

  • When the factor is positive, \(r_{\text{source}}\) increases relative to \(r\).
  • Because the algorithm uses reverse mapping, an increased source radius means the output pixel pulls its color data from a point located further outward along the radius, creating the visual effect of content being pulled inward toward the center.
  • The term \((1.0 - r)\) ensures that the distortion is strongest near the center and smoothly tapers off to zero distortion as \(r\) approaches \(1.0\) at the outer boundary.

Reverse Pixel Mapping and Interpolation

ImageMagick processes transformations using reverse (inverse) mapping rather than forward mapping:

  1. The algorithm loops through every destination pixel coordinate in the output image.
  2. It applies the mathematical formula in reverse to locate the exact coordinate on the original source image that corresponds to that destination.
  3. Because the resulting source coordinate is usually a fractional number (e.g., \(x = 104.32, y = 55.78\)), the operator applies an interpolation filter—such as bilinear or bicubic—to blend the neighboring source pixels smoothly.

Reverse mapping guarantees that every destination pixel receives a calculated color value, eliminating holes, gaps, or overlapping artifacts that would otherwise occur if pixels were pushed forward from source to target.
