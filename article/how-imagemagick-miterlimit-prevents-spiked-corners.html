How ImageMagick Miterlimit Prevents Spiked Corners

This article explains how ImageMagick’s -draw 'miterlimit' setting prevents unsightly, elongated spikes on vector polygons and paths. When drawing stroked paths with sharp angles, intersecting lines can project outward excessively. By adjusting the miter limit threshold, you can automatically clip these sharp peaks into flat, beveled edges to maintain clean, predictable vector rendering.

The Cause of Spiked Corners

When rendering vector paths or polygons using the miter line join style (stroke-linejoin miter), the outer edges of two meeting stroke segments are extended until they meet at a single point.

For wide or obtuse angles, this creates a clean, crisp corner. However, as the angle between the two lines becomes more acute, the distance from the inner point of the corner to the outer intersection point increases exponentially. At very sharp angles, this geometry produces a needle-like spike that can extend far beyond the actual bounds of the polygon, often distorting the visual composition or clipping beyond the image canvas.

The Role of miterlimit

The miterlimit property defines the maximum allowable ratio between the length of the miter (the distance from the inner vertex to the outer tip of the corner) and the stroke width:

\[\text{Miter Ratio} = \frac{\text{Miter Length}}{\text{Stroke Width}} = \frac{1}{\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}\]

where \(\theta\) represents the angle between the two joining lines.

By defining a numerical value for miterlimit, you instruct ImageMagick on how far a corner is allowed to project outward relative to the thickness of the stroke.

How It Prevents Spikes: The Bevel Fallback

Whenever the calculated miter ratio of a corner exceeds the assigned miterlimit value, ImageMagick automatically overrides the sharp miter join for that specific vertex and converts it into a bevel join.

Instead of projecting outward to a sharp, spiked point, the corner is squared off with a straight edge running perpendicular to the angle bisector. This caps the corner neatly at the stroke boundary without altering the underlying path coordinates.

Practical Implementation in ImageMagick

To use miterlimit, include it within the -draw primitive options along with your stroke properties:

magick -size 500x500 xc:white \
  -stroke black -strokewidth 10 -fill lightblue \
  -draw "stroke-linejoin miter miterlimit 2 polygon 50,50 450,250 60,70" \
  output.png

Choosing the Right Value

  • High Values (e.g., 10 or greater): Allow very acute angles to form sharp points. This is often the default behavior, but it carries a high risk of producing spikes on sharp corners.
  • Low Values (e.g., 1 to 2): Restrict miter growth aggressively. A value near 1 forces almost all angles to convert to bevel joins, eliminating all spikes while softening the vertices.
  • Standard Value (around 4): Commonly used in graphics specifications (such as SVG). A limit of 4 converts corners to bevels when the angle becomes sharper than approximately 29 degrees, striking a balance between crisp corners and spike prevention.
