How to Adjust PNG Contrast in ImageMagick?

ImageMagick provides several command-line tools to adjust the contrast of PNG images, ranging from quick single-step modifiers to fine-grained linear and non-linear tone curves. Depending on your version—ImageMagick 7 using the magick command or ImageMagick 6 using convert—you can adjust contrast using -contrast, -level, or -sigmoidal-contrast without corrupting transparent PNG alpha channels.

Quick Adjustments with the Contrast Flag

The simplest method ImageMagick offers is the -contrast operator. This option increases or decreases contrast in fixed steps around a central threshold without requiring complex numerical values.

To increase contrast:

magick input.png -contrast output.png

To decrease contrast, use the +contrast variation:

magick input.png +contrast output.png

Each occurrence of the flag applies an incremental step. To apply a stronger effect, repeat the argument:

magick input.png -contrast -contrast output.png

Linear Adjustments Using Level

The -level operator provides explicit control over the black point, white point, and midtones (gamma), making it ideal for linear contrast stretching.

The basic syntax is:

magick input.png -level <black_point>%,<white_point>%,<gamma> output.png

To increase contrast, raise the black point and lower the white point. This pushes near-black pixels to pure black and near-white pixels to pure white:

magick input.png -level 10%,90% output.png

To adjust midtone brightness simultaneously, supply the optional third value for gamma (values above 1.0 brighten midtones, while values below 1.0 darken them):

magick input.png -level 10%,90%,1.2 output.png

Natural Adjustments Using Sigmoidal Contrast

The -sigmoidal-contrast operator is often the preferred choice for photographic or complex imagery because it applies an S-curve. This preserves details in deep shadows and bright highlights while increasing or decreasing contrast across midtones.

The syntax requires two values: <contrast_factor>x<midpoint_percentage>%:

magick input.png -sigmoidal-contrast 5x50% output.png

A contrast factor typically ranges between 3 (subtle) and 10 (heavy). The midpoint percentage designates the center of the tonal curve, usually kept at 50%.

To decrease contrast smoothly instead of increasing it, invert the operator with a plus sign:

magick input.png +sigmoidal-contrast 5x50% output.png

Preserving PNG Transparency

When modifying PNGs containing transparent or semi-transparent backgrounds, tonal adjustments can inadvertently alter the alpha channel. To limit contrast changes strictly to visible colors while keeping the alpha channel intact, restrict processing to the color channels:

magick input.png -channel RGB -sigmoidal-contrast 6x50% +channel output.png

The -channel RGB argument restricts the operation to the red, green, and blue components, and +channel resets channel selection so the output PNG writes normally.
