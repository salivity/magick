How to Apply a Kuwahara Filter in ImageMagick

This article provides a direct guide on using the Kuwahara filter in ImageMagick to transform standard digital photos into artistic, painterly images. You will learn the core command-line syntax for ImageMagick, how adjusting the filter's radius parameter impacts the level of abstraction, and complementary adjustments to enhance the final oil-painting effect.

Understanding the Kuwahara Filter

The Kuwahara filter is a non-linear smoothing filter that reduces texture while preserving prominent edges. It works by dividing a square window around each pixel into overlapping sub-regions, calculating the variance in each region, and assigning the pixel the mean value of the region with the lowest variance. In image processing, this produces flat, stroke-like color blocks and sharp boundaries, closely resembling a traditional oil painting or watercolor.

Basic Command Syntax

In ImageMagick version 7, use the magick command followed by the -kuwahara operator.

magick input.jpg -kuwahara 5 output.jpg

If you are using ImageMagick version 6, replace magick with convert:

convert input.jpg -kuwahara 5 output.jpg

Parameter Tuning

The number passed to the -kuwahara option represents the radius of the sampling grid.

  • Small Radius (-kuwahara 2 to 3): Produces a subtle smoothing effect, removing digital noise and minor skin blemishes while retaining most fine details.
  • Medium Radius (-kuwahara 4 to 6): Creates a distinct painterly look, turning fine textures into distinct brushstrokes while keeping major subject outlines crisp.
  • Large Radius (-kuwahara 8 or higher): Heavily abstracts the image, creating broad, blocky patches of color suitable for posterized or abstract art styles.

Advanced Painterly Workflow

To achieve a more realistic canvas-painting aesthetic, combine the Kuwahara filter with color saturation adjustments and slight edge enhancement:

magick input.jpg -kuwahara 5 -modulate 100,125,100 -sharpen 0x1 painterly_result.jpg
  • -kuwahara 5: Smooths surfaces into flat color planes.
  • -modulate 100,125,100: Increases image saturation to 125% to mimic vibrant artist pigments.
  • -sharpen 0x1: Adds crispness along the boundaries between the flattened color strokes.
