How to Apply Cyanotype Toning in ImageMagick

This guide provides the syntax and commands required to reproduce a classic cyanotype photographic toning effect using ImageMagick. You will learn the primary command structures for both modern and legacy ImageMagick versions, how the color-mapping mechanism works to create distinctive Prussian blue tones, and how to fine-tune contrast for an authentic vintage print appearance.

Basic Cyanotype Syntax

The most effective method for simulating a cyanotype in ImageMagick is to convert the image to grayscale and map the dark and light tones to cyanotype-specific colors using the +level-colors operator.

ImageMagick v7 (Recommended)

magick input.jpg -colorspace Gray +level-colors "#002B49","#EBF3F5" output_cyanotype.jpg

ImageMagick v6 (Legacy)

convert input.jpg -colorspace Gray +level-colors "#002B49","#EBF3F5" output_cyanotype.jpg

Parameter Breakdown

  • -colorspace Gray: Strips the original color data from the source image, converting it into a standard linear or non-linear grayscale image.
  • +level-colors <black-point>,<white-point>: Remaps the black and white endpoints of the grayscale image:
    • #002B49 (Dark Tone): Represents the deep, rich Prussian blue typical of cyanotype shadows.
    • #EBF3F5 (Light Tone): Represents the off-white or slightly blue-tinted paper base for the highlights.
  • input.jpg / output_cyanotype.jpg: Your source and target image file paths.

High-Contrast Cyanotype Syntax

Historical cyanotypes often display higher contrast due to the chemical printing process. You can introduce contrast adjustments using -auto-level and -contrast-stretch or -sigmoidal-contrast:

magick input.jpg -colorspace Gray -auto-level -sigmoidal-contrast 3,50% +level-colors "#001F3F","#F0F8FF" output_high_contrast.jpg
  • -sigmoidal-contrast 3,50%: Increases the S-curve contrast around the midtones (50%) with a factor of 3.
  • #001F3F: A deeper navy blue for darker shadows.
  • #F0F8FF (AliceBlue): A brighter highlight that retains a subtle cool cast.

Alternative Method: Using the -tint Operator

If you prefer blending color directly over a desaturated base rather than remapping endpoints, use the -tint option:

magick input.jpg -colorspace Gray -fill "#1E4B6E" -tint 100% output_tint.jpg
  • -fill "#1E4B6E": Specifies the tint color.
  • -tint 100%: Applies full-strength midtone tinting while keeping absolute blacks and whites closer to their natural luminance.
