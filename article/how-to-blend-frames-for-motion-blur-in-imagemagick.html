How to Blend Frames for Motion Blur in ImageMagick

Simulating temporal motion blur in ImageMagick involves blending adjacent frames across an animation or image sequence to mimic the natural exposure effect of a physical camera shutter. This process smoothens fast movements by averaging a sequence or a sliding window of consecutive frames. Below is a direct guide detailing how to compute long-exposure motion blur, build sliding-window blended frames for video sequences, and apply weighted blending for realistic shutter effects.

Full Sequence Average (Long Exposure Effect)

To merge an entire series of images into a single static frame that captures all motion across the sequence, use the -evaluate-sequence mean operation.

magick input_frame_*.png -evaluate-sequence mean long_exposure.png

This command loads every matching frame into memory and calculates the arithmetic mean of their pixel values at each position, producing a continuous motion trail.

Sliding Window Blending (Sequential Video Motion Blur)

To add temporal motion blur across a sequence of animated frames without turning the entire animation into one image, you must average a sliding window of adjacent frames (for example, blending frame \(N-1\), \(N\), and \(N+1\) for each output frame \(N\)).

Using a Bash Loop

A simple shell loop allows you to blend a 3-frame sliding window for each frame in your sequence:

TOTAL_FRAMES=100

for (( i=2; i<$TOTAL_FRAMES; i++ )); do
    prev=$(printf "frame_%04d.png" $((i - 1)))
    curr=$(printf "frame_%04d.png" $i)
    next=$(printf "frame_%04d.png" $((i + 1)))
    out=$(printf "blurred_%04d.png" $i)

    magick "$prev" "$curr" "$next" -evaluate-sequence mean "$out"
done

Weighted Motion Blur (Realistic Camera Shutter)

Equal averaging treats all frames in the window with uniform exposure. A more physically accurate camera shutter applies a Gaussian or triangular weight, giving the most intensity to the center frame and tapering off toward the edges.

You can use the -poly operator to define custom weights for each frame. For a 3-frame blend where the center frame gets 50% weight and adjacent frames get 25% weight each:

magick frame_01.png frame_02.png frame_03.png -poly "0.25,1 0.5,1 0.25,1" blurred_frame.png

The arguments inside -poly follow the format "weight,exponent". Setting the exponent to 1 ensures a linear color calculation that normalizes to a total weight of 1.0 (0.25 + 0.50 + 0.25).

Blending Frames in an Animated GIF Directly

If your input is an animated GIF and you want to reduce frame count while smoothing motion, you can extract, duplicate, shift, and blend the frame layers directly within a single command:

magick input.gif \
  \( -clone 0--1 \) \
  \( -clone 1--1,0 \) \
  \( -clone 2--1,0-1 \) \
  -evaluate-sequence mean \
  -loop 0 output_blurred.gif

This technique creates offsets of the animation layers and averages them together simultaneously to generate a smoother, motion-blurred loop.
