How to Create a Tilt-Shift Effect in ImageMagick

This guide explains how to transform standard landscape or high-angle photography into a miniature-style scene using ImageMagick. You will learn how to simulate a shallow depth of field by generating a linear gradient mask, applying variable blurring across the image, and enhancing color saturation to achieve a convincing tilt-shift miniature effect directly from the command line.

Understanding the Tilt-Shift Miniature Effect

A tilt-shift miniature effect relies on two primary visual characteristics:

  1. Selective Focus: A narrow horizontal strip remains in sharp focus, while the top and bottom of the frame progressively blur.
  2. Vibrant Colors: Saturated colors mimic the painted plastic surfaces of scale models and dioramas.

In ImageMagick, this effect is achieved using composite blurring, where a grayscale gradient image controls the intensity of the blur across the source photo.

Step 1: Preparing the Gradient Blur Mask

ImageMagick's -compose Blur operation uses a mask where:

  • Black (#000000) represents zero blur (the focal plane).
  • White (#FFFFFF) represents maximum blur (the out-of-focus areas).

To create a horizontal focal band across the middle of an image, you can use a mirrored gradient or the -sparse-color operator.

A linear gradient transitioning from white at the top, to black in the middle, and back to white at the bottom defines the blur map:

magick input.jpg \
  ( +clone -sparse-color Barycentric "0,0 white  0,%[fx:h*0.5] black  0,%[h] white" ) \
  mask.png

Step 2: Applying the Gradient Blur and Color Enhancement

You can combine mask generation, variable blurring, and color adjustments into a single ImageMagick command.

For ImageMagick 7:

magick input.jpg \
  -modulate 105,140,100 \
  ( +clone -sparse-color Barycentric "0,0 white 0,%[fx:h*0.55] black 0,%[h] white" ) \
  -compose Blur -set option:compose:args 15x0 -composite \
  tilt_shift_output.jpg

Command Breakdown

  • input.jpg: The source photo (works best with elevated or aerial perspective shots).
  • -modulate 105,140,100: Slightly increases brightness (105%) and significantly boosts saturation (140%) to make objects look toy-like.
  • +clone: Duplicates the source image in memory to create a mask with the exact same dimensions.
  • -sparse-color Barycentric "...": Constructs a vertical gradient with three control points:
    • 0,0 white: Full blur at the top edge.
    • 0,%[fx:h*0.55] black: Sharp focus at 55% from the top (slightly below center).
    • 0,%[h] white: Full blur at the bottom edge.
  • -compose Blur: Selects the variable blur composition operator.
  • -set option:compose:args 15x0: Defines the maximum blur radius (15 pixels horizontally, 0 pixels vertically to maintain a horizontal focal plane).
  • -composite: Blends the blurred effect onto the original photo using the mask.

Customizing the Effect

  • Shift the Focal Plane: Change 0,%[fx:h*0.55] to a different fraction (such as 0.3 for upper focus or 0.7 for lower focus) depending on where your subject is located.
  • Adjust Blur Strength: Increase 15x0 to 25x0 or higher for higher-resolution images, or decrease it for subtler effects.
  • Widen the In-Focus Area: Use a multi-point gradient or adjust contrast curves on the mask to create a broader plateau of black in the center for larger subjects.
