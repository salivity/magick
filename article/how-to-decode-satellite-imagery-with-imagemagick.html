How to Decode Satellite Imagery with ImageMagick

Multispectral satellite imagery packages distinct electromagnetic wavelengths into separate grayscale bands or channels within container formats like multi-page GeoTIFFs. This article explains how to use ImageMagick, a robust command-line utility, to inspect multi-layer files, isolate specific spectral bands, and combine them into meaningful true-color and false-color composites.

Understanding Multispectral Satellite Bands

Satellite sensors (such as Landsat 8/9 or Sentinel-2) do not capture single standard RGB images. Instead, they capture separate grayscale images for distinct wavelengths, including Coastal Aerosol, Blue, Green, Red, Near-Infrared (NIR), and Shortwave-Infrared (SWIR). Decoding these images requires extracting the relevant channels and merging them into standard color spaces.

Inspecting Image Metadata and Channel Layers

Before processing, determine the number of bands and the bit depth contained in your satellite image file.

Run the following command to identify the sub-images (layers) inside a multi-band file:

magick identify input_multispectral.tif

This output lists each sub-image index (e.g., input_multispectral.tif[0], input_multispectral.tif[1]) alongside its dimensions and color depth.

Extracting Individual Spectral Channels

If your satellite data is packaged as a single multi-page file, you can extract specific bands using ImageMagick’s bracket notation [index]:

# Extract the first band (index 0)
magick input_multispectral.tif[0] band1.png

# Extract the fourth band (index 3)
magick input_multispectral.tif[3] band4.png

Combining Channels into a True-Color (RGB) Image

To reconstruct a natural true-color image, you must combine the Red, Green, and Blue bands into their corresponding color channels.

For Landsat 8/9, the standard visual channels correspond to:

  • Red: Band 4
  • Green: Band 3
  • Blue: Band 2

Use the -combine operator along with -auto-level to expand the dynamic range of the raw sensor data:

magick band4.tif band3.tif band2.tif -combine -auto-level true_color.png

If extracting directly from a multi-band GeoTIFF where Band 4 is index 3, Band 3 is index 2, and Band 2 is index 1:

magick input.tif[3] input.tif[2] input.tif[1] -combine -auto-level -depth 8 true_color.png

Creating a False-Color Infrared Composite

False-color imagery highlights environmental details such as vegetation density and water bodies. A standard Color Infrared (CIR) composite maps the Near-Infrared band to Red, the Red band to Green, and the Green band to Blue.

For Landsat 8/9:

  • Red Channel: Band 5 (NIR)
  • Green Channel: Band 4 (Red)
  • Blue Channel: Band 3 (Green)

Execute the following command:

magick band5.tif band4.tif band3.tif -combine -auto-level false_color_cir.png

In this output, healthy vegetation reflects strongly in the NIR spectrum and appears bright red, while water absorbs NIR and appears dark blue or black.

Adjusting Radiometric Contrast

Raw satellite imagery typically contains 12-bit or 16-bit integer values that look dark or washed out when viewed directly. ImageMagick provides several parameters to adjust tonal balance:

  • -auto-level: Stretches the dynamic range of each channel across the full spectrum.
  • -gamma <value>: Corrects brightness non-linearly (e.g., -gamma 1.2 brightens mid-tones).
  • -sigmoidal-contrast <contrast>x<midpoint>%: Increases contrast around a specific threshold without blowing out highlights or shadows.

Example composite with contrast correction:

magick band4.tif band3.tif band2.tif -combine -auto-level -gamma 1.15 -sigmoidal-contrast 3x50% output_enhanced.png
