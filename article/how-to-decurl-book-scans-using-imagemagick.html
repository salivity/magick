How to Decurl Book Scans Using ImageMagick

Scanning thick books often results in curved text and distorted margins near the binding spine, known as page curl. This guide explains how to correct geometric page curvature using ImageMagick’s command-line tools. You will learn how to identify the type of distortion, apply geometric correction algorithms using the -distort operator, and automate the flattening process for batch-scanned documents.

Understanding the Distortion Model

ImageMagick corrects page curl by reversing the geometric transformation caused by the curved surface of a book page. The two primary methods for correcting page curl are:

  1. Cylinder-to-Plane Mapping (Cylinder2Plane): Best for pages that curve uniformly around a central vertical axis, such as a cylindrical spine.
  2. Barrel/Pincushion Distortion (Barrel): Best for localized, non-linear curvature along the inner margins.

Method 1: Using Cylinder2Plane Distortion

When a page wraps around a book's spine, it resembles a section of a cylinder. The Cylinder2Plane distortion projects this curved surface back onto a flat plane.

The Command Syntax

magick input_page.png -distort Cylinder2Plane "FOV [Cylinder_Radius]" output_page.png
  • FOV (Field of View): The angular span of the cylinder in degrees (typically between 20 and 60).
  • Cylinder_Radius: The relative radius of the curvature.

Example

To flatten a moderately curved page scan:

magick scan_01.png -virtual-pixel white -distort Cylinder2Plane 45 output_01.png

The -virtual-pixel white setting ensures that any empty space created by unwarping the image is filled with a clean white background instead of transparency or black pixels.

Method 2: Correcting Curvature with Barrel Distortion

If the distortion is concentrated on one edge (the inner margin) rather than across the entire page, use polynomial barrel distortion.

The Command Syntax

magick input_page.png -distort Barrel "A B C D [X Y]" output_page.png
  • A, B, C: Coefficients defining the non-linear radial distortion.
  • D: Scaling factor (usually set to 1.0 - (A + B + C) to preserve original scale).
  • X, Y: The center coordinates of the distortion (optional; defaults to the image center).

Correcting a Left-Side Page (Gutter on Right)

To pull the compressed right edge outward:

magick left_page.png -virtual-pixel white -distort Barrel "0.0 0.0 -0.15 1.15" flattened_left.png

Correcting a Right-Side Page (Gutter on Left)

To shift the center of distortion toward the spine on the left:

magick right_page.png -virtual-pixel white -distort Barrel "0.0 0.0 0.15 0.85" flattened_right.png

Complete Workflow: Deskew, Decurl, and Trim

For best results, combine decurling with rotational alignment and margin cleanup:

magick scanned_raw.png \
  -deskew 40% \
  -virtual-pixel white \
  -distort Cylinder2Plane 35 \
  -fuzz 15% \
  -trim +repage \
  -bordercolor white -border 20 \
  cleaned_page.png
  • -deskew 40%: Automatically straightens any slight tilt before decurling.
  • -fuzz 15% -trim +repage: Automatically crops black scanner borders or dead space.
  • -border 20: Adds clean outer margins back to the document.

Batch Processing Scanned Pages

To process an entire folder of scans using the same parameters, use a shell loop:

Linux / macOS (Bash)

for file in *.png; do
  magick "$file" -virtual-pixel white -distort Cylinder2Plane 40 "flattened_${file}"
done

Windows (Command Prompt)

for %f in (*.png) do magick "%f" -virtual-pixel white -distort Cylinder2Plane 40 "flattened_%f"
