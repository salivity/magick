How to Stack Planetary Video Frames with ImageMagick

Stacking planetary astrophotography video frames reduces atmospheric noise and dramatically increases the signal-to-noise ratio to reveal fine surface details on targets like Jupiter, Saturn, or the Moon. While dedicated graphical software exists for this task, ImageMagick provides a lightweight, highly customizable, scriptable command-line alternative. This guide covers how to extract video frames, pre-process them, stack them using statistical blending methods, and apply initial sharpening using ImageMagick.

Step 1: Extract Video Frames

Before stacking, decompose your planetary video capture (typically recorded in AVI, SER, or MP4 formats) into uncompressed sequential image files like PNG or TIFF to preserve dynamic range.

If your ImageMagick installation includes the FFmpeg delegate, extract the frames directly with the following command:

magick capture.avi frame_%04d.png

Alternatively, you can use FFmpeg directly to ensure lossless extraction:

ffmpeg -i capture.avi -pix_fmt rgb24 frame_%04d.png

Step 2: Crop and Center the Planet

Planetary stacking produces the best results when the planet occupies the same pixel coordinates across all frames. If your telescope tracking has slight drift, you can crop to a Region of Interest (ROI) centered on the frame to eliminate extraneous black space and speed up processing:

magick mogrify -gravity center -crop 600x600+0+0 +repage frame_*.png

Step 3: Stack the Frames

ImageMagick utilizes the -evaluate-sequence operator to perform mathematical stacking across multiple images.

Mean Stacking (Average): Mean stacking sums pixel values across all frames and divides by the total frame count. This is ideal for smooth noise reduction across hundreds or thousands of frames.

magick frame_*.png -evaluate-sequence mean stacked_mean.png

Median Stacking: Median stacking selects the median pixel value at each coordinate. This method is effective for eliminating transient artifacts like satellite trails, hot pixels, or severe cosmic ray hits.

magick frame_*.png -evaluate-sequence median stacked_median.png

For high-bit-depth output (recommended for astronomical data), explicitly set the depth to 16-bit:

magick frame_*.png -depth 16 -evaluate-sequence mean stacked_mean.tiff

Step 4: Sharpening and Post-Processing

Planetary images appear soft and blurry immediately after stacking due to atmospheric seeing. To bring out latent planetary belts, craters, or rings, apply an unsharp mask:

magick stacked_mean.tiff -unsharp 0x3+1.5+0.005 planet_sharpened.png

In this command:

  • 0 lets ImageMagick determine the optimal radius automatically based on sigma.
  • 3 is the sigma value (blur radius to compare against).
  • 1.5 is the sharpening strength (amount).
  • 0.005 is the threshold to prevent sharpening flat background noise.

Adjust the sigma and amount parameters incrementally to enhance surface features without amplifying unwanted background grain.
