How to Use ImageMagick Shade for 3D Lighting

This guide covers how to use the ImageMagick -shade operator to create simulated directional 3D lighting and embossed effects on 2D images. You will learn the core command syntax, how the azimuth and elevation parameters dictate the light source's position, and practical techniques to apply these lighting effects to both grayscale heightmaps and full-color graphics.

Understanding the Shade Operator

The -shade operator simulates a distant light source shining across an image by treating pixel brightness values as a heightmap (or bump map). High-luminance pixels are rendered as peaks, while darker pixels are rendered as valleys.

The command requires two primary parameters formatted as azimuth x elevation:

magick input.png -shade {azimuth}x{elevation} output.png

1. Azimuth (Horizontal Angle)

Azimuth defines the directional angle of the light source along the horizontal plane, measured in degrees clockwise from the X-axis (East/3 o'clock):

  • 0°: Light comes from the East (right side).
  • 90°: Light comes from the South (bottom).
  • 180°: Light comes from the West (left side).
  • 270°: Light comes from the North (top).
  • 135° or 315°: Standard diagonal lighting angles (e.g., classic top-left or bottom-right illumination).

2. Elevation (Vertical Angle)

Elevation defines the height of the light source above the image horizon, measured in degrees from 0 to 90:

  • 0°: The light source is parallel to the surface, creating extreme contrast and long shadows.
  • 45°: A balanced, natural directional light angle.
  • 90°: The light source is directly overhead, producing minimal directional shading.

Basic Shading Examples

Standard Directional Shading

To illuminate an image from the top-left at a 45-degree angle:

magick input.png -shade 135x45 shaded_output.png

Low-Angle Harsh Lighting

To create stark, dramatic shadows with a light source low on the horizon from the left:

magick input.png -shade 180x15 low_angle_output.png

Applying 3D Shading to Color Images

By default, the -shade operator outputs a grayscale rendering of the calculated light and shadow. To retain the original image's color while applying the 3D lighting texture, use composition modes such as HardLight or Multiply.

Method: Shading and Blending in a Single Command

magick input.png \( +clone -shade 135x45 \) -compose HardLight -composite textured_color.png

How it works:

  1. input.png loads the original full-color image.
  2. \( +clone -shade 135x45 \) creates an in-memory copy and applies the directional shade.
  3. -compose HardLight -composite merges the shaded map back onto the color image, preserving base colors while adding highlights and shadows.

Tips for Better Shading Results

  • Pre-blur for smoother surfaces: Sharp pixel edges can produce jagged lighting artifacts. Applying a subtle Gaussian blur before shading smooths out harsh transitions: 
    magick input.png \( +clone -blur 0x2 -shade 135x45 \) -compose HardLight -composite smooth_shaded.png
  • Enhance contrast on heightmaps: If shading subtle textures, apply -normalize or -auto-level before -shade to maximize the perceived depth of the peaks and valleys.
