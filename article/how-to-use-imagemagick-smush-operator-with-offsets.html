How to Use ImageMagick Smush Operator with Offsets

This article explains how the ImageMagick smush operator handles horizontal image concatenation using custom offset values. You will learn the mechanics behind transparency detection, how horizontal smushing differs from standard appending, and how positive, negative, or zero offsets control the exact spacing and overlap between adjacent images.

Understanding the +smush Operator

In ImageMagick, the smush operator joins a sequence of images together in a single direction while accounting for transparency. The plus sign variant (+smush) executes a horizontal concatenation, placing images from left to right. The minus sign variant (-smush) handles vertical concatenation from top to bottom.

Unlike the standard +append operator—which joins images strictly edge-to-edge based on their outer bounding boxes—+smush analyzes the alpha channel of adjacent images. It attempts to slide the second image toward the first until their non-transparent pixels meet.

How Custom Offsets Function

The +smush operator accepts an integer offset parameter:

magick image1.png image2.png +smush <offset> output.png

The offset value defines the exact pixel distance between the closest non-transparent pixels of the two images:

  • Zero Offset (+smush 0): The second image slides leftward until its non-transparent pixels sit directly adjacent to the non-transparent pixels of the first image.
  • Positive Offset (+smush 20): The operator slides the second image until a 20-pixel gap remains between the nearest opaque pixels of both images.
  • Negative Offset (+smush -20): The operator forces the images to overlap, moving the second image 20 pixels beyond the point where their opaque pixels first make contact.

Smushing Images Without Transparency

If the source images do not contain an alpha channel (such as standard JPEGs or fully opaque PNGs), +smush treats the outer pixel dimensions of the images as the boundary.

In this scenario:

  • +smush 0 yields the exact same result as +append, placing the images directly edge-to-edge.
  • +smush 15 introduces a 15-pixel empty gap between the images.
  • +smush -15 overlaps the two images by 15 pixels, with the right-hand image rendered on top of the left-hand image in the overlapping zone.

Alignment and Canvas Height

When images of varying heights are horizontally smushed, ImageMagick expands the resulting canvas to match the height of the tallest image. By default, shorter images are aligned to the top-left edge of the canvas. You can change vertical alignment prior to smushing by setting the -gravity option (e.g., -gravity Center or -gravity South).

Example Usage

To horizontally concatenate two transparent icons with an exact 10-pixel gap between their visible contents:

magick icon1.png icon2.png -gravity Center +smush 10 result.png

To create a horizontally overlapping collage effect where each consecutive photo overlaps the previous one by 50 pixels:

magick photo1.jpg photo2.jpg photo3.jpg +smush -50 collage.jpg
