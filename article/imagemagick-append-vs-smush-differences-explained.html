ImageMagick Append vs Smush Differences Explained

ImageMagick provides two primary methods for joining multiple images together: the traditional append operator and the newer smush operator. While both operations can stitch images horizontally or vertically into a single output, they handle spacing, boundaries, and transparency differently. This article breaks down the technical and practical differences between -append and -smush to help you choose the right command for your image processing workflows.

Understanding the Append Operator

The append operator joins images strictly edge-to-edge based on their total bounding dimensions.

  • Horizontal stacking: +append places images side-by-side from left to right.
  • Vertical stacking: -append stacks images on top of each other from top to bottom.

When using append, ImageMagick treats each image as a solid rectangle. If images differ in width or height, the smaller images are padded with the current background color to match the largest image in the opposite dimension. The operator does not accept an offset argument; spacing can only be altered by manually adding margins or borders to individual frames prior to appending.

Understanding the Smush Operator

The smush operator is a smarter, content-aware alternative to append. Introduced to provide greater control over spacing, it attempts to overlap images until their opaque pixels collide, or it separates them by a specific distance.

  • Horizontal smushing: +smush <offset> joins images from left to right.
  • Vertical smushing: -smush <offset> joins images from top to bottom.

Unlike append, smush requires an integer offset parameter:

  • A positive offset (e.g., +smush 10) adds a fixed gap of 10 pixels between the images.
  • An offset of zero (+smush 0) pushes the images together until their actual non-transparent pixels touch, regardless of transparent padding around the borders.
  • A negative offset (e.g., +smush -5) forces the non-transparent portions of adjacent images to overlap by 5 pixels.

Key Differences

  1. Parameter Requirements: append takes no arguments, whereas smush always requires a numerical distance offset.
  2. Handling Transparency: append only considers the rectangular canvas boundaries of each file. In contrast, smush analyzes alpha channels and pushes transparent areas into one another until solid pixels meet.
  3. Spacing Flexibility: With append, introducing gaps or overlapping elements requires pre-configuring borders or canvases. With smush, gaps and overlaps are directly controlled by specifying positive or negative pixel values in the command itself.

When to Use Each

Use append when you need predictable, fast tiling of rectangular images, charts, or photo grids where the outer canvas dimensions represent the intended layout edges.

Use smush when working with irregularly shaped graphics, transparent PNGs, custom spacing between elements, or when you need tight packing without having to trim whitespace manually beforehand.
