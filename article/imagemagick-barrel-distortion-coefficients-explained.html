ImageMagick Barrel Distortion Coefficients Explained

ImageMagick’s Barrel distortion operator uses a four-term polynomial equation parameterized by coefficients \(a\), \(b\), \(c\), and \(d\) to remap pixels and correct optical lens aberrations. Lens imperfections typically produce barrel distortion, where straight lines bow outward, or pincushion distortion, where lines bend inward toward the center. By tuning these four parameters, ImageMagick reconstructs a geometrically corrected image by adjusting the radial distance of each pixel relative to the image center.

The Radial Distortion Formula

The operator maps the destination radius (\(r\)) from the center of the image to the source radius (\(r_{src}\)) in the original image using the following polynomial:

\[r_{src} = r \cdot (a \cdot r^3 + b \cdot r^2 + c \cdot r + d)\]

In this formula, \(r\) represents the normalized radial distance from the center (where \(r = 0\) is the optical center and \(r = 1\) is typically half the minimum image dimension). The coefficients define how severely and at what distance from the center the pixel positions are shifted.

The Function of Each Coefficient

  • Coefficient \(d\) (Base Scale): The \(d\) term is the linear scaling factor (\(r \cdot d\)). It controls the overall magnification or zoom level of the image without introducing curvature. If \(d = 1\) and \(a, b, c = 0\), the image remains unchanged. To preserve the scale of the image at the boundary (\(r = 1\)), the coefficients typically satisfy the constraint \(a + b + c + d = 1\). Consequently, \(d\) is often calculated as \(1 - (a + b + c)\).
  • Coefficient \(b\) (Primary Curvature): The \(b\) term acts on \(r^3\) and serves as the primary parameter for correcting standard, symmetric radial distortion. A negative \(b\) value corrects traditional barrel distortion by pushing pixels outward toward the edges, while a positive \(b\) value counters pincushion distortion by pulling pixels toward the center.
  • Coefficient \(a\) (Outer Edge Curvature): The \(a\) term acts on \(r^4\) and targets the outermost edges and corners of the frame. Because the influence of \(r^4\) rises sharply near the perimeter, \(a\) is used to correct severe wide-angle distortion or complex "moustache" distortion, where barrel distortion in the center transitions to pincushion distortion at the far edges.
  • Coefficient \(c\) (Mid-Tone Curvature): The \(c\) term acts on \(r^2\) and primarily influences the mid-field area between the center and the edges. It allows fine-tuning for lenses that do not follow a simple cubic curve, balancing the transition between the center of the frame and the regions dominated by \(b\) and \(a\).

Practical Implementation

When executing the -distort Barrel operator, values are supplied in order: A B C [D]. If \(d\) is omitted, ImageMagick automatically sets \(d = 1 - (a + b + c)\) to maintain scale at the edges. For standard wide-angle corrections, users typically leave \(a\) and \(c\) at zero and adjust \(b\) to flatten curvature, introducing \(a\) only when wide-angle or fisheye optics produce complex edge warping.
