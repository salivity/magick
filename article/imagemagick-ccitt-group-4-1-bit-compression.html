ImageMagick CCITT Group 4 1-Bit Compression

This article explains how ImageMagick processes, converts, and applies 1-bit monochrome bitmap compression schemes, focusing specifically on CCITT Group 4 (Fax) compression. It covers the internal mechanics of bilevel image handling, format compatibility, and the precise command-line configurations required to produce standards-compliant, highly compressed 1-bit TIFF and PDF documents.

Understanding 1-Bit Monochrome and CCITT Group 4

1-bit monochrome (or bilevel) images contain only two possible color values per pixel: pure black and pure white. CCITT Group 4 (also known as ITU-T T.6 or Group 4 Fax) is a lossless data compression algorithm specifically optimized for bilevel images.

Unlike byte-oriented compression schemes like LZW or Deflate/ZIP, CCITT Group 4 uses a two-dimensional coding scheme that tracks line-by-line runs of black and white pixels. It references the previous line to encode the current line, resulting in exceptional compression ratios for scanned documents, architectural drawings, and text.

How ImageMagick Processes 1-Bit Images

ImageMagick internally handles images in high color depths (typically Quantum 16 or 8). When writing to a 1-bit format with CCITT Group 4 compression, ImageMagick performs the following pipeline:

  1. Color Quantization and Thresholding: Any grayscale or color image is reduced to two colors using thresholding or dithering algorithms.
  2. Bit-Depth Reduction: The image structure is reduced to 1 bit per sample (-depth 1).
  3. Delegation to LibTIFF: For TIFF outputs, ImageMagick passes the bilevel raw pixel data to the underlying libtiff library, which handles the exact bit-packing and encoding specified by the CCITT Group 4 standard.

Converting Images to CCITT Group 4 with ImageMagick

To generate a valid CCITT Group 4 image, you must explicitly enforce a 1-bit depth and select the compression scheme.

Basic Conversion Syntax

magick input.png -monochrome -compress Group4 output.tif

In this command:

  • -monochrome: Enforces a 2-color black-and-white dithered palette.
  • -compress Group4: Instructs the encoder to use CCITT Group 4 compression (equivalent to -compress Fax in older conventions).
  • output.tif: The target container format.

Recommended Production Command

To prevent unexpected dithering artifacts on clean text or line art, thresholding is preferred over standard monochrome dithering:

magick input.jpg -colorspace Gray -threshold 50% -depth 1 -compress Group4 output.tif
  • -colorspace Gray: Ensures color channels are combined into a single luminance channel.
  • -threshold 50%: Forces pixels above 50% brightness to white and below to black without dithering patterns.
  • -depth 1: Explicitly sets the output channel depth to 1 bit per pixel.

Supported Formats for CCITT Group 4

ImageMagick supports CCITT Group 4 compression in specific file formats:

  • TIFF (.tif, .tiff): The primary and most common standard for Group 4 compression. ImageMagick writes the appropriate TIFF tags (Compression=4, PhotometricInterpretation=0 or 1, and BitsPerSample=1).
  • PDF (.pdf): When generating PDF files from bilevel images, specifying -compress Group4 embeds the raster image stream using the standard /CCITTFaxDecode filter, keeping PDF file sizes extremely small.

Common Issues and Solutions

Image Inversion

Group 4 TIFFs can store black as 0 (WhiteIsZero) or black as 1 (BlackIsZero). If the output appears inverted in certain viewers:

magick input.png -threshold 50% -negate -depth 1 -compress Group4 output.tif

Compression Failures on Non-Bilevel Data

If -compress Group4 is specified without reducing the image to 1 bit, ImageMagick will either automatically force a threshold conversion or fail with an encoder error depending on the output format. Always include -depth 1 or -threshold to ensure predictable results.
