ImageMagick Chop Along the Horizontal Axis

The ImageMagick -chop operator, when applied along the horizontal axis, removes a vertical strip or specific columns of pixels from an image and seamlessly shifts the remaining content together to reduce the total width. Unlike cropping, which isolates a designated rectangular region and discards the rest, horizontal chopping extracts a specified width of pixels from the interior or edges of an image and stitches the remaining left and right sections together.

How Horizontal Chopping Works

The -chop operator uses the standard geometry syntax: WIDTHxHEIGHT+X+Y. When targeting the horizontal axis, the vertical parameter (HEIGHT) is typically set to 0, focusing the operation purely on the horizontal plane.

  • Width (WIDTH): Defines the number of pixel columns to delete.
  • X-Offset (+X): Defines the horizontal starting point where the cut begins, measured from the image's origin (top-left by default, or dictated by -gravity).

When executed, ImageMagick deletes WIDTH columns starting at offset X. Any pixels to the right of the cut are shifted leftward by WIDTH pixels to close the gap, reducing the overall image width by the chopped amount while leaving the height unchanged.

Syntax and Examples

To remove a 50-pixel-wide vertical strip starting 100 pixels from the left side of an image:

magick input.png -chop 50x0+100+0 output.png

If combined with the -gravity setting, chopping adjusts its reference point:

  • Removing Left Margins: Setting -gravity West -chop 30x0 removes 30 pixels directly from the left edge.
  • Removing Right Margins: Setting -gravity East -chop 30x0 removes 30 pixels directly from the right edge.

Common Use Cases

  • Removing Margins or Borders: Quickly stripping uniform blank space or unwanted borders from the left or right sides of images.
  • Deleting Content Columns: Excising unwanted vertical elements (such as visual dividers, timestamps, or column artifacts) from spreadsheets, scanned documents, or screenshots.
  • Batch Trimming: Automating the reduction of canvas widths across sets of images where a fixed column slice must be discarded.
