ImageMagick Chop Operator Explained

The ImageMagick -chop operator is a command-line tool designed to remove a horizontal or vertical slice from an image and seamlessly stitch the remaining pieces together. This article explains how the -chop operator functions, breaks down its geometry syntax for removing rows or columns of pixels, and highlights the key differences between chopping and standard cropping.

How the Chop Operator Works

Unlike the -crop operator, which extracts a specified region and discards everything else, the -chop operator does the exact opposite. It cuts out a defined strip of pixels and discards it, then collapses the remaining image content inward to fill the missing space. This reduces the total dimensions (width or height) of the image by the exact size of the removed slice.

Removing Horizontal Slices

To remove a horizontal slice, you define a height dimension and a vertical offset while keeping the width set to zero.

  • Geometry format: 0xHEIGHT+0+Y_OFFSET
  • Mechanics: The operator cuts a full-width strip of pixels with a height equal to HEIGHT, starting at Y_OFFSET pixels from the top edge. The bottom portion of the image is then shifted upward to meet the top portion.

For example, to remove a 50-pixel-high horizontal strip starting 100 pixels from the top:

magick input.jpg -chop 0x50+0+100 output.jpg

The resulting image retains its original width, but its height is reduced by 50 pixels.

Removing Vertical Slices

To remove a vertical slice, you specify a width dimension and a horizontal offset while keeping the height set to zero.

  • Geometry format: WIDTHx0+X_OFFSET+0
  • Mechanics: The operator cuts a full-height column of pixels with a width equal to WIDTH, starting at X_OFFSET pixels from the left edge. The right portion of the image is shifted to the left to close the gap.

For example, to remove an 80-pixel-wide vertical strip starting 200 pixels from the left:

magick input.jpg -chop 80x0+200+0 output.jpg

The resulting image retains its original height, but its width is reduced by 80 pixels.

The Role of the Gravity Setting

By default, offsets are calculated from the top-left corner of the image (NorthWest). You can modify the origin point of the chop by applying the -gravity setting prior to the -chop command.

  • Setting -gravity South calculates vertical offsets starting from the bottom edge.
  • Setting -gravity East calculates horizontal offsets starting from the right edge.

For instance, to remove 30 pixels directly from the bottom edge of an image:

magick input.jpg -gravity South -chop 0x30 output.jpg

Summary of Chop vs. Crop

  • Crop (-crop): Keeps the selected bounding box and deletes all surrounding pixels.
  • Chop (-chop): Deletes the selected slice and joins the surrounding pieces into a single, smaller image.
