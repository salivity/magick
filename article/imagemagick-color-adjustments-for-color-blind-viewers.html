ImageMagick Color Adjustments for Color Blind Viewers

This guide explains how to use ImageMagick to adapt digital images for viewers with color vision deficiencies, such as protanopia, deuteranopia, and tritanopia. By applying color matrix transformations, selective color replacement, and hue shifts from the command line, you can increase visual contrast and ensure critical details remain distinguishable to color-blind audiences.

1. Applying a Color Matrix Transformation

Color matrix transformations allow you to recalculate RGB values mathematically, transferring detail from color channels that are difficult to distinguish (such as red and green) into channels that are easily perceived (such as blue). This technique, often referred to as Daltonization or channel mixing, preserves image information without losing critical contrast.

To apply a 3x3 or 5x5 color correction matrix in ImageMagick, use the -color-matrix operator:

magick input.png -color-matrix " \
  0.0 0.0 0.0 \
  0.7 1.0 0.0 \
  0.7 0.0 1.0 " output_adjusted.png

In this example, information from the red channel is redistributed into the green and blue channels, increasing contrast for individuals with red-green color blindness (protanopia and deuteranopia).

2. Selective Color Replacement for Infographics and UI

In data visualizations, maps, and UI elements, problematic color pairs (such as pure red and pure green) can be replaced with accessible alternatives like blue and orange or magenta and yellow.

Use the -fuzz and -fill operators to replace specific shades:

magick chart.png -fuzz 15% -fill "#D55E00" -opaque "red" \
                 -fuzz 15% -fill "#0072B2" -opaque "green" \
                 accessible_chart.png
  • -fuzz 15%: Matches variations of the target color within a 15% tolerance range.
  • -fill "#D55E00": Specifies an accessible color (e.g., vermilion/orange).
  • -opaque "red": Targets the color to be replaced.

3. Shifting Hue and Boosting Saturation

When detailed channel matrix tuning is not required, rotating the entire hue spectrum can shift conflicting hues into distinguishable ranges. Increasing saturation also helps color-deficient viewers distinguish between nearby tones.

Use the -modulate option to alter brightness, saturation, and hue simultaneously:

magick input.jpg -modulate 100,130,66 output_hue_shift.jpg
  • 100: Preserves original brightness.
  • 130: Increases saturation by 30%.
  • 66: Shifts the hue by approximately 120 degrees counterclockwise, moving red tones closer to yellow or magenta.

4. Enhancing Luminance and Edge Contrast

Color-blind viewers rely heavily on luminance (light-to-dark contrast) and edge boundaries rather than chromatic differences. If two adjacent colors share the same lightness, adjusting tone curves or applying edge emphasis makes boundaries clear.

To expand tonal range across the image:

magick input.png -auto-level -contrast-stretch 1%x1% output_contrast.png

To introduce subtle edge definition between adjacent color blocks:

magick input.png \( +clone -edge 1 -negate \) -compose Multiply -composite output_outlined.png

This command duplicates the image, extracts its edges, and blends them over the original graphic, ensuring adjacent color fields remain distinct regardless of the viewer's color perception.
