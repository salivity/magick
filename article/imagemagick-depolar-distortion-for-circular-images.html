ImageMagick DePolar Distortion for Circular Images

This article explains how the DePolar distortion operator works in ImageMagick, specifically focusing on its ability to unroll circular and radial graphics into flat, rectangular images. It covers the mathematical transformation behind the operator, how the coordinate mapping translates visual features, and the primary use cases for unwrapping circular visual data.

Understanding the DePolar Operator

The DePolar operator performs a coordinate transformation that converts an image from polar coordinates \((r, \theta)\) to Cartesian coordinates \((x, y)\). When applied to a circular image, it effectively slices the circle along a radius and "unrolls" it into a standard rectangle.

In this transformation:

  • Horizontal Axis (Width): Represents the angle (\(\theta\)) around the circular image, typically traversing 360 degrees from left to right.
  • Vertical Axis (Height): Represents the radial distance (\(r\)) from the center of the circle to its outer perimeter.

By default, the center point of the circular image is mapped to the bottom edge of the resulting rectangle, while the outer circumference is mapped to the top edge.

How DePolar Affects Circular Content

Applying DePolar fundamentally changes how curved and radial features appear:

  • Concentric Rings Become Horizontal Lines: Any circular path or ring centered on the original image becomes a straight, horizontal band across the output image.
  • Radial Spokes Become Vertical Lines: Lines emanating outward from the center point of the circle are straightened into vertical lines spanning the height of the output.
  • Circular Text Becomes Linear Text: Text printed along an arc, such as the lettering on a coin, bottle cap, or circular stamp, is straightened out into standard, readable horizontal text.

Practical Applications

The DePolar operator is predominantly used in image processing and computer vision workflows requiring radial unwrapping:

  1. Optical Character Recognition (OCR): Standard OCR engines struggle with text curved along circular paths. Unrolling the circle with DePolar aligns the text horizontally, making automated recognition significantly more reliable.
  2. Panoramic Unwrapping: Images captured with upward-facing 360-degree fisheye lenses or curved conical mirrors produce circular "donut" images. DePolar unwraps these circular projections into traditional wide-angle rectangular panoramas.
  3. Inspection and Quality Control: In manufacturing, inspecting circular parts (like bearings, seals, or printed disc labels) is simplified when the perimeter is converted into a uniform rectangular strip.

Syntax and Control Arguments

In ImageMagick, the basic command takes the following form:

magick input.png -distort DePolar 0 output.png

Setting the parameter to 0 tells ImageMagick to automatically calculate the center point, outer radius, and output dimensions based on the source image dimensions.

Users can also provide custom parameters to control the transformation:

  • Radius (Max and Min): Setting an inner radius allows you to extract an annular band (a ring) rather than the entire disk, ignoring the inner core.
  • Center Coordinates: Explicitly defining the \(X\) and \(Y\) center prevents distortion if the circular subject is not perfectly centered in the source frame.
  • Angle Range: You can restrict the unwrapping to a specific angular segment instead of the full 360-degree circumference.
