ImageMagick Dissolve Alpha Blending Explained

The ImageMagick -dissolve operator controls alpha blending between a foreground (source) and background (destination) image by scaling their respective alpha channels before performing standard Porter-Duff compositing. Depending on whether a single percentage or a dual-percentage parameter is provided, ImageMagick either executes a continuous crossfade from 0% to 200% or explicitly scales the transparency of each layer independently.

The Single-Value Crossfade Mechanism

When you provide a single percentage to the -dissolve argument, ImageMagick does not treat 50% as an equal balance between both images. Instead, it utilizes a 0% to 200% scale designed to facilitate seamless transitions:

  • 0% to 100% (Fade-in foreground): The background image maintains 100% of its original alpha channel. The foreground layer’s alpha channel is multiplied by percentage / 100. At 0%, the foreground is completely invisible. At 100%, the foreground is fully opaque and overlays the background normally.
  • 100% to 200% (Fade-out background): The foreground image remains at 100% opacity. The background layer's alpha channel is reduced linearly according to the formula (200 - percentage) / 100. At 150%, the background is reduced to 50% opacity under the fully opaque foreground. At 200%, the background is completely transparent, leaving only the foreground visible.

This behavior ensures that an animation incrementing smoothly from 0% to 200% transitions seamlessly from pure background, through standard overlay composition, to pure foreground.

Dual-Percentage Independent Control

To bypass the continuous crossfade scale and assign explicit transparency weights to each layer, -dissolve accepts a two-value syntax formatted as src_percent x dst_percent (for example, -dissolve 50x50):

  • src_percent: Scales the alpha channel of the foreground (source) image. A value of 50 halves the opacity of every foreground pixel.
  • dst_percent: Scales the alpha channel of the background (destination) image. A value of 50 halves the opacity of every background pixel before the foreground is composited on top.

Using explicit values such as 50x50 ensures both images contribute equal amounts of opacity to the combined output, regardless of the single-value transition rules.

Porter-Duff Composite Application

Once the -dissolve parameter determines and multiplies the individual alpha levels of each image, ImageMagick calculates the final pixel color using the standard Porter-Duff Over composition operator:

  1. Color Weighting: The visible color is computed as Color_result = (Color_src * Alpha_src) + (Color_dst * Alpha_dst * (1 - Alpha_src)).
  2. Resulting Alpha: The combined output alpha channel is calculated as Alpha_result = Alpha_src + Alpha_dst * (1 - Alpha_src).

Because the Over operation places the source on top of the destination, an opaque foreground will always occlude the background beneath it. The -dissolve operator balances the layers strictly by modifying the Alpha_src and Alpha_dst variables before this blending equation is evaluated.
