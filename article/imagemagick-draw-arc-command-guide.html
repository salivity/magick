ImageMagick Draw Arc Command Guide

This article explains how to render an arc segment in ImageMagick using the -draw command. You will learn the exact syntax structure, how coordinates and degree angles define the curve, and how to apply practical command-line examples to draw arcs on canvas backgrounds or existing images.

The Arc Command Syntax

In ImageMagick, the -draw primitive for drawing an arc segment uses the following syntax:

-draw "arc x0,y0 x1,y1 a0,a1"

Parameter Breakdown

  • x0,y0: The coordinates (in pixels) for the upper-left corner of the bounding rectangle that encloses the ellipse/circle of the arc.
  • x1,y1: The coordinates for the lower-right corner of the bounding rectangle.
  • a0: The starting angle of the arc in degrees.
  • a1: The ending angle of the arc in degrees.

Angle Measurement Rules

  • 0 degrees begins at the 3 o'clock position (the positive X-axis).
  • Angles increase in a clockwise direction (90 degrees is 6 o'clock, 180 degrees is 9 o'clock, and 270 degrees is 12 o'clock).

Practical Examples

1. Basic Arc on a Blank Canvas

To draw a standard semi-circle arc, define a bounding box, set the fill to none (so the arc does not close into a pie wedge), specify a stroke color, and provide the start and end angles:

magick -size 300x300 xc:white \
  -stroke black -strokewidth 4 -fill none \
  -draw "arc 50,50 250,250 0,180" \
  output_arc.png

This draws a lower semi-circle from 0° (3 o'clock) to 180° (9 o'clock) inside a 200x200 pixel bounding box.

2. Elliptical Arc

If the bounding box coordinates do not form a square, the arc will stretch along the elliptical path:

magick -size 400x200 xc:white \
  -stroke red -strokewidth 3 -fill none \
  -draw "arc 20,20 380,180 45,225" \
  elliptical_arc.png

3. Filled Arc (Pie Slice)

If you define a -fill color other than none, ImageMagick connects the endpoints of the arc to the center of the bounding box to form a filled wedge:

magick -size 200x200 xc:white \
  -fill blue -stroke black -strokewidth 2 \
  -draw "arc 20,20 180,180 0,90" \
  pie_slice.png
