ImageMagick EdgeOut vs Standard Edge Detection

ImageMagick provides multiple techniques for finding boundaries in an image, ranging from classical convolution filters to mathematical morphology. While standard edge detection techniques compute intensity gradients across pixel neighborhoods to highlight transitions, the morphology EdgeOut operator applies structural dilation and subtraction to isolate pixels lying strictly outside an object's boundary. Understanding the differences between these two approaches comes down to their mathematical mechanics, edge alignment, and optimal use cases.

Algorithmic Mechanics

Standard edge detection in ImageMagick—most commonly invoked via the -edge <radius> operator or explicit convolution kernels like Sobel, Roberts, and Laplacian—relies on linear convolution. These algorithms calculate the first or second derivative of pixel intensity across an image. They measure how rapidly color or brightness changes in a local window and mark the peaks of these gradients as edges.

In contrast, EdgeOut belongs to mathematical morphology and operates using non-linear neighborhood ranking. Specifically, EdgeOut calculates the morphological external gradient:

\[\text{EdgeOut} = \text{Dilate}(I) - I\]

ImageMagick first dilates the original image using a defined structuring element (kernel), expanding the boundaries of foreground objects outward. It then subtracts the original image from this dilated result.

Edge Alignment and Thickness

The most significant visual difference between the two approaches is the physical location of the resulting edge relative to the source object:

  • Standard Edge Detection: Edges are centered symmetrically on the boundary of highest contrast. Half of the edge typically overlaps the foreground object, while the other half overlaps the background.
  • EdgeOut: The edge is positioned entirely outside the original foreground boundary. The original shape remains completely untouched inside the perimeter, with the detected line forming a halo or outer border directly adjacent to the object's perimeter.

The thickness in EdgeOut is explicitly determined by the radius and shape of the structuring element (such as Disk, Diamond, or Square), allowing precise, uniform pixel-width control. Standard edge filters often produce anti-aliased, feathered, or variable-width lines depending on the steepness of the original image gradient.

Input Data and Artifact Handling

Standard convolution-based edge detection excels on continuous-tone photographs and natural scenes where edge boundaries are soft or defined by subtle lighting changes. However, it is sensitive to high-frequency noise, which often produces spurious edge artifacts unless pre-filtered with a Gaussian blur.

The EdgeOut operator is primarily suited for segmented, binary, or high-contrast graphics, masks, and silhouettes. Because it subtracts the exact pixel values of the original image from the dilated version, applying EdgeOut to continuous-tone photos can create harsh, solarized relief effects rather than clean contours.

When to Use Each Method

Choose standard edge detection (-edge) when processing natural photographs, generating line drawings from continuous-tone inputs, or identifying general optical features where gradient intensity matters.

Choose morphology EdgeOut (-morphology EdgeOut <kernel>) when creating outlines around transparent PNG graphics, generating outward-expanding boundary strokes around text or masks, or when the edge must strictly avoid encroaching on the interior pixels of the subject.
