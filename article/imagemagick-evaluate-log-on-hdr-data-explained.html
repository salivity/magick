ImageMagick Evaluate Log on HDR Data Explained

This article provides an overview of how the -evaluate Log operator processes high-dynamic-range (HDR) imagery in ImageMagick. It details the underlying mathematical transformation, explains how logarithmic scaling compresses extreme luminance ranges into viewable formats, and highlights the practical effects of this operator on HDR shadow details and highlights.

The -evaluate Log operator in ImageMagick applies a logarithmic transfer function across the pixel channels of an image. In standard-dynamic-range (SDR) images, luminance values are strictly confined between black and white (typically 0.0 to 1.0 in normalized floating-point terms). However, HDR images capture scenes with physical light values that span multiple orders of magnitude, often containing values far greater than 1.0. Applying -evaluate Log specifically targets this broad spectrum by non-linearly compressing the dynamic range.

When processing HDR data, the log operator acts as a basic tone-mapping algorithm. High-intensity values that would otherwise clip to pure white or require aggressive truncation are smoothly brought down toward a perceptually balanced range. Mathematically, the operator applies the formula:

output = log(value * input + 1.0) / log(value + 1.0)

In this formula, input represents the normalized pixel value, and value is the user-defined scaling constant provided as the operator's argument. Because the logarithm grows rapidly at lower numbers and levels off as numbers become substantially larger, dark and midtone values are stretched upward, while extreme highlights are heavily compressed.

The visual result on HDR data is twofold:

  1. Highlight Retention: Extremely bright pixel values—such as direct light sources, specular reflections, or sunlit skies—are remapped into a finite range without losing their relative tonal relationships.
  2. Shadow Expansion: Low-end luminance levels that appear visually crushed in linear representation become perceptible, pulling subtle details out of the shadows.

The strength of the compression depends entirely on the numerical parameter supplied with the operator. A smaller value preserves a more linear distribution, while a larger value increases the steepness of the initial curve, yielding severe compression on the brightest HDR regions and maximizing contrast expansion across the lowest values. Consequently, -evaluate Log serves as an essential utility in ImageMagick workflows for converting raw sensor data, astronomical captures, and 32-bit floating-point HDR renders into standard formats suitable for standard displays.
