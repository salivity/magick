ImageMagick Evaluate-Sequence Across Channels

ImageMagick's -evaluate-sequence operator merges multiple images mathematically by applying functions such as Mean, Add, Max, or Multiply across the entire stack. This article explains how to restrict -evaluate-sequence to individual image channels using the -channel setting, as well as how to perform different sequence operations on separate channels using -separate and -combine.

Restricting -evaluate-sequence to Specific Channels

By default, -evaluate-sequence operates on all active color channels simultaneously. To target specific channels, define the active channel mask with the -channel flag before invoking the operator.

Example: Apply to a Single Channel

To calculate the average (mean) of multiple images exclusively for the Red channel while leaving other channels untouched from the first image in the sequence:

magick image1.png image2.png image3.png -channel R -evaluate-sequence Mean +channel output.png
  • -channel R: Restricts subsequent operations to the Red channel. You can specify combinations like R, G, B, A, or color spaces like Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, Black.
  • -evaluate-sequence Mean: Merges the pixel values of the loaded images across the active channel.
  • +channel: Resets the channel mask to default, ensuring subsequent operators affect all channels.

Processing Different Channels Independently

If you need to apply different evaluate-sequence methods to different channels (for example, finding the Max of the Red channel and the Min of the Blue channel across an image set), separate the channels first, process each sequence, and recombine them.

Step-by-Step Channel Separation and Recombination

  1. Separate the images into their individual channel planes.
  2. Group the corresponding channel layers across all images.
  3. Apply -evaluate-sequence independently to each channel group.
  4. Combine the resulting channel images back into a single output.
magick \( image1.png image2.png -channel R -separate +channel -evaluate-sequence Max \) \
       \( image1.png image2.png -channel G -separate +channel -evaluate-sequence Mean \) \
       \( image1.png image2.png -channel B -separate +channel -evaluate-sequence Min \) \
       -combine output.png

In this command:

  • Parentheses \( ... \) create isolated sub-stacks for each channel.
  • -separate extracts the targeted channel as a grayscale image.
  • Each sub-stack applies its own -evaluate-sequence method (Max, Mean, Min).
  • -combine merges the three resulting grayscale layers back into the final RGB image.

Key Considerations

  • ImageMagick 7 vs. ImageMagick 6: In ImageMagick 7, use the magick command instead of convert. ImageMagick 7 strictly respects channel masks during sequence operations, whereas ImageMagick 6 may require explicit channel separation (-separate) depending on the build and sequence method used.
  • Alpha Channel Handling: If working with transparent images, ensure the alpha channel is explicitly included (e.g., -channel RGBA) if you want transparency values factored into the sequence calculation. To prevent alpha modification, omit A from the -channel parameter.
