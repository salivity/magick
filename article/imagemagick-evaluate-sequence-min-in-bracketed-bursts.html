ImageMagick Evaluate-Sequence Min in Bracketed Bursts

Using the evaluate-sequence Min operator in ImageMagick across a series of bracketed bursts generates a composite image composed exclusively of the lowest pixel values found at each corresponding coordinate. Depending on whether the bracketing is based on exposure or temporal movement, this operation yields an image dominated by the darkest exposures, effectively preserving blown-out highlight details while crushing shadows into pure black, or retaining the darkest moving objects across a static scene.

How the Min Operator Works

The -evaluate-sequence Min command performs an element-wise, per-channel comparison across all images loaded in the current sequence stack. For every individual pixel coordinate \((x, y)\) and color channel (Red, Green, Blue, Alpha), ImageMagick compares the values across all input frames and assigns the absolute minimum value to the output image:

\[P_{\text{out}}(x, y) = \min(P_1(x, y), P_2(x, y), \dots, P_n(x, y))\]

Because it evaluates each color channel independently by default, the final color at any coordinate is formed by the darkest red, green, and blue components available across the entire burst.

Output on Exposure-Bracketed Bursts

When processing an exposure-bracketed burst (captures taken at varying shutter speeds, apertures, or ISO values):

  • Preserved Highlight Detail: High-exposure shots typically blow out bright areas (like skies, light bulbs, or reflections). Because the underexposed shots in the burst contain lower digital values in these bright zones, the Min operator selects the pixel data from the darkest frames, successfully retaining highlight texture.
  • Crushed Shadows and Midtones: In areas where normal or overexposed images contain visible shadow detail, the underexposed images contain near-zero or zero values. The Min operator forces these areas to black, eliminating midtone information and shadow recovery.
  • Severely Darkened Final Output: The overall composite resembles the most heavily underexposed frame of the bracket, but with extreme contrast and potential color shifts caused by independent channel-level selection.

Output on Motion Bursts

When applied to bursts taken with identical exposure settings over time:

  • Removal of Bright Moving Objects: Any transient object that is brighter than the background (such as white vehicles, pedestrians in light clothing, or headlights) is replaced by the darker background pixels from other frames in the sequence.
  • Retention of Dark Moving Objects: Any object darker than the background (such as shadows, dark clothing, or silhouettes) persists along its entire movement path, resulting in a dark multi-exposure trail.

Example Syntax

To execute this operation on a set of burst images, the standard command syntax is:

magick burst_*.jpg -evaluate-sequence Min output_min.jpg

This operation does not perform HDR tone mapping or intelligent edge blending; it is a strict mathematical minimum filter applied through the image stack.
