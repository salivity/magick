ImageMagick Evaluate Sqr on Normalized Pixels

Applying the ImageMagick -evaluate Sqr operator to normalized pixel values squares each channel value in the image, resulting in an overall darkening of the midtones while leaving pure black and pure white unchanged. Because normalized pixels exist on a floating-point scale from 0.0 to 1.0, multiplying any fractional value by itself produces a smaller number, which alters the image contrast curve similarly to applying a power-law (gamma) adjustment.

Mathematical Behavior

In ImageMagick, normalized pixel values map the minimum intensity to 0.0 and the maximum quantum depth (pure white) to 1.0. When the Sqr (square) function is executed:

\[\text{Output} = \text{Input}^2\]

  • Black Pixels (\(0.0\)): \(0.0^2 = 0.0\) (remains unchanged).
  • White Pixels (\(1.0\)): \(1.0^2 = 1.0\) (remains unchanged).
  • Midtone Pixels (\(0.0 < x < 1.0\)): Since the square of any fraction between 0 and 1 is strictly less than the original fraction, all intermediate tones decrease in brightness. For example, a middle gray value of \(0.5\) becomes \(0.25\).

Visual and Practical Impact

  • Midtone Darkening: The most noticeable effect is that shadows and midtones become substantially darker, with mid-grays dropping by half their intensity.
  • Increased Highlight Contrast: The rate of change along the curve is steeper near 1.0, meaning bright areas retain distinct separations, while low-end shadow detail is compressed toward black.
  • Equivalent to Gamma 2.0: The mathematical operation is identical to applying an exponential power curve with an exponent of 2.0, effectively functioning as a non-linear contrast adjustment rather than a simple linear brightness reduction.
