ImageMagick Exclusion Blending Mode Math

The Exclusion blending operator in ImageMagick combines foreground and background colors to produce an effect similar to the Difference blend mode, but with reduced contrast and softer transitions. This article breaks down the exact mathematical formulas used to calculate color and alpha values during an Exclusion composite operation, explains the algebraic properties of the operator, and demonstrates its behavior across common color values.

The Core Mathematical Formula

When ImageMagick processes an Exclusion composite operation (invoked with -compose Exclusion -composite), pixel channel values are normalized to a floating-point range of \([0.0, 1.0]\), where \(0.0\) represents minimum intensity (black) and \(1.0\) represents maximum intensity (white).

For fully opaque pixels, the resulting color value \(R\) from the source pixel \(S\) (the overlay) and the destination pixel \(D\) (the background) is calculated as:

\[R = S + D - (2 \times S \times D)\]

Alternative Algebraic Forms

The formula can also be expressed in alternative equivalent forms that clarify its behavior:

  1. Sum of Inverted Products:
    \[R = S(1 - D) + D(1 - S)\]
    This form shows how the Exclusion operator functions as a continuous, analog equivalent of the Boolean XOR (exclusive OR) logic gate.

  2. Distance from Midpoint:
    \[R = 0.5 - 2 \times (S - 0.5) \times (D - 0.5)\]
    This highlights the symmetry of the operator around 50% gray.

Key Boundary Behaviors

The Exclusion formula produces distinct mathematical results when evaluating specific input values:

  • Blending with Black (\(S = 0.0\)):
    \[R = 0.0 + D - 2(0.0)(D) = D\]
    Blending any image with pure black leaves the destination image completely unchanged.

  • Blending with White (\(S = 1.0\)):
    \[R = 1.0 + D - 2(1.0)(D) = 1.0 - D\]
    Blending with pure white produces an exact negative (inversion) of the destination image.

  • Blending with 50% Gray (\(S = 0.5\)):
    \[R = 0.5 + D - 2(0.5)(D) = 0.5 + D - D = 0.5\]
    Blending with 50% gray neutralizes the destination entirely, resulting in uniform 50% gray regardless of the underlying color.

  • Self-Blending (\(S = D\)):
    \[R = 2D - 2D^2 = 2D(1 - D)\]
    Blending an image with itself produces an inverted quadratic curve peaking at \(0.5\) when \(D = 0.5\).

Alpha Channel Compositing

When pixels contain transparency, ImageMagick incorporates the source alpha (\(\alpha_S\)) and destination alpha (\(\alpha_D\)) according to the W3C compositing and blending specifications.

The resulting output alpha (\(\alpha_R\)) is calculated first:

\[\alpha_R = \alpha_S + \alpha_D \times (1 - \alpha_S)\]

The final color channel \(C_R\) is calculated using the weighted blend formula:

\[C_R = \frac{(1 - \alpha_D) \times \alpha_S \times C_S + (1 - \alpha_S) \times \alpha_D \times C_D + \alpha_S \times \alpha_D \times (C_S + C_D - 2 \times C_S \times C_D)}{\alpha_R}\]

Where \(C_S\) and \(C_D\) represent the individual normalized color channel values of the source and destination. After computation, the resulting values are mapped back to the target image bit depth (such as 0–255 for 8-bit or 0–65535 for 16-bit images).
