ImageMagick Features Operator for Texture Analysis

ImageMagick’s -features operator performs spatial texture analysis on digital images by computing Grey-Level Co-occurrence Matrices (GLCM) and deriving mathematical Haralick texture features. This article explains how the operator functions, breaks down the multi-directional statistical metrics it generates per color channel, and details how to read the resulting numerical output for image classification and texture characterization.

The Mechanism: Grey-Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM)

When you execute -features <distance> in ImageMagick, the tool analyzes the spatial relationship between pixel intensities at a specified pixel distance. It evaluates pixel pairs across four standard angular directions:

  • 0° (Horizontal)
  • 45° (Positive Diagonal)
  • 90° (Vertical)
  • 135° (Negative Diagonal)

The operator constructs a GLCM for each direction and calculates statistical properties based on how often specific gray-level intensity combinations occur relative to each other.

The 14 Haralick Texture Features

For each color channel (such as Red, Green, Blue, or Gray) and across each of the four directions, the -features operator outputs 14 standard Haralick texture descriptors, followed by the directional mean and range:

  1. Angular Second Moment (ASM) / Energy: Measures textural uniformity and orderliness. High values indicate a homogeneous or periodic image with fewer gray-level transitions.
  2. Contrast: Measures the local intensity variations. High values signify sharp local contrasts and distinct edges.
  3. Correlation: Quantifies linear dependency of gray levels between neighboring pixels. High values indicate predictable linear patterns.
  4. Sum of Squares: Variance: Reflects the dispersion of the gray-level distribution around the mean within the co-occurrence matrix.
  5. Inverse Difference Moment (IDM) / Homogeneity: Measures local homogeneity. It weights inverse pixel differences heavily, returning higher values for smooth, low-contrast regions.
  6. Sum Average: Calculates the average sum of intensity values of adjacent pixel pairs.
  7. Sum Variance: Measures the spread of the sum of intensity values relative to the Sum Average.
  8. Sum Entropy: Measures the degree of randomness associated with the sum distribution of gray levels.
  9. Entropy: Measures the overall disorder or randomness of the texture. Complex, noisy, or chaotic textures yield high entropy.
  10. Difference Variance: Measures the dispersion of differences in gray level between pixel pairs.
  11. Difference Entropy: Measures the degree of disorder specifically among the differences in adjacent pixel values.
  12. Information Measure of Correlation 1: A normalized statistical measure assessing the information dependency between pixel pairs.
  13. Information Measure of Correlation 2: A secondary complementary metric for correlation structure, bounded between 0 and 1.
  14. Maximum Correlation Coefficient: Reflects the square root of the second-largest eigenvalue of the normalized GLCM matrix, representing the strength of the most dominant correlation.

Interpreting the Output Format

The output is printed as structured text directly to standard output or an info stream. For each channel, the terminal displays:

  • Individual Angular Rows: Four lines containing the 14 computed metrics for 0°, 45°, 90°, and 135°.
  • Mean: The average of each metric across all four directions, offering a rotation-invariant representation of the texture.
  • Range: The difference between the maximum and minimum values across the four angles, which indicates directional bias (anisotropy) in the texture.

By comparing the mean and range across metrics like Contrast, Homogeneity, and Entropy, you can numerically differentiate fine textures, coarse surfaces, directional grains, and smooth gradients.
