ImageMagick Hu Invariant Moments for Shape Recognition

Hu invariant moments are a set of seven distinct mathematical features derived from an image that describe its shape independently of translation, scale, rotation, and reflection. When implemented in the ImageMagick software suite, these moments provide a robust numerical fingerprint for objects within a digital raster. By condensing complex geometric structures into a standardized array of values, ImageMagick enables automated computer vision pipelines to match, identify, and categorize shapes consistently across varying orientations and view sizes.

What are Hu Invariant Moments?

Formulated in 1962 by Ming-Kuei Hu, Hu invariant moments are calculated using normalized central geometric moments of an image. Central moments measure how pixel intensity is distributed relative to the shape's center of mass, making them naturally invariant to translation (position in the frame).

By further normalizing these moments relative to the total area (mass), they become scale-invariant. Hu derived seven specific non-linear combinations of these normalized central moments:

  • Moments 1 to 6: Invariant to scale, translation, and 2D rotation.
  • Moment 7: A skew invariant that distinguishes mirrored (reflected) images from one another.

Extracting Moments with ImageMagick

ImageMagick provides built-in tools to extract these metrics directly from the command line using the -moments or -verbose options with the identify utility.

When executed on an image or isolated binary silhouette, ImageMagick computes both the standard spatial moments (like center of gravity) and the seven Hu invariants for each color channel:

magick identify -moments input_shape.png

The output yields seven floating-point values (\(I_1\) through \(I_7\)) representing the fundamental geometric signature of the primary object.

How Hu Moments Aid Shape Recognition

  1. Pose-Independent Feature Vectors: In real-world computer vision, objects rarely appear at identical angles or distances. Because the first six moments remain identical whether a shape is tilted, moved, or resized, they eliminate the need to manually align or resize images before comparison.

  2. Dimensionality Reduction: Instead of comparing thousands or millions of individual pixels across two images, shape comparison is reduced to matching two 7-dimensional numerical vectors. This drastically reduces computational complexity and memory usage.

  3. Distance-Based Matching: To determine if an unknown shape matches a known reference template, algorithms compute the mathematical distance (such as Euclidean distance or Cosine similarity) between their log-transformed Hu moment vectors. A smaller distance indicates a higher likelihood of an exact shape match.

  4. Noise Resistance: Because moments are calculated as integrals across the entire surface area of the shape, minor pixel noise, boundary artifacts, or compression imperfections have minimal impact on the overall invariant values.

By combining ImageMagick's fast image processing engine with Hu invariant moments, developers can implement lightweight, highly effective 2D object recognition, defect detection, and OCR systems without relying on computationally heavy machine learning models.
