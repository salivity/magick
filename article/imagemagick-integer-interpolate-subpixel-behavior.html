ImageMagick Integer Interpolate Subpixel Behavior

This article provides an overview of how ImageMagick handles fractional subpixel coordinates when using the -interpolate Integer setting. It explains the underlying mathematical mechanism, contrasts it with nearest-neighbor interpolation, and details the visual artifacts and coordinate shifting that occur during geometric transformations.

The Truncation Mechanism

When ImageMagick processes transformations such as rotations, shearing, or arbitrary distortions, target pixel coordinates mapped back to the source image rarely align with exact whole pixels. Instead, they produce floating-point subpixel coordinates, such as (12.75, 4.30).

When the -interpolate Integer setting is active, ImageMagick resolves these fractional coordinates by strictly truncating them to their integer components. It drops the fractional part entirely (functioning essentially as a floor() operation on positive coordinates), mapping (12.75, 4.30) directly to the discrete pixel at (12, 4).

Unlike continuous or blending interpolation methods, no weighted averaging or color mixing takes place between surrounding pixels.

Difference Between Integer and Nearest Interpolation

While -interpolate Integer and -interpolate Nearest (or NearestNeighbor) are both non-blending, zero-order methods, they differ fundamentally in how they address fractional positions:

  • Nearest: Rounds coordinates to the closest pixel center (equivalent to adding 0.5 and truncating). A coordinate of 12.75 rounds up to 13.
  • Integer: Ignores proximity and drops the fraction regardless of magnitude. A coordinate of 12.75 truncates down to 12, just as 12.05 would.

Because pixel centers in ImageMagick's coordinate space are defined at half-pixel offsets (x + 0.5, y + 0.5), truncating to raw integer values produces a systematic half-pixel to full-pixel positional bias toward the image origin (0,0).

Visual and Rendering Impacts

Encountering fractional coordinates under Integer interpolation leads to specific rendering characteristics:

  • Positional Drift: Transformed elements appear shifted upward and to the left compared to where mathematical projection predicts them to fall.
  • Severe Aliasing: Edge transitions are hard and jagged. Without color smoothing, high-frequency boundaries show stair-stepping (jaggies).
  • Moiré Patterns: Downscaling or periodic pattern transformations exhibit severe interference artifacts due to unweighted subsampling.
  • Asymmetric Distortion: Rotating an image can produce uneven pixel doubling or thinning across different quadrants due to the one-directional bias of truncation.

Typical Applications

Because of the spatial shift and aliasing it introduces, -interpolate Integer is rarely used for general photo manipulation or graphic design. It is primarily utilized for:

  • Extreme processing speed when quality is irrelevant.
  • Exact, unblended data sampling in non-visual raster data (such as segmentation masks, index maps, or digital elevation matrices).
  • Debugging coordinate transform pipelines to verify raw lookup boundaries.
