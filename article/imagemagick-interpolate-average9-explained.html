ImageMagick Interpolate Average9 Explained

This article explores the -interpolate Average9 setting in ImageMagick, detailing how it functions during continuous image sampling. Readers will learn the mechanics behind this specific interpolation algorithm, how it calculates pixel values across non-integer coordinates, and the visual effects it produces during geometric transformations and distortions.

What is the -interpolate Setting?

In ImageMagick, the -interpolate setting determines how colors are calculated when sampling an image at non-integer (floating-point) coordinates. Operations such as -distort, -resize, and custom FX operations frequently require color values that fall between physical pixel grid lines. The chosen interpolation method defines the mathematical rule used to reconstruct a continuous signal from these discrete image pixels.

How Average9 Operates

The Average9 method is a localized neighborhood averaging filter. When ImageMagick samples a continuous coordinate under this setting, it identifies the nearest pixel and samples a symmetric 3x3 pixel area centered on that location.

  • Pixel Count: It samples exactly 9 pixels (the target pixel plus its 8 surrounding neighbors).
  • Weighting: Unlike bicubic, Gaussian, or bilinear interpolation—which weight pixels based on their exact distance from the sampling coordinate—Average9 applies equal weight to all 9 pixels.
  • Calculation: The resulting value is the arithmetic mean (average) of the color channels across the entire 3x3 block:

\[\text{Output Color} = \frac{1}{9} \sum_{i=1}^{9} \text{Pixel}_i\]

Behavior in Continuous Sampling

During continuous transformations (such as continuous image rotation, affine mapping, or polar distortions), Average9 produces specific behavioral outcomes:

  1. Box Blur Smoothing: Because it takes an unweighted average of a 3x3 neighborhood, it acts essentially as an integrated box blur applied directly during the sampling phase. This suppresses high-frequency noise and harsh gradients.
  2. Stepped Transitions: Because the 3x3 footprint snaps to the nearest integer grid before averaging, it does not provide smooth sub-pixel transitions. As the sampling point traverses across pixel boundaries, the entire 3x3 window shifts abruptly by one discrete pixel, which can introduce subtle blocky or stepped artifacts rather than smooth continuous gradations.
  3. Contrast Reduction: Fine details smaller than 3x3 pixels are smoothed out, softening edge definitions more aggressively than standard bilinear or bicubic interpolation.

Use Cases

Average9 is primarily selected in workflows where:

  • Intentional Softening: A low-cost, uniform softening or defocalization effect is needed during a distortion operation without running a separate -blur pass.
  • Noise Mitigation: Sampling noisy or dithered textures where localized smoothing improves mapping fidelity.
  • Algorithmic Consistency: Replicating non-weighted neighborhood averaging models found in computer vision or procedural texture pipelines.

Command Syntax Example

To use Average9 in a command line operation:

magick input.png -interpolate Average9 -distort SRT 45 output.png

In this command, ImageMagick applies a 45-degree rotation using continuous sampling governed by the 3x3 averaging filter.
