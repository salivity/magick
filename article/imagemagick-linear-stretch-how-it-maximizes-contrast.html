ImageMagick Linear-Stretch: How It Maximizes Contrast

The ImageMagick -linear-stretch operator increases the dynamic range of an image by discarding a predetermined amount of shadow and highlight data and scaling the remaining tonal values across the entire brightness spectrum. By clipping extreme outliers and mapping the new boundaries to absolute black and pure white, the operator removes dull, low-contrast hazes while preserving proportional brightness relationships throughout the midtones.

The Purpose of Clipping

Standard contrast stretching typically maps the single darkest pixel in an image to pure black and the single brightest pixel to pure white. However, if an image contains even one stray black or white artifact (such as sensor noise or hot pixels), traditional stretching fails to improve overall contrast.

The -linear-stretch operator solves this by using clipping parameters, typically defined as -linear-stretch black-point[xwhite-point], either in absolute pixel counts or percentages (for example, 1%x1%). Rather than evaluating extreme values, it cuts off a small fraction of the darkest and lightest pixels to establish more representative dynamic range limits.

Step-by-Step Operation

  1. Histogram Generation: ImageMagick scans the image to generate a cumulative tonal histogram, counting how many pixels exist at each intensity level from minimum (0) to maximum (QuantumRange).

  2. Identifying the Black Cutoff: The operator traverses the histogram from the darkest tones upward until the cumulative number of pixels equals the specified black-point. The intensity value reached at this threshold becomes the new black point.

  3. Identifying the White Cutoff: Simultaneously, the operator traverses the histogram from the brightest tones downward until the cumulative number of pixels reaches the specified white-point. The intensity value reached at this threshold becomes the new white point.

  4. Clipping: All pixels with an intensity lower than or equal to the black cutoff are clipped and forced to pure black (value 0). All pixels with an intensity higher than or equal to the white cutoff are clipped and forced to pure white (QuantumRange).

  5. Linear Scaling: The remaining pixels that fall between the two cutoffs are linearly remapped to fill the full span between pure black and pure white using a linear interpolation formula:

\[\text{New Value} = \frac{\text{Current Value} - \text{Black Cutoff}}{\text{White Cutoff} - \text{Black Cutoff}} \times \text{QuantumRange}\]

Results on Dynamic Range

Because the intermediate values are scaled linearly rather than curved, the tonal proportions and natural relationships between midtones are preserved without introducing non-linear gamma distortions. The resulting image exhibits heightened overall contrast, deeper blacks, crisp highlights, and improved visual clarity, effectively eliminating underexposed or overexposed color casts.
