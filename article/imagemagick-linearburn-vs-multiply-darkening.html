ImageMagick LinearBurn vs Multiply Darkening

Both LinearBurn and Multiply are darkening compositing operators in ImageMagick, but they manipulate color channels using fundamentally different mathematical formulas. While Multiply reduces brightness proportionally by multiplying pixel values, LinearBurn darkens an image through linear subtraction. This difference causes LinearBurn to produce far deeper shadows, higher contrast, and aggressive clipping to pure black compared to the smoother, more gradual darkening of Multiply.

The Mathematical Difference

To understand how each operator darkens an image, assume pixel color values are normalized on a scale from \(0.0\) (pure black) to \(1.0\) (pure white):

  • Multiply: \[\text{Result} = A \times B\]
  • LinearBurn: \[\text{Result} = A + B - 1 \quad (\text{clamped at } 0.0)\]

In both methods, pure white (\(1.0\)) acts as a neutral identity color that produces no change:

  • \(A \times 1.0 = A\)
  • \(A + 1.0 - 1.0 = A\)

However, their treatment of any value lower than \(1.0\) diverges sharply.

Shadow Clipping and Pure Black

The most noticeable difference between the two operators is how quickly they push pixels to pure black:

  • Multiply preserves shadow details: Because it relies on multiplication, the result can only reach pure black (\(0.0\)) if at least one of the input pixels is already pure black. Combining two dark pixels (for example, \(0.2 \times 0.2\)) yields \(0.04\). Although dark, tonal variation and image data are still retained.
  • LinearBurn crushes shadows: Because it subtracts the inverted blend value from the base image (\(A - (1 - B)\)), any pixel pair whose sum is less than or equal to \(1.0\) is clipped directly to \(0.0\). Combining the same two \(0.2\) pixels results in \(0.2 + 0.2 - 1.0 = -0.6\), which clamps to \(0.0\). As a consequence, large portions of midtones and shadows collapse entirely into pure black.

Midtone Compression and Contrast

Multiply darkens the image with a smooth, curved falloff. Midtones blend proportionally without drastically skewing the overall dynamic range of the image. The result resembles looking through two overlapping photographic slides, which preserves the relative tonal relationships across the canvas.

LinearBurn reduces brightness linearly, which increases perceived contrast and saturation before pixels clip to black. It removes brightness uniformly rather than scaling it down, making midtone areas darken much faster relative to standard Multiply.

Summary of Behavioral Differences

  • Rate of Darkening: LinearBurn darkens significantly faster and harsher across all non-white values than Multiply.
  • Shadow Detail: Multiply retains subtle details within dark regions, whereas LinearBurn frequently clips shadows to pure black.
  • Contrast: LinearBurn significantly boosts image contrast and saturation, while Multiply produces a balanced, natural-looking shadow blend.
