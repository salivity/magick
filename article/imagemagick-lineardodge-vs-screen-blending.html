ImageMagick LinearDodge vs Screen Blending

Both the LinearDodge and Screen composition methods in ImageMagick are designed to increase the brightness of an image by combining the color values of a source and destination layer. However, they achieve this through fundamentally different mathematical formulas: LinearDodge utilizes direct linear addition, producing aggressive highlights and rapid clipping to pure white, whereas Screen applies an inverted multiplication algorithm that yields a softer, more gradual brightening curve that preserves highlight details.

The Mathematical Formulas

The primary difference between the two blending modes lies in the underlying math applied to normalized color values (where black is 0.0 and white is 1.0):

  • LinearDodge (Add): The formula is simply Result = A + B. The pixel values of both images are summed directly. If the sum exceeds 1.0 (or 255 in standard 8-bit channels), the value is clipped to white.
  • Screen: The formula is Result = 1 - (1 - A) * (1 - B), which simplifies algebraically to Result = A + B - (A * B). By subtracting the product of the two layers, the operation prevents the sum from easily reaching absolute white unless one of the contributing pixels is already pure white.

Visual Differences and Highlight Retention

Because LinearDodge directly sums values, it significantly boosts exposure and contrast. It pushes midtones toward pure white very quickly, often leading to blown-out, clipped highlights with high color saturation. This makes it ideal for producing glowing special effects, light flares, laser beams, or intense optical luminescence where severe clipping is desirable.

In contrast, Screen behaves like projecting two photographic slides simultaneously onto the same white screen. It brightens darker tones while compressing highlights, ensuring that tonal gradation and fine texture in bright areas are preserved rather than clipped. Colors rendered with Screen appear lighter and slightly desaturated compared to the punchy, high-contrast output of LinearDodge.

ImageMagick Usage

In ImageMagick, you define these blending modes using the -compose operator alongside -composite.

For standard additive brightening with high contrast:

magick base.png overlay.png -compose LinearDodge -composite output_dodge.png

For softer, highlight-safe brightening:

magick base.png overlay.png -compose Screen -composite output_screen.png

When deciding between the two methods, choose Screen for natural photographic adjustments, blending exposures, or rendering translucent elements like smoke and fog. Choose LinearDodge when you need high-energy lighting effects, intense specular highlights, or strict mathematical summation of pixel values.
