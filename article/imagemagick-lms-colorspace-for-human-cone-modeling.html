ImageMagick LMS Colorspace for Human Cone Modeling

In digital image processing, transforming standard RGB data into physiological representations is necessary to evaluate how human eyes actually perceive light and color. The ImageMagick -colorspace LMS setting maps pixel data into the LMS color space, directly corresponding to the spectral sensitivities of the human eye's three cone photoreceptors: Long (L), Medium (M), and Short (S) wavelengths. This article explains how this setting models biological cone responses, why it differs from standard display color spaces, and how it is applied in vision science, chromatic adaptation, and color vision deficiency simulation.

The Biological Basis of the LMS Color Space

Human photopic (daylight) vision relies on three distinct classes of cone photoreceptors located in the retina:

  • L cones: Sensitive primarily to long-wavelength light, peaking around 560 nm (reddish spectrum).
  • M cones: Sensitive to medium-wavelength light, peaking around 530 nm (greenish spectrum).
  • S cones: Sensitive to short-wavelength light, peaking around 420 nm (bluish spectrum).

Standard digital color spaces such as sRGB, Adobe RGB, or standard RGB define coordinates based on arbitrary display phosphor or LED emission spectra, not human retinal absorption curves. When the -colorspace LMS operator is applied in ImageMagick, the engine converts device-dependent trichromatic data (typically via an intermediate CIE XYZ profile) into three-channel outputs representing the calculated excitation levels of these three cone types.

The Transformation Process

When executing magick input.png -colorspace LMS output.png, ImageMagick performs a linear matrix transformation on the image data:

  1. Linearization: Non-linear gamma-corrected RGB values are linearized to ensure physical light intensity is conserved.
  2. XYZ Conversion: The linear RGB values are projected into CIE XYZ tristimulus space, standardizing the colorimetry relative to a reference white point (usually D65).
  3. LMS Projection: A transformation matrix (such as the CAT02 or Hunt-Pointer-Estevez matrix) multiplies the XYZ coordinates to obtain the biological L, M, and S excitation states.

In the resulting file, the red, green, and blue channels no longer designate monitor output; instead, Channel 0 represents L-cone excitation, Channel 1 represents M-cone excitation, and Channel 2 represents S-cone excitation.

Key Functions in Cone Response Modeling

Simulating Color Vision Deficiency (CVD)

The primary functional utility of ImageMagick’s LMS conversion is simulating human color blindness, including protanopia (absence of L cones), deuteranopia (absence of M cones), and tritanopia (absence of S cones). Because standard RGB channels contain overlapping stimuli for both L and M cones, accurate deficiency modeling cannot be achieved through simple channel subtraction in RGB. In LMS space, an algorithm can mathematically flatten or project one cone axis onto the remaining operational axes before converting the image back to sRGB.

Chromatic Adaptation and White Balancing

The human visual system automatically adjusts perceived color based on the illumination source, a mechanism described by the von Kries adaptation model. The von Kries transform operates under the assumption that chromatic adaptation occurs independently within each cone class by scaling the L, M, and S responses. By isolating these channels using -colorspace LMS, researchers can scale individual cone channels to simulate how visual stimuli appear under varying illuminants (e.g., daylight versus incandescent lighting).

Visual Perception and Psychophysics

For computational models that evaluate perceived image difference, masking, or contrast sensitivity, processing images in LMS provides biologically grounded input data. Evaluating contrast across separate L-M (red-green opponent) and S-(L+M) (blue-yellow opponent) neural pathways requires LMS cone catch data as the initial computational step.
