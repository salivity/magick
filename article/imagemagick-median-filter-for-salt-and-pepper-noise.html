ImageMagick Median Filter for Salt-and-Pepper Noise

This article provides a quick guide on using ImageMagick to eliminate salt-and-pepper noise using the median filter. You will learn the exact command-line syntax for ImageMagick versions 6 and 7, how to configure the filter's radius and geometry parameters, and why median filtering is the most effective method for cleaning impulse noise while preserving sharp edges.

Basic Syntax

In ImageMagick version 7, use the magick command with the -median or -statistic median operator:

magick input.png -median <radius> output.png

Alternatively, you can define the neighborhood window explicitly using the -statistic option:

magick input.png -statistic median <width>x<height> output.png

For ImageMagick version 6, replace magick with convert:

convert input.png -median <radius> output.png

How the Parameters Work

  • Radius (<radius>): Specifies the radius of the pixel neighborhood used to calculate the median. A standard radius of 1 or 1.5 creates a small search window (typically 3x3 pixels), which is usually sufficient for single-pixel noise.
  • Geometry (<width>x<height>): When using -statistic median, this defines the exact pixel dimensions of the filtering matrix, such as 3x3 or 5x5.

Practical Examples

Standard Noise Reduction (3x3 Matrix)

To eliminate light to moderate salt-and-pepper noise with minimal blurring of image details:

magick input.jpg -median 1 output.jpg

Or using explicit dimensions:

magick input.jpg -statistic median 3x3 output.jpg

Heavy Noise Reduction (5x5 Matrix)

For dense or clustered impulse noise, increase the neighborhood size:

magick input.jpg -statistic median 5x5 output.jpg

Why Median Filtering Suppresses Salt-and-Pepper Noise

Salt-and-pepper noise introduces extreme high and low intensity values (isolated black and white pixels). Linear filters like Gaussian or mean blur average these outliers into surrounding pixels, causing halos and blurring.

The median filter sorts all pixel values in the surrounding window and selects the middle value. Because the noise pixels are extreme statistical outliers, they are discarded during the median selection, effectively removing the noise while keeping legitimate edge boundaries intact.
