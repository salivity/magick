ImageMagick Motion Blur: Angle and Sigma Guide

ImageMagick's -motion-blur operator simulates the visual effect of linear camera movement or fast-moving subjects during an exposure. By applying a one-dimensional Gaussian blur across a specific path, the operator transforms static images to give the illusion of physical velocity. The primary controls for this simulation are the angle and sigma parameters, which dictate the trajectory and the perceived speed of the motion, respectively.

The Role of the Angle Parameter

The angle parameter determines the direction of the simulated motion along a 360-degree axis.

  • 0 Degrees: Produces a strictly horizontal motion blur moving from left to right.
  • 90 Degrees: Produces a vertical motion blur moving from bottom to top.
  • 180 and 270 Degrees: Reverse the directional polarity of the horizontal and vertical trajectories.
  • Intermediate Values (e.g., 45°): Create diagonal motion blurs.

By setting the angle, you establish the exact vector of movement, simulating either a panning camera or an object traveling across the camera sensor along that specific plane.

The Role of the Sigma Parameter

The sigma parameter defines the standard deviation of the Gaussian distribution used to calculate the blur, directly simulating the magnitude or speed of the motion.

  • Blur Length: A larger sigma spreads pixel data further across the path defined by the angle, representing a faster-moving object or a longer exposure time (slower shutter speed).
  • Blur Softness: A smaller sigma produces a tighter, more subtle trail, simulating slight camera jitter or slow movement.

How Angle and Sigma Work Together

The full syntax for the operator is -motion-blur radiusxsigma+angle. While radius defines the physical pixel radius of the convolution kernel matrix, sigma is the mathematically vital parameter that dictates the visual stretch of the motion, and angle rotates that stretch across the 2D image plane. Together, they accurately simulate linear directional movement by spreading light values outward along a precisely oriented vector.
