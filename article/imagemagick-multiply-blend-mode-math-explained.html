ImageMagick Multiply Blend Mode Math Explained

The Multiply blend mode in ImageMagick is a basic compositing operation that darkens an image by multiplying the color values of the source and destination pixels. This article explains the underlying mathematical formula of the Multiply blend mode, how ImageMagick handles color channels and dynamic range, and the visual effects produced by multiplying different color values.

The Mathematical Formula

At its core, the Multiply blend mode performs standard arithmetic multiplication on corresponding pixel color channels. However, because pixel values represent light intensity, they are calculated on a normalized scale from \(0.0\) (pure black) to \(1.0\) (pure white/maximum intensity).

The normalized formula for each color channel (Red, Green, Blue) is:

\[\text{Result} = \text{Source} \times \text{Destination}\]

In ImageMagick, internal computations depend on the compiled quantum depth (such as Q8 for 8-bit or Q16 for 16-bit). For integer representations where values range from \(0\) to \(\text{QuantumRange}\) (e.g., \(0\) to \(255\) in 8-bit, or \(0\) to \(65535\) in 16-bit), the formula scales down by the quantum range to keep the output within the correct bounds:

\[\text{Result} = \frac{\text{Source} \times \text{Destination}}{\text{QuantumRange}}\]

Key Mathematical Properties and Behavior

  1. Multiplying by White (1.0 or QuantumRange): When any color is multiplied by pure white (\(1.0\)), the output is identical to the original color (\(X \times 1.0 = X\)). White acts as the multiplicative identity and has no visual effect on the base image.

  2. Multiplying by Black (0.0): When any color is multiplied by pure black (\(0.0\)), the result is always pure black (\(X \times 0.0 = 0.0\)).

  3. Darkening Intermediate Values: Because all normalized color components lie between \(0.0\) and \(1.0\), multiplying any two values less than \(1.0\) always yields a smaller number. As a result, the Multiply blend mode always produces a result that is darker than or equal to the original inputs.

  4. Commutativity: Multiplication is commutative (\(A \times B = B \times A\)). In ImageMagick, swapping the source and destination layers produces the exact same color result, assuming both layers are fully opaque.

Alpha Channel Consideration

When images include transparency, ImageMagick factors the alpha channel into the composite calculation. The color channels are blended proportionally to the overlapping alpha coverage using standard Porter-Duff compositing rules combined with the multiplicative color formula, ensuring smooth transitions along transparent and semi-transparent edges.
