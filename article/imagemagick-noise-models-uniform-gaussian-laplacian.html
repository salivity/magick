ImageMagick Noise Models: Uniform, Gaussian, Laplacian

ImageMagick provides several procedural noise generators used for texturing, dithering, and simulating physical sensor imperfections. This guide compares the three fundamental additive noise models available via the +noise operator: Uniform, Gaussian, and Laplacian. You will learn the mathematical distribution, visual characteristics, and practical use cases for each model to help you choose the right noise type for your image processing workflows.

1. Uniform Noise (+noise Uniform)

Mathematical Distribution

Uniform noise uses a flat probability density function. Every value within the specified minimum and maximum threshold has an identical probability of being generated.

Visual Characteristics

  • Produces a distinct, harsh, and flat grain.
  • Lacks clustering around a central mean; bright and dark noise pixels appear with equal frequency across the full amplitude range.
  • Resembles raw digital quantization errors or uncalibrated analogue TV static.

Typical Use Cases

  • Simulating digital sensor quantization errors or bit-depth reduction artifacts.
  • Uniform random dithering for low-bit-depth rendering.
  • Creating baseline randomized textures where no central bias is desired.

Command Example:

magick input.png +noise Uniform output_uniform.png

2. Gaussian Noise (+noise Gaussian)

Mathematical Distribution

Gaussian noise follows a standard normal distribution (a bell curve). Most pixel value alterations cluster tightly around a mean of zero, while extreme deviations occur with exponentially lower probability.

Visual Characteristics

  • Produces a natural, soft-edged grain that blends smoothly into surrounding image data.
  • Avoids abrupt high-contrast spikes, resulting in a more photorealistic appearance than uniform distribution.
  • Accurately mimics standard electronic noise found in optical hardware.

Typical Use Cases

  • Simulating realistic camera ISO sensor noise in low-light photography.
  • Softening harsh digital gradients to prevent color banding.
  • Natural-looking grain addition for film emulation workflows.

Command Example:

magick input.png +noise Gaussian output_gaussian.png

3. Laplacian Noise (+noise Laplacian)

Mathematical Distribution

Laplacian noise follows a double-exponential distribution. It is characterized by a much sharper central peak at zero compared to Gaussian noise, along with "heavier tails" that allow occasional large deviations.

Visual Characteristics

  • Keeps the majority of the image relatively clean while introducing sharp, high-contrast, isolated noise spikes.
  • Visually resembles an intermediate state between smooth Gaussian grain and impulse ("salt-and-pepper") noise.
  • Emphasizes high-frequency transitions, creating a spiky or gritty appearance.

Typical Use Cases

  • Simulating heavy-tailed transmission interference or edge-related sensor noise.
  • Generating rough surface textures such as stone, concrete, or distressed paper.
  • Stress-testing edge-detection algorithms and noise-reduction filters.

Command Example:

magick input.png +noise Laplacian output_laplacian.png

Summary of Differences

Feature Uniform Gaussian Laplacian
Distribution Flat / Equal probability Normal (Bell curve) Double exponential
Visual Texture Flat, harsh, static-like Soft, blended, realistic Gritty with sharp spikes
Extreme Outliers Uniform across range Very rare Frequent (Heavy tails)
Best For Digital quantization & dithering Camera ISO & film emulation Edge noise & rough textures
