ImageMagick Nonpeak Filter for Text Separation

This article explains how the -statistic Nonpeak filter in ImageMagick separates background paper grain from printed text in document scans. It covers the mechanics of local statistical filtering, why paper texture poses a challenge for binarization, and how bypassing dominant local pixel frequencies suppresses grain while preserving high-contrast text edges.

The Problem of Paper Grain in Document Scans

Scanned physical documents rarely have a uniform white background. Paper fibers create subtle shifts in brightness and shadows, known as paper grain or texture. When performing optical character recognition (OCR) or document binarization, standard thresholding often misidentifies these micro-contrasts as ink, leaving behind a speckle pattern or "salt-and-pepper" noise around and between text characters.

Standard blurring filters (like Gaussian blur) can smooth this grain, but they also soften the crisp edges of printed characters, causing thin strokes to break or adjacent letters to merge.

How the Nonpeak Statistic Operates

The -statistic operator in ImageMagick computes pixel values based on the distribution of colors within a moving window or neighborhood (defined by a geometry such as 3x3 or 5x5).

Unlike Mean (which calculates an average) or Median (which takes the middle value), Nonpeak evaluates the histogram of the local neighborhood and avoids the "peak"—the most frequently occurring pixel value (the local mode). Specifically:

  1. The filter builds a frequency histogram of all pixel intensities within the defined kernel.
  2. It identifies the dominant intensity value that represents the local peak.
  3. If the target pixel is part of this dominant peak, the filter selects an alternative value from the distribution that differs from that mode.

Suppressing Grain While Retaining Ink

In a typical scan, paper grain consists of tiny, repetitive fluctuations clustered tightly around the dominant background brightness. Because these values form the local statistical peak in areas with no text, the Nonpeak filter systematically rejects this dominant background value.

This behavior produces two distinct effects depending on the region of the image:

  • In Background Regions: The repetitive, high-frequency texture of the paper is stripped of its primary mode. When combined with subsequent normalization or thresholding, the flattened variation collapses into a uniform background field.
  • At Character Boundaries: Ink strokes consist of values that are sharply distinct from the background. Because text pixels do not share the dominant background frequency, their edge contrasts remain pronounced rather than blurred. The filter highlights the non-background components, maintaining the structural sharpness of the letterforms.

Basic Implementation in ImageMagick

To apply the Nonpeak filter for paper grain reduction, pass the filter type and kernel size to the -statistic option:

magick scan.png -colorspace Gray -statistic Nonpeak 3x3 cleaned.png

A 3x3 kernel is generally sufficient for fine, high-resolution paper grain. For coarser textures or lower-resolution scans, expanding the kernel to 5x5 may be necessary. Once the grain is suppressed by the Nonpeak filter, applying a standard thresholding operation (such as -threshold or -lat) yields a clean, high-contrast black-and-white output with clean margins and intact text.
