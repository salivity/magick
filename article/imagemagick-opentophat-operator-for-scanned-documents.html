ImageMagick OpenTopHat Operator for Scanned Documents

In ImageMagick, the OpenTopHat morphology operator isolates features that are brighter than their immediate surroundings and smaller than the structuring element (kernel) used in the operation. This article explains how the operator functions mathematically, what specific details it extracts from scanned documents under standard and inverted color configurations, and how it is utilized to eliminate uneven lighting and isolate target document features.

How OpenTopHat Works

The OpenTopHat operator, traditionally known in mathematical morphology as the "White Top-Hat" transform, calculates the difference between the input image and its morphological opening:

\[\text{OpenTopHat}(I) = I - (I \circ K)\]

Where \(I\) is the image and \(K\) is the structuring element. The opening operation (\(I \circ K\)) performs an erosion followed by a dilation, which erases all bright details smaller than the kernel while preserving broader background brightness levels. By subtracting this opened image from the original, only the small, bright elements that were removed by the opening remain.

What OpenTopHat Isolates in Standard Scans

Standard document scans feature dark text against a light or white background. Because OpenTopHat exclusively detects features that are lighter than their immediate neighborhood:

  • Bright Scanning Artifacts: It isolates light specks, white dust, pinholes, or tiny tears in the paper that appear brighter than the surrounding page.
  • Intra-Character Voids: It can capture small white spaces inside dark loops, such as the counters in letters like "e", "o", or "a", if the kernel size is configured properly.
  • Dark Text is Ignored: Standard dark ink is suppressed because it is darker than the surrounding paper. To isolate dark text on light paper, the complementary CloseTopHat (Black Top-Hat) operator is typically used instead.

What OpenTopHat Isolates in Inverted Scans

In automated document analysis, scans are frequently inverted prior to morphological processing so that the text characters become the bright foreground against a dark background. When applied to an inverted scan:

  • Text and Fine Line Work: The operator isolates character strokes, punctuation marks, and thin line drawings, provided the kernel size is slightly larger than the maximum stroke width of the fonts.
  • Non-Uniform Background Suppression: It removes large-scale gradients, scan shadows, bleed-through from reverse pages, and uneven document illumination. The background is flattened to near-black, leaving only the bright text strokes.

Practical Application in ImageMagick

To isolate thin bright features or text on an inverted scan, the operator is applied with a defined kernel geometry:

magick scan_inverted.png -morphology OpenTopHat Disk:3 isolated_text.png

In this command, any bright feature smaller than a 3-pixel radius disk is preserved, while broader background variations and large bright regions are stripped away. Selecting a kernel size just larger than the thickest stroke ensures clean isolation of legible document content without retaining background page glare.
