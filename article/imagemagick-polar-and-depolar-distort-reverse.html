ImageMagick Polar and DePolar Distort Reverse

This article explains the mathematical relationship between the Polar and DePolar operators in ImageMagick's -distort function. While -distort Polar maps rectangular Cartesian images into circular polar coordinates, -distort DePolar performs the inverse transformation by unwrapping polar circular spaces back into Cartesian planes. Understanding the underlying trigonometric formulas and parameter pairings allows for seamless, mathematically reversible image transformations.

The Inverse Relationship

In ImageMagick, -distort DePolar and -distort Polar are exact inverse functions of each other.

  • -distort Polar wraps a standard rectangular image (where the horizontal axis represents angle and the vertical axis represents radius) into an annulus, ring, or full disk.
  • -distort DePolar unwraps an annular or circular image back into a standard rectangular grid.

Applying -distort DePolar immediately following a -distort Polar operation (using the same radius, angle, and center coordinates) restores the original Cartesian geometry, subject only to pixel interpolation and antialiasing sampling limits.

Mathematical Coordinate Mapping

The transition between Cartesian coordinates \((x, y)\) and Polar coordinates \((r, \theta)\) forms the basis of both operators.

Cartesian to Polar (The Polar Operator)

When converting a Cartesian source pixel \((x, y)\) relative to a designated center \((x_0, y_0)\):

  1. Radius (\(r\)): \[r = \sqrt{(x - x_0)^2 + (y - y_0)^2}\]

  2. Angle (\(\theta\)): \[\theta = \text{atan2}(y - y_0, x - x_0)\]

In the output generated by -distort Polar, the horizontal coordinate of the input corresponds to the angle \(\theta\), and the vertical coordinate corresponds to the radius \(r\).

Polar to Cartesian (The Reverse / DePolar Operator)

To reverse the polar transformation back into a rectangular space, -distort DePolar applies standard trigonometric reconstruction:

  1. X-coordinate: \[x = x_0 + r \cdot \cos(\theta)\]

  2. Y-coordinate: \[y = y_0 + r \cdot \sin(\theta)\]

In the unwrapped rectangular result:

  • The X-axis maps the sweep angle \(\theta \in [\theta_{\text{start}}, \theta_{\text{end}}]\).
  • The Y-axis maps the radial distance \(r \in [r_{\text{inner}}, r_{\text{outer}}]\).

ImageMagick Parameters and Alignment

Both operations accept an identical set of optional parameters:

-distort Polar "radius, angle_start, angle_end, center_x, center_y"
-distort DePolar "radius, angle_start, angle_end, center_x, center_y"
  • radius: Specifies the maximum outer circle radius (\(r_{\text{outer}}\)). If set to 0, ImageMagick automatically calculates the distance from the center to the furthest image corner.
  • angle_start / angle_end: Defines the angular sweep. By default, ImageMagick spans from \(-180^\circ\) to \(+180^\circ\), starting from the bottom-center (south) moving clockwise.
  • center_x / center_y: The origin point \((x_0, y_0)\) around which the radial projection is centered. Defaults to the image center.

To achieve exact mathematical reversal, ensure the parameter string passed to -distort DePolar matches the parameters applied during -distort Polar.
