ImageMagick Polar Distort: Unwrapping Circular Patterns

This article explains how ImageMagick transforms concentric, circular visual patterns into flat, horizontal strips using polar coordinate distortion. By projecting polar coordinates—specifically radial distance and angular sweep—onto a standard Cartesian grid, ImageMagick reconstructs annular and concentric rings into linear, horizontal rows. Below, we break down the coordinate mapping mechanics, the role of inverse transformation, and the specific parameters controlling the unwrapping process.

Polar to Cartesian Coordinate Mapping

ImageMagick processes geometric transformations through mathematical coordinate conversion. Concentric patterns naturally exist in a polar coordinate system defined by an origin center \((x_0, y_0)\), a radius (\(r\)), and an angle (\(\theta\)).

When unwrapping these concentric patterns into a rectangular image, the software performs a transformation where:

  • Angle (\(\theta\)) maps to the X-axis (Width): The circumference around the center point is unrolled horizontally. A full \(360^\circ\) circle sweeps across the width of the destination image, typically starting from the top or right depending on the offset angle parameter.
  • Radius (\(r\)) maps to the Y-axis (Height): Concentric rings situated at different distances from the center are mapped vertically. The inner radius corresponds to one vertical edge (usually the top or bottom), while the outer radius corresponds to the opposite edge.

The Role of -distort DePolar and -distort Polar

In ImageMagick, circular distortion utilizes two complementary operations:

  1. Polar: Maps a standard rectangular image into an annular or circular shape (wrapping).
  2. DePolar: Maps a circular or concentric pattern into a standard rectangular strip (unwrapping).

While users frequently refer to the general technique as "polar distortion," the unwrapping action is specifically executed using -distort DePolar (or an inverted matrix with -distort Polar). DePolar calculates the distance from the defined center to the outer perimeter, translating each concentric circle at radius \(r\) into a perfectly straight horizontal line at coordinate \(y\).

Inverse Pixel Mapping and Resampling

ImageMagick does not push pixels from the circular source into the rectangular destination; instead, it uses inverse coordinate mapping. For every pixel coordinate \((x', y')\) in the destination rectangular image:

  1. The algorithm computes the corresponding polar coordinate: \[\theta = \text{angle\_offset} + \left(\frac{x'}{\text{width}}\right) \times 360^\circ\] \[r = r_{\text{min}} + \left(\frac{y'}{\text{height}}\right) \times (r_{\text{max}} - r_{\text{min}})\]
  2. It converts these polar coordinates back to Cartesian source coordinates \((x, y)\): \[x = x_0 + r \cos(\theta)\] \[y = y_0 + r \sin(\theta)\]
  3. The software samples the pixel color from the source image at \((x, y)\).

To prevent aliasing, moiré effects, and distortion artifacts caused by stretching pixels near the center, ImageMagick applies Elliptical Weighted Average (EWA) resampling during this lookup, ensuring clean, continuous horizontal bands.

Configurable Operator Arguments

The unwrapping process can be customized by passing specific arguments to the operator:

-distort DePolar "Radius_Max, Radius_Min, Center_X, Center_Y, Angle_Start, Angle_End"
  • Radius limits (Radius_Max, Radius_Min): Define the inner and outer boundaries of the concentric bands to unwrap. Setting an inner radius greater than zero skips empty or irrelevant centers.
  • Center coordinates (Center_X, Center_Y): Defines the exact focal point of the concentric rings. Accurate centering is crucial; an offset center will produce wavy sinusoidal lines instead of straight horizontal strips.
  • Angle limits (Angle_Start, Angle_End): Defines the rotational slice to unroll, allowing partial arcs to be converted into rectangles rather than forcing a full \(360^\circ\) sweep.
