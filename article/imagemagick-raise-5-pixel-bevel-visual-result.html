ImageMagick -raise 5-Pixel Bevel Visual Result

Applying the ImageMagick -raise operator with a five-pixel bevel transforms a flat image into a simulated three-dimensional button by altering the brightness along its perimeter. This command creates the illusion of depth by lightening the top and left outer edges while darkening the bottom and right outer edges, producing an elevated, tactile appearance without altering the dimensions of the original file.

Visual Appearance and Lighting Mechanics

The primary visual result is an extruded or raised look commonly found in traditional graphical user interface buttons. The effect relies on standard directional light simulation, where the virtual light source originates from the upper-left corner of the viewing area.

When configured with a five-pixel setting (such as -raise 5 or -raise 5x5), ImageMagick applies the following pixel adjustments:

  • Top and Left Edges: A five-pixel-wide strip along the top and left borders is lightened. This highlight simulates light hitting the raised bevel.
  • Bottom and Right Edges: A five-pixel-wide strip along the bottom and right borders is darkened. This shadow simulates the shaded side of the elevated area.
  • Corners: The mitered corners blend the bright and dark strips at a 45-degree angle, giving the border clean, picture-frame-like corners.

Dimension and Canvas Behavior

The -raise operator does not expand the canvas or increase the image's overall resolution. Instead, the five-pixel bevel is drawn directly inside the image boundaries, overwriting the outer five pixels of the existing content on all four sides.

Aesthetic Characteristics of a 5-Pixel Bevel

A width of five pixels provides a prominent, easily noticeable border on low- to medium-resolution images. On a small graphic or icon (such as 32x32 or 64x64 pixels), a five-pixel bevel appears thick and heavy, occupying a significant portion of the image. On a standard web graphic or larger photo, it provides a clean, well-defined frame that distinctly separates the image from its surrounding background.

Unlike drop shadows, which sit outside the image boundaries, the -raise bevel remains entirely internal, giving the visual impression that the graphic is physically popping out toward the viewer. Replacing -raise with +raise inverts these tonal adjustments, darkening the top and left while lightening the bottom and right to make the image appear pressed or sunken into the surface.
