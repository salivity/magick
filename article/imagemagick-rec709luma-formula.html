ImageMagick Rec709Luma Formula

When processing images to determine perceived brightness, ImageMagick relies on standard photometric models that reflect human visual sensitivity to different wavelengths of light. Using the Rec709Luma setting, ImageMagick transforms RGB color values into a single grayscale value using the standard coefficients defined by ITU-R Recommendation BT.709. This article explains the exact mathematical formula used by ImageMagick for this calculation, breaks down its coefficients, and clarifies how the software processes pixel data.

The Mathematical Formula

ImageMagick calculates perceived brightness under Rec709Luma using the following linear combination of the gamma-corrected Red (\(R'\)), Green (\(G'\)), and Blue (\(B'\)) channels:

\[Y' = 0.2126R' + 0.7152G' + 0.0722B'\]

In this equation:

  • \(Y'\) represents the resulting luma (perceived brightness) value.
  • \(R'\) is the gamma-corrected red component of the pixel.
  • \(G'\) is the gamma-corrected green component of the pixel.
  • \(B'\) is the gamma-corrected blue component of the pixel.

Coefficient Breakdown

The coefficients sum to exactly \(1.0\) (\(0.2126 + 0.7152 + 0.0722 = 1.0\)), ensuring that pure white (\(R'=1, G'=1, B'=1\)) outputs a maximum luma value of \(1.0\), and black (\(R'=0, G'=0, B'=0\)) outputs \(0\).

The specific weights reflect the spectral sensitivity of human vision:

  • Green (\(71.52\%\)): The human eye is most sensitive to green light, giving this channel the dominant weight in the brightness calculation.
  • Red (\(21.26\%\)): The eye has moderate sensitivity to red light.
  • Blue (\(7.22\%\)): The eye has the least sensitivity to blue wavelengths, so it contributes the smallest portion to overall perceived brightness.

Luma vs. Luminance in ImageMagick

It is important to distinguish between Luma (\(Y'\)) and Luminance (\(Y\)):

  • Rec709Luma (\(Y'\)): Computed directly from non-linear, gamma-compressed RGB values typically found in standard image files like PNG and JPEG. This is computationally fast and directly matches the operational standard used in high-definition television encoding.
  • Rec709Luminance (\(Y\)): Applies the same coefficients (\(0.2126, 0.7152, 0.0722\)), but only after de-gammaing the input pixels into a linear color space.

When using -colorspace Rec709Luma or setting -intensity Rec709Luma, ImageMagick applies the formula directly to the non-linear pixel values without performing a prior linear gamma conversion.
