ImageMagick Right to Left Text Direction Guide

Rendering scripts such as Arabic, Hebrew, Persian, and Urdu requires a non-standard text flow that progresses horizontally from right to left. This article explains how ImageMagick’s -direction right-to-left setting manages text placement, adjusts character progression vectors, and interacts with font rendering engines to correctly display Middle Eastern languages.

Understanding Horizontal Text Progression

By default, ImageMagick processes text using a left-to-right (LTR) vector, standard for Latin, Cyrillic, and Greek alphabets. Under this standard behavior, the rendering engine positions the initial glyph at an anchor coordinate and advances subsequent glyphs incrementally along the positive X-axis.

When the -direction right-to-left flag is specified, ImageMagick reverses this progression vector. Instead of advancing to the right, the rendering engine advances along the negative X-axis. The base coordinate origin for the text string shifts, anchoring the first character on the right side of the bounding box and laying down each subsequent character to its left.

Interaction with Text Shaping Engines

Middle Eastern languages—particularly Arabic and Persian—require more than just reversing the order of characters. They require complex text layout (CTL), which involves:

  1. Contextual Shaping: Arabic glyphs change their visual form depending on whether they appear in an isolated, initial, medial, or final position within a word.
  2. Ligature Substitution: Specific character pairs merge into distinct, single glyphs.
  3. Bidirectional (BiDi) Ordering: Strings containing a mix of Latin numbers/words and RTL script must properly alternate directions according to the Unicode Bidirectional Algorithm.

ImageMagick delegates this complex shaping to libraries such as FreeType, HarfBuzz, and Raqm. When -direction right-to-left is declared in an environment compiled with Raqm and HarfBuzz support, ImageMagick passes the direction flag directly to the text shaper. This ensures that:

  • Glyphs are shaped according to their correct logical reading order rather than visual insertion order.
  • Kerning and mark positioning (such as Arabic tashkeel or Hebrew niqqud) are calculated relative to the right-to-left progression.
  • Numbers within the text maintain standard left-to-right digit progression within the overall right-to-left sentence flow.

Syntax and Implementation

To render RTL text using standard ImageMagick tools, the -direction parameter must precede the text definition (such as label: or annotate:).

magick -background white -fill black \
       -font "Amiri-Regular.ttf" -pointsize 48 \
       -direction right-to-left \
       label:"مرحبا بالعالم" \
       output.png

When using -annotate or -draw, the coordinate supplied represents the right-hand starting point of the baseline when -direction right-to-left is active.

Alignment and Gravity Adjustments

The -direction setting directly affects how ImageMagick interprets text alignment via the -gravity option.

  • In default LTR rendering, left-aligned text (-gravity West) anchors the start of the line.
  • In RTL rendering, the start of the line corresponds to the right margin. Therefore, pairing -direction right-to-left with -gravity East or -gravity NorthEast aligns the start of the text block against the right edge of the canvas, mirroring the standard layout expected by readers of Middle Eastern scripts.
