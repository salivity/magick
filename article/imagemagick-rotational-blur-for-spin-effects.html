ImageMagick Rotational Blur for Spin Effects

This article explains how ImageMagick’s -rotational-blur operator simulates the appearance of rapid circular motion, specifically for creating spinning wheel effects. It covers the underlying mechanics of angular pixel averaging around a center point, the role of the sweep angle parameter, and how to effectively isolate and blur wheels on static objects without distorting the rest of the image.

The -rotational-blur command (which replaced the older, ambiguously named -radial-blur in ImageMagick 7) generates an optical illusion of spin by sweeping pixels along concentric circular paths rather than along a linear axis. The process defaults to using the exact geometric center of the image canvas as the rotation axis. Every pixel at a given radius from this center is sampled and averaged with neighboring pixels along an arc at that same distance, mimicking the exposure smearing captured by a camera shutter tracking a rotating object.

The physical plausibility of the spinning wheel effect relies on how rotational geometry works. Points near the outer edge of a circle travel a greater distance per rotation than points near the center. Because -rotational-blur takes an angle in degrees as its primary argument, the physical length of the blur path increases proportionally with the distance from the center. Consequently, the wheel's central hub retains significant visual sharpness, while the spokes and the outer tire tread smear heavily along their circumference, exactly mimicking real-world motion blur.

To apply this to a specific element like a car wheel, the basic syntax is:

magick wheel.png -rotational-blur 10 spinning_wheel.png

The numeric parameter represents the rotational sweep in degrees. A low value (e.g., 2 to 5 degrees) simulates moderate speed, while higher values (e.g., 15 to 30 degrees) generate high-velocity spin where spokes blend into a near-translucent disc.

Because -rotational-blur calculates its effect relative to the center of the image, applying it directly to an entire photograph of a vehicle will distort the vehicle body around the image center. To produce a clean, realistic composite:

  1. Crop or isolate the wheel: Extract the wheel as a standalone square image where the axle hub is positioned dead center.
  2. Apply the blur: Execute -rotational-blur with the desired angle on the isolated asset.
  3. Composite back: Mask and composite the blurred wheel back onto the original vehicle image using a circular alpha mask, preserving the static chassis while displaying dynamic movement on the wheels.
