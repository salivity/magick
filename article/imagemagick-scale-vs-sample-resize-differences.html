ImageMagick -scale vs -sample Resize Differences

This article explains the core differences between the -scale and -sample geometry operators in ImageMagick. While both options provide faster alternatives to the standard -resize command, they use fundamentally different algorithms to handle pixel data during enlargement and reduction. Understanding these differences helps you choose the right operator based on your needs for speed, visual quality, and color preservation.

How -sample Works

The -sample operator uses the nearest-neighbor interpolation method exclusively for both upscaling and downscaling.

  • Upscaling: Duplicates existing pixels to fill the new dimensions, resulting in sharp, blocky edges.
  • Downscaling: Simply drops rows and columns of pixels to shrink the image.
  • Color Handling: It never creates new colors. The output image retains the exact color palette of the original file.
  • Speed: It is the fastest resizing method in ImageMagick because it requires no mathematical blending or averaging.

How -scale Works

The -scale operator uses a hybrid approach: area-averaging (box filter) for reduction and pixel duplication for enlargement.

  • Upscaling: Acts identically to -sample by duplicating pixels, creating a sharp, pixelated enlargement without adding blur.
  • Downscaling: Averages groups of adjacent pixels into a single pixel.
  • Color Handling: It creates new blended intermediate colors when downscaling to smooth out transitions, but retains original colors when upscaling.
  • Speed: Faster than full filter-based -resize (such as Lanczos or Mitchell), but slightly slower than -sample during reduction due to the averaging calculations.

Visual Quality Comparison

  • Downscaling with -sample: Can produce severe aliasing, jagged lines, and moiré patterns because it discards pixel data without blending. Fine details, such as thin lines or text, may disappear entirely.
  • Downscaling with -scale: Produces a cleaner, smoother image than -sample by averaging pixels over target areas, preventing aliasing while preserving general contrast.
  • Upscaling: Both -scale and -sample yield visually identical pixelated results.

When to Use Each Option

Choose -sample when:

  • You are working with indexed color formats (such as GIF or 8-bit PNG) and must strictly prevent the creation of new colors.
  • You are scaling pixel art and want hard, crisp pixel borders.
  • Maximum processing throughput is required and visual artifacts on downscaled images are acceptable.

Choose -scale when:

  • You need to quickly generate thumbnails with reasonable visual quality without the heavy CPU overhead of -resize.
  • You want anti-aliased, smooth downscaling without introducing complex bicubic or Lanczos blurring.
