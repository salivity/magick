ImageMagick Shade Azimuth and Elevation Angles

The ImageMagick -shade operator creates a 3D relief or embossed effect on an image by simulating a distant light source. This effect is controlled entirely by two angular parameters: azimuth and elevation, formatted as -shade <azimuth>x<elevation>. Understanding how these two angles define the position and height of the virtual light source allows you to control the direction and intensity of highlights and shadows across your image.

Understanding the Shade Syntax

The command format is:

magick input.png -shade <azimuth>x<elevation> output.png

Both parameters are defined as angles in degrees.

Azimuth: Horizontal Direction of Light

The azimuth defines the directional angle of the light source along the horizontal image plane (X-Y plane), essentially acting like a compass bearing.

  • Range: Measured from 0° to 360°.
  • Starting Point (\(0^\circ\)): Positioned directly to the right (East, along the positive X-axis).
  • Rotation Direction: The angle rotates counter-clockwise around the image center.
    • \(0^\circ\): Light comes from the right (East / 3 o'clock).
    • \(90^\circ\): Light comes from the top (North / 12 o'clock).
    • \(180^\circ\): Light comes from the left (West / 9 o'clock).
    • \(270^\circ\): Light comes from the bottom (South / 6 o'clock).
    • \(360^\circ\): Completes the circle back to the right.

For standard embossed appearances, an azimuth between \(120^\circ\) and \(135^\circ\) (top-left) is commonly used to match natural light expectations.

Elevation: Vertical Angle of Light

The elevation defines the height of the light source above the surface of the image plane (along the Z-axis).

  • Range: Typically measured from 0° to 90°.
  • \(0^\circ\) (Horizon level): The light source is parallel to the surface plane. This creates maximum contrast, extreme shadows, and sharp relief outlines.
  • \(90^\circ\) (Zenith / Directly overhead): The light source is positioned directly perpendicular above the image. This produces even illumination across the surface with minimal directional shadowing.
  • \(45^\circ\) (Mid-level): A balanced height that provides clear depth without excessively harsh shadows.

Common Angle Combinations

  • Classic Top-Left Light: -shade 135x45 (Light originates from the upper left at a \(45^\circ\) angle, providing standard UI/graphic shading).
  • High Contrast / Low Light: -shade 180x15 (Light directly from the left, very close to the horizon for intense relief).
  • Flat Overhead Light: -shade 0x90 (Light source directly above the center).
