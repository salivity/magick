ImageMagick Shade Operator Parameters Explained

The ImageMagick -shade operator simulates a distant light source across an image to produce a 3D embossed or shaded relief effect. The operator's behavior, specifically the angle and intensity of the lighting, is controlled by two parameters formatted as azimuth x elevation.

The Syntax

The basic syntax for applying the shade effect is:

magick input.png -shade <azimuth>x<elevation> output.png

Azimuth (Light Angle and Direction)

Azimuth defines the directional angle of the light source around the horizontal plane, measured in degrees from 0 to 360. It dictates where the shadows will fall based on the compass direction of the light:

  • 0°: Light shines from the East (right side of the image).
  • 90°: Light shines from the North (top of the image).
  • 180°: Light shines from the West (left side of the image).
  • 270°: Light shines from the South (bottom of the image).

A standard starting value is often 120° or 135°, which places the light source at the top-left, matching the conventional lighting direction used in modern graphical user interfaces.

Elevation (Light Height and Shadow Intensity)

Elevation defines the angle of the light source above the horizon plane, measured in degrees from 0 to 90. This parameter directly controls the intensity, length, and contrast of the generated shadows:

  • 0° (Horizon level): The light is completely parallel to the surface, creating maximum shadow contrast, deep relief, and the most intense 3D effect.
  • 45° (Mid-level): The default balance, creating standard depth and moderate shadowing.
  • 90° (Directly overhead): The light shines straight down from above (the zenith). At this angle, directional shadows disappear, resulting in a flat, bright image with minimal relief effect.

Example Usage

To create a natural 3D bevel effect with light coming from the upper-left at a balanced height, run:

magick input.png -shade 135x45 output.png

To create a dramatic, high-intensity relief with elongated shadows from the top:

magick input.png -shade 90x15 output.png
