ImageMagick Swirl Distortion Parameters

This article explains the parameters used to control the swirl distortion effect in ImageMagick. The swirl effect twists pixels around the center of an image, simulating a vortex or whirlpool. While the primary control is the rotation angle specified in degrees, additional settings such as virtual-pixel behavior, interpolation methods, and region constraints directly influence the appearance and quality of the final distorted image.

The Primary Parameter: Degrees of Rotation

The core parameter for the swirl effect is the angle in degrees, applied using the -swirl operator:

magick input.jpg -swirl [degrees] output.jpg
  • Positive Values (e.g., -swirl 180): Rotates pixels around the center in a clockwise direction.
  • Negative Values (e.g., -swirl -180): Rotates pixels around the center in a counter-clockwise direction.
  • Zero (-swirl 0): Leaves the image unchanged.
  • Magnitude: Larger absolute numbers create a tighter, more pronounced spiral. Typical values range between -360 and 360, but higher values (e.g., 720 or 1440) can be used for extreme distortion.

Controlling Edge Behavior with -virtual-pixel

As pixels are twisted, areas near the image boundaries may pull in data from beyond the image edges. The -virtual-pixel setting controls how these boundary pixels are rendered:

  • edge: Replicates the border pixels outward (default behavior).
  • background: Fills exposed edge areas with the defined background color.
  • mirror or tile: Continues the swirl using reflected or repeated copies of the image.
  • transparent: Fills out-of-bounds pixels with transparency (ideal for PNG outputs).

Example:

magick input.jpg -virtual-pixel transparent -swirl 180 output.png

Smoothing Distortion with -interpolate

Distorting pixel coordinates can cause aliasing or jagged artifacts. The -interpolate parameter controls the algorithm used to sample and smooth colors between pixels:

  • Bilinear or Bicubic: Offers smoother blending and reduces pixelation artifacts within high-rotation zones.
  • Nearest: Produces a pixelated, retro look with no blending.
  • Average or Catrom: Provides balanced sharpness and smoothing.

Example:

magick input.jpg -interpolate Bicubic -swirl 240 output.jpg

Modifying the Swirl Center with -region

By default, ImageMagick applies the swirl distortion around the exact center of the image. To swirl a specific section or shift the center of the vortex, combine -swirl with the -region parameter:

magick input.jpg -region 300x300+100+100 -swirl 180 output.jpg

This restricts the swirl operation exclusively to a 300x300 pixel box offset by 100 pixels from the top-left corner, placing the center of the swirl inside that designated area rather than the canvas center.
