ImageMagick Transverse vs Vertical Flip

ImageMagick provides several geometric operators to manipulate image orientation, with -flip and -transverse representing two distinct types of reflections. A simple vertical flip reflects pixels exclusively across the horizontal centerline without modifying the image's canvas dimensions. In contrast, a transverse transformation reflects an image across its bottom-left to top-right anti-diagonal, simultaneously inverting axes and swapping the canvas width and height. This article breaks down how ImageMagick executes these two operations at the pixel and matrix levels, detailing coordinate mapping, aspect ratio changes, and computational differences.

The Mechanics of a Simple Vertical Flip (-flip)

In ImageMagick, the -flip operator executes a standard vertical reflection. It scans the source image along the vertical axis and swaps the scanlines from top to bottom while leaving the horizontal axis completely untouched.

  • Coordinate Transformation: A pixel located at coordinates \((x, y)\) in an image with width \(W\) and height \(H\) is mapped directly to \((x, H - 1 - y)\).
  • Dimensional Impact: The output dimensions remain identical to the input dimensions (\(W \times H\)).
  • Memory Handling: Because entire horizontal scanlines remain intact, ImageMagick can process a vertical flip with relatively low memory overhead by swapping row pointers or copying rows in reverse order without transposing pixel buffers.

Command syntax:

magick input.png -flip output.png

The Mechanics of a Transverse Transformation (-transverse)

The -transverse operator performs a reflection across the secondary diagonal (the anti-diagonal line extending from the bottom-left corner to the top-right corner of the image canvas).

  • Coordinate Transformation: A pixel at coordinate \((x, y)\) is remapped to \((H - 1 - y, W - 1 - x)\). Both spatial coordinates are inverted and swapped.
  • Dimensional Impact: The image's aspect ratio changes if the source is rectangular. An image with dimensions \(W \times H\) becomes \(H \times W\).
  • Compound Equivalence: In terms of standard affine operations, a transverse transformation is functionally identical to rotating the image 270 degrees clockwise and then applying a vertical flip (-rotate 270 -flip), or rotating 90 degrees clockwise and applying a horizontal flip (-rotate 90 -flop).
  • Memory Handling: Because pixels along horizontal rows must be relocated into vertical columns, the operation requires non-contiguous memory access or full buffer transposition, making it more computationally intensive than a single-axis flip.

Command syntax:

magick input.png -transverse output.png

Key Differences Summary

Feature Vertical Flip (-flip) Transverse (-transverse)
Reflection Axis Horizontal axis (\(Y = H / 2\)) Anti-diagonal (bottom-left to top-right)
Axis Inversion Vertical axis only Both horizontal and vertical axes
Coordinate Mapping \((x, y) \to (x, H - 1 - y)\) \((x, y) \to (H - 1 - y, W - 1 - x)\)
Canvas Dimensions Preserved (\(W \times H\)) Transposed (\(H \times W\))
Equivalent Steps Single-step mirror 90° rotation + horizontal reflection
