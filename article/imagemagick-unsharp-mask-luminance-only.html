ImageMagick Unsharp Mask Luminance Only

Applying an unsharp mask directly to an RGB image can amplify color noise and produce unsightly color fringing around high-contrast edges. By converting the image to a colorspace that isolates lightness from chromatic data—such as CIELAB or YCbCr—you can apply the sharpening filter strictly to the luminance channel. This article provides the exact ImageMagick commands required to sharpen brightness details while leaving color information completely untouched.

The Solution: Sharpening in the Lab Colorspace

In the Lab (CIELAB) colorspace, the L channel represents Lightness (luminance), while the a and b channels represent chromatic components. By targeting only the Lightness channel, sharpening artifacts are restricted to grayscale tonal transitions.

ImageMagick 7 Command

magick input.jpg -colorspace Lab -channel 0 -unsharp 0x1.0+1.0+0.05 +channel -colorspace sRGB output.jpg

ImageMagick 6 Command

convert input.jpg -colorspace Lab -channel 0 -unsharp 0x1.0+1.0+0.05 +channel -colorspace sRGB output.jpg

Command Breakdown

  • -colorspace Lab: Converts the source image from sRGB to the CIELAB colorspace.
  • -channel 0 (or -channel R): Limits subsequent operations to the first channel only. In Lab colorspace, the first channel corresponds to L (Lightness).
  • -unsharp {radius}x{sigma}+{amount}+{threshold}: Applies the unsharp mask filter to the active channel:
    • radius: The radius of the Gaussian blur (set to 0 to let ImageMagick calculate it automatically from sigma).
    • sigma: The standard deviation of the Gaussian blur (typically 0.5 to 2.0).
    • amount: The strength of the sharpening (typically 0.5 to 2.0).
    • threshold: The minimum tonal difference required before edge enhancement is applied, preventing amplification of subtle noise (typically 0.02 to 0.05).
  • +channel: Resets the channel mask so that subsequent operations apply to all channels again.
  • -colorspace sRGB: Converts the image back to the standard sRGB colorspace for viewing and saving.

Alternative: Using the YUV / YCbCr Colorspace

You can achieve similar results using the YCbCr colorspace, where the Y channel carries luminance:

magick input.jpg -colorspace YCbCr -channel 0 -unsharp 0x1.0+1.0+0.05 +channel -colorspace sRGB output.jpg

Both Lab and YCbCr completely separate luminance from chrominance, successfully eliminating chromatic halos and noise amplification during sharpening workflows.
