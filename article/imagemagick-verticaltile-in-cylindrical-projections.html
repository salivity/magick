ImageMagick VerticalTile in Cylindrical Projections

The -virtual-pixel VerticalTile setting in ImageMagick defines how pixels outside the native image bounds are sampled, wrapping the image seamlessly along the vertical axis while terminating it horizontally. In cylindrical projections, this configuration treats the vertical edges (top and bottom) as a continuous, closed loop around the cylinder's circumference, while treating the horizontal edges (left and right) as the finite ends or caps of the cylinder.

Mechanics of VerticalTile

When ImageMagick performs geometric distortions—such as -distort Cylinder2Plane or cylindrical warping—the algorithm frequently needs to sample pixel coordinates that fall outside the source image boundaries. The -virtual-pixel option controls this behavior.

With VerticalTile, ImageMagick applies a modulo operation to the vertical coordinate:

\[y' = y \pmod{\text{height}}\]

This creates an infinite vertical repetition of the source image. Horizontally, however, the coordinate is not wrapped. Any coordinate where \(x < 0\) or \(x \ge \text{width}\) is treated as out-of-bounds and falls back to the configured background color or transparency.

Handling Cylindrical Projection Borders

In cylindrical mapping, one dimension represents the continuous circumference (spanning \(360^\circ\)), and the perpendicular dimension represents the length or height along the central axis.

Vertical Borders (Top and Bottom)

Because VerticalTile repeats the image along the Y-axis, sampling just past the bottom edge retrieves pixels from the corresponding top edge, and vice versa.

  • Seam Elimination: When the circumference of the cylinder is aligned vertically, this wrapping prevents hard seams, dark lines, or interpolation artifacts where the edges meet.
  • Filter Smoothing: Resampling filters (such as Gaussian or Lanczos) can overlap the top and bottom boundaries without clipping, ensuring that pixel blending remains consistent across the seam.

Horizontal Borders (Left and Right)

The horizontal axis remains non-periodic under VerticalTile.

  • Distinct Boundaries: Pixels mapped past the left or right boundaries do not bleed into the opposing side. Instead, they produce clean terminations representing the circular caps or the open ends of the cylinder.
  • Edge Artifacts: If a distortion pulls coordinates past the horizontal limits, ImageMagick uses the image's canvas background color. To prevent a visible transition to the background color at these terminal borders, the source image must either be pre-padded or clipped appropriately.

Typical Use Case

Standard panoramic projections typically align the cylinder's circumference along the horizontal axis, requiring HorizontalTile. The VerticalTile setting is specifically utilized when:

  1. The cylindrical axis is oriented horizontally (a "rolling cylinder" or barrel alignment).
  2. The image data has been rotated 90 degrees prior to processing to optimize scanline performance or fit vertical unrolling algorithms.
