ImageMagick Vignette Radius Sigma and Offset

This article provides a technical overview of how ImageMagick’s -vignette operator shades photo borders to create soft-focus edge effects. It explains the geometry string format—radiusxsigma{+-}x{+-}y—and details the precise roles that radius, sigma, and coordinate offsets play in controlling border falloff, blur feathering, and the shape of the visible frame.

The -vignette command in ImageMagick simulates an optical vignette by generating an elliptical mask, shading the outer edges toward the current background color. The full argument takes the following geometry syntax:

-vignette {radius}x{sigma}{+-}x{+-}y

Radius and Sigma: Controlling Edge Feathering

The radius and sigma arguments determine the softness and falloff curve of the vignette transition. They function identically to the parameters in ImageMagick’s Gaussian blur operator:

  • Sigma (\(\sigma\)): Represents the standard deviation of the Gaussian blur applied to the vignette mask. It is the primary value controlling how softly the central, untouched image dissolves into the shaded border. A small sigma produces a sharp, abrupt edge, while a large sigma creates an expansive, gradual fade.
  • Radius (\(r\)): Sets the physical pixel radius of the blur convolution kernel. While sigma dictates the blur profile, radius sets its mathematical limits. If radius is set to 0, ImageMagick automatically calculates an optimal kernel radius based on the specified sigma (typically roughly three times the sigma value) to ensure a complete, smooth fade without unnecessary computation.

Offset Parameters (X and Y): Defining Border Margins

The x and y offsets dictate the geometry and reach of the ellipse before feathering is applied. They measure inward from the outer borders of the image:

  • X Offset: Controls the horizontal inset from the left and right edges. Increasing this value pulls the vignette boundary further inward toward the center along the horizontal axis, narrowing the clear area of the photo.
  • Y Offset: Controls the vertical inset from the top and bottom edges. Increasing this value pulls the vignette boundary further inward from the top and bottom margins.

Offsets can be expressed in absolute pixel values or as percentages by appending the % symbol (e.g., +10+10 or +5%+5%). When offsets are omitted, ImageMagick defaults to an offset of 10% of the image's dimensions, producing a centered ellipse that fits neatly inside the frame.

How the Parameters Work Together

ImageMagick processes the command by first constructing a solid elliptical mask inscribed inside the boundaries determined by the image dimensions minus the x and y offsets. Once this base boundary is established, it applies a Gaussian blur defined by the radius and sigma values to the mask edges.

The resulting blurred mask acts as an alpha map: pixels fully inside the boundary retain 100% of their original color, pixels outside the feather boundary take on the designated background color (defaulting to white or transparent depending on the image profile), and intermediate pixels are shaded smoothly between the original photo and the border color.
