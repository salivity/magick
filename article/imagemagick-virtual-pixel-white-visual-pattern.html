ImageMagick -virtual-pixel White Visual Pattern

This guide explains the visual behavior of the -virtual-pixel White setting in ImageMagick when processing boundaries beyond active canvas limits. When image transformations, distortions, or spatial filters query coordinates outside the defined image dimensions, this setting defines how those external areas are rendered. The following sections describe the exact visual pattern produced, its interaction with common operations, and how it behaves compared to other virtual pixel options.

The Resulting Visual Pattern

The -virtual-pixel White setting produces a uniform, flat, solid white area spanning infinitely outside the active canvas boundary. It does not generate complex geometric patterns, gradients, textures, or edge repetitions. Instead, any pixel lookup that lands outside the image's coordinate limits returns a fully saturated white value across all relevant color channels (for example, RGB value 255, 255, 255 in an 8-bit image or 65535, 65535, 65535 in a 16-bit Q16 build). If the image format supports an alpha channel, this virtual white space is rendered as completely opaque unless explicitly defined otherwise.

Behavior During Common Transformations

The solid white pattern manifests visually during several types of image manipulation:

  • Distortions and Rotations: When using operations such as -distort Perspective, -distort Affine, or -rotate without expanding the output canvas, previously out-of-frame areas that rotate into view appear as a seamless, flat white background behind the transformed image content.
  • Neighborhood Operations and Convolutions: When applying filters like -blur, -gaussian-blur, or sharpening operations near the edges of an image, the algorithm samples pixels beyond the border. With White enabled, the algorithm treats neighboring off-canvas space as solid white, causing the outer edges of the image to fade toward white rather than dark, transparent, or repeated border colors.
  • Displacements: In displacement mapping, any mapping vector that pushes coordinates beyond the canvas will sample pure white, resulting in white streaks or fields invading the frame from the boundary.

Comparison to Other Modes

Unlike pattern-generating settings such as -virtual-pixel Tile (which repeats the image in a repeating grid) or -virtual-pixel Mirror (which flips and repeats the image across the boundary), the White option behaves identically to an infinite solid color canvas. It is structurally equivalent to -virtual-pixel Background when the background color is explicitly set to #FFFFFF.
