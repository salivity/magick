ImageMagick Wave Operator Mathematical Waveform

This article explains the specific mathematical waveform applied by ImageMagick’s -wave operator to distort an image across the vertical axis. It details the underlying mathematical formula, explains how pixel coordinates are mapped during transformation, and outlines the role of the operator's configuration parameters.

The ImageMagick -wave operator applies a sine wave (sinusoidal waveform) to displace pixels along the vertical axis.

When the -wave operator is executed, it alters the image by shifting each vertical column of pixels up or down according to a standard trigonometric sine function. The vertical shift for any given point is calculated using the following formula:

\[\Delta y = \text{amplitude} \times \sin\left(\frac{2\pi \times x}{\text{wavelength}}\right)\]

In this mathematical operation:

  • \(x\) is the horizontal coordinate of the pixel column.
  • \(\Delta y\) is the vertical offset applied to pixels within that column.
  • amplitude sets the peak vertical displacement in pixels, controlling the height of the wave's crests and the depth of its troughs.
  • wavelength sets the horizontal distance in pixels over which the sinusoidal cycle repeats.

While the displacement occurs strictly along the vertical axis (moving pixels up and down), the magnitude and direction of the movement depend directly on the horizontal position. This produces a smooth, continuous ripple effect across the width of the image.
