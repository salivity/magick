ImageMagick YUV and YCbCr Color Space Conversion

ImageMagick provides built-in mechanisms to convert standard RGB imagery into video-oriented color models such as YUV and YCbCr. This article covers how ImageMagick handles these conversions, the distinction between YUV and YCbCr in its processing pipeline, how to configure color space transformations via the command line, and how to manage chroma subsampling and signal ranges for broadcast workflows.

Understanding YUV vs. YCbCr in ImageMagick

In digital imaging and broadcast engineering, YCbCr is the scaled, digital representation of color differences, whereas YUV historically refers to composite analog video signals. ImageMagick includes definitions for both color spaces, though digital workflows primarily target YCbCr matrices defined by ITU-R standards (such as BT.601 for SD video and BT.709 for HD video).

When invoked, ImageMagick applies mathematical transformation matrices to convert red, green, and blue values into:

  • Y (Luma): Perceived brightness.
  • Cb / U (Chroma Blue): Blue-difference chroma component.
  • Cr / V (Chroma Red): Red-difference chroma component.

Basic Color Space Conversion Commands

The primary flag for switching color spaces in ImageMagick is -colorspace.

To convert an RGB image to YCbCr:

magick input.png -colorspace YCbCr output.png

To convert an RGB image to YUV:

magick input.png -colorspace YUV output.png

During this operation, ImageMagick transforms the underlying pixel data into the target three-channel representation. If saved to a standard container like PNG, the channels store the luma and chroma values directly in the container's standard R, G, and B channels.

Full Range vs. Limited (Broadcast) Range

A critical consideration in broadcast workflows is the signal range:

  • Full Range (PC Levels): Values span the entire 8-bit depth from 0 to 255.
  • Limited Range (Studio/Broadcast Levels): Luma (Y) spans 16 to 235, while Chroma (Cb/Cr) spans 16 to 240.

By default, ImageMagick's mathematical transform outputs full-range (0–255) values. To prepare assets strictly conforming to broadcast-legal limited ranges, you must apply scaling and level offsets manually using operators like -level or custom color matrix transformations (-color-matrix) to remap black and white points to studio swing limits.

Chroma Subsampling Control

Broadcast video formats often reduce color bandwidth via chroma subsampling (such as 4:2:2 or 4:2:0). ImageMagick allows control over this sampling scheme using the -sampling-factor option when saving to formats that support native subsampling, such as JPEG or raw video planes.

Common sampling configurations include:

  • 4:4:4 (No Subsampling): -sampling-factor 1x1,1x1,1x1
  • 4:2:2 (Horizontal Subsampling): -sampling-factor 2x1,1x1,1x1
  • 4:2:0 (Horizontal and Vertical Subsampling): -sampling-factor 2x2,1x1,1x1

Example:

magick input.png -colorspace YCbCr -sampling-factor 2x2,1x1,1x1 output.jpg

Handling Raw YUV and YCbCr Streams

Raw YUV files contain planar or interleaved byte streams without metadata headers specifying dimensions or bit depth. To process raw YUV streams, ImageMagick requires explicit dimension, depth, and sampling arguments on input:

magick -size 1920x1080 -depth 8 -sampling-factor 4:2:0 input.yuv output.png

To extract individual components (such as isolating the Y luma channel for analysis), you can separate the channels after conversion:

magick input.png -colorspace YCbCr -separate channel_%d.png

This command outputs three individual grayscale images corresponding to the Y, Cb, and Cr planes.
