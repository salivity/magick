Inspect CMYK Ink Coverage with ImageMagick

Measuring ink coverage is a crucial pre-press step to prevent ink smearing, ensure standard color reproduction, and comply with commercial printing limits. This guide demonstrates how to use ImageMagick to calculate average channel percentages, determine overall ink usage, find the peak Total Area Coverage (TAC) per pixel, and locate areas exceeding printer ink limits.

1. Inspect Average Ink Coverage Per Channel

To inspect the average percentage of ink used for each individual CMYK channel (Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, and Key/Black), use the -format option combined with the %[fx:...] operator:

magick input.tif -format "Cyan: %[fx:mean.c*100]%%\nMagenta: %[fx:mean.m*100]%%\nYellow: %[fx:mean.y*100]%%\nBlack: %[fx:mean.k*100]%%\n" info:

This outputs the mean density of each ink across the entire image on a scale from 0% to 100%.

2. Calculate Total Average Ink Coverage

To get the combined average ink percentage across all four channels, add the channel means together:

magick input.tif -format "Total Average Coverage: %[fx:(mean.c+mean.m+mean.y+mean.k)*100]%%\n" info:

The theoretical maximum output is 400% (100% ink coverage on all four plates across the entire surface).

3. Find the Peak Total Area Coverage (TAC / TIC)

Commercial print providers enforce strict Total Area Coverage (TAC) or Total Ink Coverage (TIC) limits (typically between 280% and 320%) to ensure paper stock can absorb the ink.

To determine the maximum TAC percentage found on any single pixel in the image:

magick input.tif -channel CMYK -separate +channel -evaluate-sequence add -format "Maximum TAC: %[fx:maxima*100]%%\n" info:

Explanation of the command:

  • -channel CMYK -separate +channel: Splits the CMYK image into four distinct grayscale layers representing C, M, Y, and K.
  • -evaluate-sequence add: Sums the values of all four layers per pixel.
  • -format "%[fx:maxima*100]%%\n": Finds the highest combined pixel value in the image and displays it as a percentage (where 1.0 = 100% and 4.0 = 400%).

4. Highlight Areas Exceeding Ink Limits

If your image exceeds the maximum allowable TAC, you can generate a visual black-and-white mask highlighting the problematic pixels.

For example, to find all areas exceeding a 300% TAC limit:

magick input.tif -channel CMYK -separate +channel -evaluate-sequence add -threshold 300% over_inked_mask.png

In the resulting over_inked_mask.png file, all white pixels represent coordinates where the combined CMYK ink coverage exceeds 300%.
