Isolate Specular Highlights in ImageMagick

Isolating specular highlights into a separate mask using ImageMagick involves extracting luminance information from an image, applying high-pass thresholding to isolate near-white reflective spots, and refining the edges using morphology or blur operations. This guide covers the direct commands needed to separate bright, non-diffuse reflections into a clean, reusable grayscale mask.

Method 1: Luminance Thresholding

Specular highlights are typically the brightest pixels in an image. The quickest way to isolate them is by converting the image to grayscale and applying a high percentage threshold.

magick input.jpg -colorspace Gray -threshold 90% highlight_mask.png
  • -colorspace Gray: Converts the RGB channels into a single luminance channel.
  • -threshold 90%: Turns all pixels with a brightness value above 90% pure white (#FFFFFF) and all other pixels pure black (#000000). Adjust this percentage based on the intensity of your highlights.

Method 2: Isolating via the HSL Lightness Channel

If the image contains saturated bright colors (like vibrant yellows or cyans) that you do not want included in your specular mask, isolate the Lightness channel from the HSL colorspace instead of standard grayscale conversion.

magick input.jpg -colorspace HSL -channel 2 -separate -threshold 92% highlight_mask.png
  • -colorspace HSL: Switches the image data to Hue, Saturation, and Lightness.
  • -channel 2 -separate: Extracts only the Lightness (L) channel (index 2 in ImageMagick).
  • -threshold 92%: Isolates only the highest peaks of luminosity.

Method 3: Advanced Isolation with Mask Cleanup

Specular highlights often create tiny, noisy pinpricks across textured surfaces. To generate a cleaner, production-ready mask, you can combine thresholding with morphological operations to remove noise and soften edges.

magick input.jpg -colorspace Gray \
  -threshold 88% \
  -morphology Open Disk:1 \
  -blur 0x1 \
  highlight_mask.png
  • -morphology Open Disk:1: Removes isolated single-pixel noise while preserving the shape of larger highlight areas.
  • -blur 0x1: Adds a slight gradient to the mask edges, making it blend naturally when used as an alpha mask or compositing layer.

Applying the Mask Back to the Image

To verify the isolation or extract the isolated specular highlights onto a transparent background, use the generated mask as an alpha channel:

magick input.jpg highlight_mask.png -alpha off -compose CopyOpacity -composite highlights_only.png

This applies the mask directly over the source image, leaving only the isolated specular reflections visible on a transparent canvas.
