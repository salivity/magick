LinearLight Composite Mode in ImageMagick

The LinearLight composite mode in ImageMagick is a contrast-increasing blend operator that combines the effects of Linear Dodge and Linear Burn based on the brightness of the composite (overlay) image. This article explains how the underlying mathematical formula directly impacts pixels, brightening highlights when the blend layer is above 50% gray and deepening shadows when it is below, resulting in pronounced tonal shifts and high-contrast effects.

How LinearLight Calculates Pixel Values

ImageMagick processes pixel values on a normalized scale from 0.0 (pure black) to 1.0 (pure white), where 0.5 represents neutral 50% gray. The mathematical formula governing the LinearLight operator is:

\[\text{Output} = \text{Base} + 2 \times \text{Blend} - 1.0\]

In this equation:

  • Base is the destination image pixel.
  • Blend is the source (overlay) image pixel.
  • Output is clamped between 0.0 and 1.0.

Because the calculation relies on simple addition and subtraction without the exponential curves found in modes like SoftLight or Overlay, the tonal transformation is strictly linear and aggressive.

Alteration of Highlights

When the blend image pixel value is greater than 0.5 (lighter than neutral gray), LinearLight behaves as a Linear Dodge (addition):

  • Mechanism: The formula simplifies to adding an offset: \(\text{Base} + 2 \times (\text{Blend} - 0.5)\).
  • Impact on Highlights: Midtones and bright areas in the base image are pushed higher toward pure white. Because the adjustment is linear, highlights do not gently roll off; instead, they expand outward quickly.
  • Clipping: High-luminosity areas rapidly reach 1.0 (white clipping), blowing out subtle highlight details if the blend layer contains strong light tones.

Alteration of Shadows

When the blend image pixel value is less than 0.5 (darker than neutral gray), LinearLight behaves as a Linear Burn (subtraction):

  • Mechanism: The formula decreases pixel luminosity: \(\text{Base} - 2 \times (0.5 - \text{Blend})\).
  • Impact on Shadows: Dark pixels and lower-midtones in the base image are driven further down toward pure black. Shadow regions lose ambient light rapidly, creating deep, harsh blacks.
  • Crushing: Subtle shadow gradients are frequently crushed to 0.0 (black clipping), leading to high-contrast silhouettes or dramatically reduced dynamic range in the darker portions of the image.

Behavior at Neutral Gray

When the blend pixel is exactly 0.5 (neutral gray), the formula yields:

\[\text{Base} + 2 \times (0.5) - 1.0 = \text{Base}\]

At this value, the base pixel remains completely unchanged. Neutral gray acts as an invisible threshold, allowing editors to isolate highlight boosts and shadow decreases by painting above or below the 50% luminance mark on the overlay layer.

Practical Usage in ImageMagick

To apply this mode using modern ImageMagick syntax:

magick base.png overlay.png -compose LinearLight -composite output.png

Because of its harsh highlight clipping and aggressive shadow crushing, LinearLight is primarily utilized in specialized workflows, such as frequency separation for texture sharpening, specular highlight enhancement, or creating stylized, high-contrast graphic art.
