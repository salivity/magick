Map a Flat Graphic to a Cylinder in ImageMagick

Mapping a flat 2D graphic onto a cylindrical shape in ImageMagick allows you to create realistic mockups of products such as soda cans, bottles, and coffee mugs. This process simulates 3D curvature and perspective compression by using ImageMagick's built-in distortion operators or displacement mapping techniques.

Method 1: Using the Arc Distortion Operator

The -distort Arc operator is the quickest way to bend a flat rectangular image into a cylindrical or conical arc. This is ideal for curved labels or graphics mapped along a curved surface.

magick input.png -virtual-pixel transparent -distort Arc 60 output.png
  • -virtual-pixel transparent: Prevents the edges of the image from smearing across the newly exposed canvas area.
  • -distort Arc <angle>: Specifies the curvature angle in degrees. A smaller value (e.g., 30 to 60) creates a subtle curve, while larger values (e.g., 180 to 360) wrap the graphic more aggressively into a full ring or cylinder.

To refine the arc with custom radius and center controls:

magick input.png -virtual-pixel transparent -distort Arc "60 100 200 0" output.png

The four arguments represent: Angle, Radius, Target_Width, and Center_Offset_Y.

Method 2: Realistic Cylindrical Projection Using Displacement Maps

For a true front-facing 3D cylinder view (such as a label on a straight-sided beverage can), graphics should compress horizontally toward the outer edges according to a sinusoidal curve rather than simply bending in an arc.

Step 1: Generate a Cylindrical Displacement Map

Create a horizontal gradient that represents the non-linear curve of a cylinder:

magick -size 500x300 gradient:black-white -function Sinusoid 1,-90,0.5,0.5 disp_map.png

Step 2: Apply the Displacement to the Graphic

Apply the displacement map to compress the graphic toward the left and right edges:

magick label.png disp_map.png -virtual-pixel transparent -compose Displace -set option:compose:args 40x0 -composite cylinder_label.png
  • -set option:compose:args 40x0: Defines the maximum horizontal pixel displacement (e.g., 40 pixels) and vertical displacement (0 pixels). Adjust the horizontal value based on your graphic's width and the desired curvature depth.

Method 3: Adding 3D Cylinder Shading

To complete the 3D cylindrical effect, combine the displaced graphic with a shading layer that mimics specular highlights and edge shadows:

magick label.png disp_map.png -virtual-pixel transparent -compose Displace -set option:compose:args 40x0 -composite \
\( -size 500x300 gradient:black-white -function Sinusoid 1,-90 -level 0%,80% \) \
-compose Multiply -composite final_cylinder.png

This pipeline warps the label around the cylindrical plane and multiplies a smooth cylindrical lighting gradient across the surface to produce a realistic product render.
