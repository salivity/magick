Mean vs Median Brightness in ImageMagick Identify

When analyzing an image using ImageMagick's identify -verbose command, the output provides detailed channel statistics, including mean and median brightness values. While both metrics measure the central tendency of pixel intensities, they do so differently: the mean represents the mathematical average of all pixel values and is heavily influenced by extreme highlights or shadows, whereas the median represents the exact middle value of all sorted pixels, making it resistant to outliers and high-contrast anomalies.

Understanding Mean Pixel Brightness

The mean brightness is calculated by summing the intensity values of every pixel in a specific channel (or across all channels) and dividing that total by the total number of pixels.

\[\text{Mean} = \frac{\sum \text{Pixel Values}}{\text{Total Pixels}}\]

Characteristics of Mean Value:

  • Sensitivity to Outliers: If an image is mostly dark but contains a few extremely bright highlights (such as streetlights in a night photo), the mean value will be pulled significantly higher.
  • Overall Energy: The mean accurately reflects the total luminous energy of an image, which is useful for exposure correction, calculating average brightness across video frames, or tone mapping.
  • Format: In ImageMagick, mean values are reported either in raw quantum range (0–65535 for Q16 builds, 0–255 for Q8 builds) or normalized (0.0 to 1.0) depending on how you query the format string (e.g., %[mean] vs. %[fx:mean]).

Understanding Median Pixel Brightness

The median brightness is calculated by sorting all pixel intensity values in ascending order and selecting the value located at the exact 50th percentile.

Characteristics of Median Value:

  • Resistance to Outliers: Extreme values do not distort the median. A small patch of blown-out white pixels will not shift the median if the rest of the image is dark.
  • Representative Midtone: The median reflects the typical brightness of the majority of the image, making it ideal for background separation, noise filtering, and thresholding.
  • Format: Similar to the mean, ImageMagick provides median values across the channel's quantum depth or via formatted strings like %[median].

Key Differences Summary

Feature Mean Brightness Median Brightness
Calculation Arithmetic sum divided by pixel count Middle value of sorted pixel list
Outlier Sensitivity High (skewed by hot pixels, glare, deep shadows) Low (ignores localized extreme values)
Best Used For Overall exposure measurement, total luminosity Typical scene brightness, thresholding, noise-robust analysis
ImageMagick String %[mean] or %[standard:mean] %[median]

Example: High-Contrast Comparison

Consider a 10-pixel grayscale image with pixel intensities ranging from 0 (black) to 100 (white):

[10, 12, 12, 14, 15, 15, 16, 18, 95, 98]

  • Mean: \((10 + 12 + 12 + 14 + 15 + 15 + 16 + 18 + 95 + 98) / 10 = \mathbf{30.5}\)
  • Median: Middle values are 15 and 15 \(\rightarrow \mathbf{15}\)

In this scenario, the median accurately indicates that the majority of pixels are dark (around 15), while the mean suggests a moderately bright image (30.5) due to the two extreme highlights.

Extracting Values Using ImageMagick

To view both values in standard verbose output:

magick identify -verbose input.jpg

To extract only the numerical mean and median values directly using format specifiers:

magick identify -format "Mean: %[mean]\nMedian: %[median]\n" input.jpg

For normalized values between 0.0 and 1.0:

magick identify -format "Mean (0-1): %[fx:mean]\nMedian (0-1): %[fx:median]\n" input.jpg
