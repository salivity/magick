Normalize Document Backgrounds in ImageMagick

Scanned documents often suffer from uneven lighting, yellowed paper, shadows, and scan artifacts that reduce legibility and waste printer ink. ImageMagick provides robust command-line tools to remove these imperfections by flattening the background to pure white while preserving the sharpness of dark text. This guide covers the most effective ImageMagick techniques to clean document scans, ranging from simple color leveling to adaptive background division.

Method 1: Background Division (Best for Uneven Lighting and Shadows)

When a document has non-uniform lighting, such as a gradient shadow near the spine of a book, simple thresholding will cut off text. The division method isolates the background lighting into a blurred mask and divides the original image by this mask, effectively flattening the lighting across the entire page.

magick input.png ( +clone -blur 0x25 ) -compose Divide -composite output.png

How it works:

  1. +clone creates a duplicate of the scan in memory.
  2. -blur 0x25 blurs the clone heavily so that text vanishes and only the background illumination and paper color remain.
  3. -compose Divide -composite divides the original image pixel values by the blurred background, turning the paper white and normalizing all lighting variations.

To enhance contrast after division, chain a -level adjustment:

magick input.png ( +clone -blur 0x25 ) -compose Divide -composite -level 5%,95% output.png

Method 2: White Balance with Threshold and Level Adjustments

If the lighting across the page is uniform but the paper is gray or discolored, adjusting the white and black points using -level is the fastest method.

magick input.png -colorspace Gray -level 15%,85% output.png
  • -colorspace Gray: Removes color tinting (e.g., yellow aged paper).
  • -level 15%,85%: Forces all pixels with brightness above 85% to become pure white (#FFFFFF) and all pixels below 15% to become pure black (#000000), expanding the midtones.

To dynamically stretch the contrast based on image content without guessing percentages:

magick input.png -colorspace Gray -contrast-stretch 1%x99% output.png

Method 3: Local Adaptive Thresholding (Best for Low-Quality Scans)

For low-contrast documents or scans where fine text is fading into a dark background, Local Adaptive Thresholding (-lat) analyzes pixel neighborhoods to distinguish text from the surrounding paper.

magick input.png -colorspace Gray -negate -lat 25x25+10% -negate output.png
  • -negate: Inverts colors because the -lat operator expects light text on a dark background.
  • -lat 25x25+10%: Evaluates a 25x25 pixel window and sets pixels to white if they are 10% brighter than the local average.
  • Second -negate: Inverts the image back to dark text on a white background.

Complete Production Recipe

To process a batch of scanned documents, convert them to crisp grayscale, remove dark borders, and achieve a uniform white background in one operation:

magick input.png \
  -colorspace Gray \
  ( +clone -blur 0x30 ) -compose Divide -composite \
  -linear-stretch 1%x1% \
  -deskew 40% \
  output.png
  • -deskew 40%: Automatically straightens pages tilted up to 40 degrees.
  • -linear-stretch 1%x1%: Ensures maximum contrast between text and background after illumination flattening.
