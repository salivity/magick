Normalize Paper Background Colors in ImageMagick

Scanned documents often suffer from uneven lighting, yellowing, shadows, and varying paper textures that degrade legibility and Optical Character Recognition (OCR) accuracy. This guide demonstrates how to use ImageMagick to flatten background inconsistencies, equalize illumination, and normalize paper color variations across scanned forms, producing uniform white backgrounds while keeping foreground text and form lines sharp.

The Division Normalization Method

The most effective way to eliminate uneven background color and lighting gradients without destroying fine text is division normalization. This technique creates a heavily blurred clone of the document representing the background illumination map, and then divides the original image by that blurred mask.

Run the following command in ImageMagick v7:

magick input.png ( +clone -blur 0x25 ) -compose Divide -composite output.png
  • +clone: Creates a temporary copy of the original scan in memory.
  • -blur 0x25: Blurs the cloned image extensively so text disappears, leaving only the lighting gradients and background color shifts. Adjust the radius/sigma (e.g., 0x15 to 0x50) based on your scan resolution; higher resolution files require larger values.
  • -compose Divide -composite: Divides the original image pixel values by the blurred background, neutralizing any background variations and turning the paper into a uniform white.

Complete Multi-Step Form Processing Pipeline

For real-world scanned forms, combining division normalization with grayscale conversion and contrast stretching ensures maximum clarity:

magick input.png \
  -colorspace Gray \
  \( +clone -blur 0x30 \) \
  -compose Divide -composite \
  -auto-level \
  -contrast-stretch 0.5%x0.5% \
  output.png
  • -colorspace Gray: Removes color artifacts and scanner tint (yellow/blue cast).
  • -auto-level: Stretches the dynamic range across the entire grayscale spectrum.
  • -contrast-stretch 0.5%x0.5%: Clips the brightest 0.5% to pure white and the darkest 0.5% to pure black, guaranteeing that faint residual gray patterns vanish entirely.

Local Adaptive Thresholding for Extreme Variations

When forms contain deep fold shadows or extreme non-uniform illumination, local adaptive thresholding (LAT) can be used to convert the image into a clean black-and-white mask based on neighboring pixel intensities:

magick input.png -colorspace Gray -negate -lat 25x25+10% -negate output.png
  • -lat WxH+offset%: Inspects a pixel window of size WxH and calculates a localized threshold. The offset% controls the sensitivity—higher values preserve lighter text, while lower values eliminate noise.

Batch Processing Form Directories

To apply background normalization to an entire folder of scanned forms, use the mogrify tool:

magick mogrify -path ./normalized -colorspace Gray -blur 0x0 \( +clone -blur 0x30 \) -compose Divide -composite -contrast-stretch 0.5%x0.5% *.png

This processes all PNG files in the current directory and places the cleaned, normalized documents into the ./normalized directory.
