Protect Foreground Objects in ImageMagick Seam Carving

Seam carving, known as liquid rescaling in ImageMagick, is a content-aware image resizing technique that reduces or expands images by removing or duplicating low-energy paths of pixels. However, automated seam carving often misidentifies important foreground subjects as low energy, resulting in unnatural warping and distortion. This article explains how to prevent distortion by generating protection masks and applying them using ImageMagick's liquid rescale features.

Understanding the Liquid Rescale Protection Mechanism

ImageMagick utilizes the liblqr library to perform content-aware resizing via the -liquid-rescale operator. When an image is resized, the algorithm computes an energy map across all pixels and removes the paths (seams) with the lowest cumulative energy.

To prevent the algorithm from cutting through foreground subjects, you must supply a feature mask (protection mask). A protection mask artificially increases the energy of designated pixels to the maximum possible value, forcing the seam-carving algorithm to route seams around your foreground subject rather than through it.

Step 1: Create a Protection Mask

A protection mask is a grayscale image with the exact same pixel dimensions as your source image:

  • White pixels (#FFFFFF): High energy (100% protected from removal).
  • Black pixels (#000000): Standard energy (eligible for seam removal).
  • Gray tones: Partial weighting (proportional protection).

You can create this mask using any image editing software by painting white over the subjects you want to protect (such as people, text, or primary products) and keeping the rest of the canvas completely black. Save this file as mask.png.

Step 2: Run the ImageMagick Liquid Rescale Command

In ImageMagick 7, protection masks are defined using the -define operator prior to running the -liquid-rescale command.

Basic Syntax for Width Reduction

To reduce an image's width while protecting the masked area:

magick input.jpg -define liquid-rescale:feature-mask=mask.png -liquid-rescale 600x100% output.jpg

In this command:

  • input.jpg is the original image.
  • -define liquid-rescale:feature-mask=mask.png points ImageMagick to the protection mask.
  • -liquid-rescale 600x100% scales the image width down to 600 pixels while keeping 100% of the original height.
  • output.jpg is the preserved, resized output.

Combining Feature and Discard Masks

You can also simultaneously protect foreground subjects and target unwanted background areas for prioritized deletion using a discard mask:

magick input.jpg \
  -define liquid-rescale:feature-mask=protect_mask.png \
  -define liquid-rescale:discard-mask=remove_mask.png \
  -liquid-rescale 500x400 output.jpg

The discard-mask assigns zero energy to specified areas, ensuring the algorithm removes those seams first before touching the rest of the image.

Step 3: Fine-Tuning Rigidity and Seam Behavior

If the background surrounding your foreground begins to show stretching artifacts, you can adjust the rigidity parameter to make seams behave more uniformly:

magick input.jpg \
  -define liquid-rescale:feature-mask=mask.png \
  -define liquid-rescale:rigidity=1.5 \
  -liquid-rescale 70%x100% output.jpg
  • A higher rigidity value (greater than 0) forces seams to be straighter, reducing wave-like pixel distortions near the edges of protected regions.
  • A lower value allows seams to navigate complex shapes more freely.

Best Practices for Optimal Results

  • Avoid Over-Masking: If the total width of the protected area in the mask is wider than the target output width, the algorithm will be forced to cut through protected pixels. Always ensure the protected subject can physically fit within the targeted dimensions.
  • Feather Mask Edges: Slightly blurring or feathering the edge of your white mask helps create a smooth transition between protected and uncompressed regions, preventing abrupt seam boundaries.
