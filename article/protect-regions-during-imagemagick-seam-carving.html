Protect Regions During ImageMagick Seam Carving

Content-aware resizing, or seam carving, allows you to change an image's aspect ratio by removing or inserting low-energy pixels across the canvas. However, this process can frequently distort important subjects like faces, logos, or defined geometric structures. To prevent this, ImageMagick allows you to supply a protection mask alongside the -liquid-rescale operator, effectively telling the algorithm which pixel paths to avoid during carving.

Prerequisites

To use seam carving in ImageMagick, your installation must be compiled with the Liquid Rescale library (liblqr). You can verify support by checking magick -version or convert -version for the lqr feature flag.

How Protection Masks Work

Seam carving determines which pixels to discard based on an "energy" map, where low-contrast regions have low energy and high-contrast edges have high energy. Pixels along the lowest energy seams are removed first.

By supplying an auxiliary grayscale mask, you manually manipulate these energy levels:

  • White (high energy): Protects the region. The algorithm routes seams around these areas to preserve their shape and detail.
  • Black (low energy / neutral): Treated as standard image areas or targets for prioritized removal.

Step-by-Step Protection Process

1. Create the Protection Mask

Open your source image in any image editor and create a new layer over it. Paint pure white (#FFFFFF) over the subjects you want to protect and fill the rest of the canvas with pure black (#000000). Save this file with the exact same pixel dimensions as your original image (e.g., mask.png).

2. Apply the Mask with -liquid-rescale

In ImageMagick, you composite or feed the mask into the command pipeline so the LQR delegate can read the protection weights:

magick input.jpg mask.png -define lqr:feature-mask=mask.png -liquid-rescale 70%x100% output.jpg

In standard syntax where the mask is piped directly into the operator:

magick input.jpg \( mask.png -alpha off \) -set option:lqr:feature-mask "%[f]" -liquid-rescale 600x400! output.jpg

Best Practices for Clean Results

  • Add Padding Around Critical Features: Paint slightly outside the edges of the object you want to protect. Tight masks may still suffer edge warping if neighboring seams bend sharply.
  • Avoid Over-Constraining: If you protect too large a percentage of the image's width or height, the algorithm will run out of viable seams to carve, forcing seams through protected areas or producing severe compression artifacts in the unmasked background.
  • Combine with Standard Scaling: For dramatic dimension changes, scale the image normally by 10–20% before applying -liquid-rescale to minimize extreme background distortion.
