Remove Bleed-Through with ImageMagick Channel Subtraction

Scanned double-sided documents often suffer from ink bleed-through, where faint text from the reverse side interferes with readability and optical character recognition (OCR). By leveraging ImageMagick’s image arithmetic and color channel subtraction, you can isolate differences in ink intensity and paper diffusion to eliminate ghosting artifacts. This guide demonstrates how to apply channel-based operations in ImageMagick to filter out reverse-side show-through, yielding clean, high-contrast front-page text.

The Principle of Channel Separation for Bleed-Through

When ink bleeds through paper, light passes through the fibrous substrate twice. This causes reverse-side ink to appear diffused, lower in contrast, and slightly shifted in color temperature (often leaning warm or yellowish due to paper tint) compared to the crisp, neutral, high-density ink on the front page.

In a standard RGB color scan, front-page black ink absorbs light equally across all channels (Red, Green, Blue). Bleed-through ink absorbs more blue light while transmitting red and green through the paper matrix. By separating these channels and subtracting the channel where the bleed-through is most prominent from the channel that retains true ink density, the shared bleed-through signal cancels out, leaving only the primary front-facing text.

Step-by-Step Channel Subtraction Process

To remove bleed-through from a single-sided color scan, the Red and Blue channels are separated and combined mathematically.

1. Extract and Subtract Channels

The Red channel generally carries strong paper reflection and bleed-through information, while the Blue channel captures maximum ink absorption. Subtracting the Blue channel from the Red channel isolates the differential values:

magick input.jpg \
  \( -clone 0 -channel R -separate \) \
  \( -clone 0 -channel B -separate \) \
  -compose Subtract -composite \
  difference.png

The resulting difference.png highlights regions where color discrepancy exists, neutralizing faint uniform show-through.

2. Recombining with Luminance and Inverting

To rebuild a readable monochrome document, invert the differential map and multiply or subtract it against a grayscale representation of the original document:

magick input.jpg \
  \( -clone 0 -colorspace Gray \) \
  \( -clone 0 -channel R -separate \) \
  \( -clone 0 -channel B -separate \) \
  -delete 0 \
  \( -clone 1 -clone 2 -compose Subtract -composite -negate \) \
  -delete 1,2 \
  -compose Multiply -composite \
  -auto-level \
  clean_front.png

In this pipeline:

  • The input scan is split into a base grayscale image and individual Red and Blue channels.
  • The Blue channel is subtracted from the Red channel to map spectral differences.
  • The difference mask is negated and multiplied back into the grayscale image to suppress pixels that only register as paper-diffused ink.
  • -auto-level stretches the dynamic range to ensure pure whites and deep blacks.

Two-Sided Alignment Subtraction

If you have scans of both the front page and the back page, channel subtraction becomes even more effective:

  1. Scan both the front and reverse sides.
  2. Horizontally flip the reverse-side image to match the orientation of the bleed-through.
  3. Align the images to line up the bleed-through pattern with the back-page text.
  4. Scale the back-page intensity to match the faintness of the bleed-through, then subtract it directly:
magick front.png \
  \( back.png -flop -colorspace Gray -evaluate multiply 0.25 \) \
  -compose Plus -composite \
  -auto-level \
  corrected_front.png

Adjusting the -evaluate multiply parameter dictates the cancellation strength, tuning the subtraction to match the specific opacity and weight of the paper.
