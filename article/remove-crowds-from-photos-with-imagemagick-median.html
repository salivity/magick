Remove Crowds from Photos with ImageMagick Median

Removing moving pedestrians, vehicles, and crowds from architectural or landscape photos is achievable by blending multiple static frames using a mathematical median filter. By mounting a camera on a sturdy tripod and capturing a sequence of photos over several minutes, moving subjects shift positions while stationary architecture remains unchanged. Using ImageMagick’s evaluate-sequence Median command, the software analyzes the color values of each corresponding pixel across all images in the sequence and selects the middle value, completely erasing non-static elements without the need for manual cloning or masking.

Step 1: Capture the Source Images

To use this technique effectively, your input photos must meet specific criteria:

  • Use a Tripod: The background must remain perfectly locked in place. Any camera movement will cause blur in the final output.
  • Manual Camera Settings: Lock exposure, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, and white balance so lighting stays uniform across frames.
  • Timing: Capture between 10 to 30 photos at regular intervals (e.g., one shot every 5 to 15 seconds). Ensure that every section of the scene is clear of people in at least half of the photos.

Step 2: Organize Your Image Files

Place all source photos into a dedicated working directory and ensure they are sorted in chronological order. Standardize their formats (e.g., .jpg or .png).

mkdir scene_project
cd scene_project
# Move your files here, e.g., input_01.jpg, input_02.jpg, ...

Step 3: Run the ImageMagick Command

ImageMagick provides the -evaluate-sequence operator, which applies an arithmetic operation across an entire list of loaded images to produce a single composite.

For ImageMagick 7, use the magick command:

magick input_*.jpg -evaluate-sequence median output_clean.jpg

For ImageMagick 6, use the convert command:

convert input_*.jpg -evaluate-sequence median output_clean.jpg

How the Median Algorithm Works

The median filter evaluates the color channels of a specific pixel coordinate \((x, y)\) across all provided frames and sorts them numerically:

  • Static background pixels appear in almost every image, meaning their pixel values dominate the dataset and fall directly in the median position.
  • Moving objects only appear on that specific pixel coordinate in a few frames. Their differing color values become extreme outliers (either too light or too dark compared to the background).
  • The median calculation rejects these statistical outliers, reconstructing the hidden background beneath the crowd.

Handling Slight Camera Shifts

If hand-held shots or slight wind-induced tripod jitter occurred during capture, align the frames before running the median filter using align_image_stack (part of the Hugin toolset):

align_image_stack -a aligned_ input_*.jpg
magick aligned_*.tif -evaluate-sequence median output_clean.jpg

Tips for Best Results

  • More frames equal cleaner results: A minimum of 9 to 15 frames is recommended. An odd number of images is ideal, as it allows ImageMagick to select an exact middle value rather than averaging two central values.
  • Loitering crowds: If someone stands completely still throughout the entire sequence, the algorithm cannot remove them. Wait for static individuals to move before finishing your photo sequence.
