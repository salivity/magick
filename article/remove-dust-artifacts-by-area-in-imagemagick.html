Remove Dust Artifacts by Area in ImageMagick

Removing dust artifacts, speckles, and small blemishes from an image can be automated in ImageMagick by filtering connected pixel components based on their area. By utilizing the -connected-components operator alongside the area-threshold definition, you can identify isolated clusters of pixels below a specific size threshold and merge them into the surrounding background color without degrading the larger structural elements of the image.

Understanding Connected Components Labeling

ImageMagick provides a Connected Components Labeling (CCL) algorithm via the -connected-components operator. It groups adjacent pixels of similar colors into distinct objects (components) and calculates metrics such as bounding box, centroid, and total pixel area.

To eliminate dust or "salt-and-pepper" noise, you can configure the operator to ignore or replace any component whose total area falls below a defined pixel count.

The Basic Dust Removal Command

For grayscale, binary, or high-contrast scanned documents, you can remove small specs in a single pass using the following command structure:

magick input.png \
  -define connected-components:area-threshold=10 \
  -define connected-components:mean-color=true \
  -connected-components 8 \
  output.png

Parameter Breakdown

  • -define connected-components:area-threshold=10: Specifies the maximum size (in pixels) of an artifact to remove. Any connected region containing fewer than 10 pixels will be merged into the neighboring region.
  • -define connected-components:mean-color=true: Replaces the filtered components with the average color of the surrounding area rather than leaving empty holes.
  • -connected-components 8: Runs the analysis considering 8-connectivity (diagonal and orthogonal neighbors). You can also use 4 for orthogonal-only connectivity.

Removing Dust from Scanned Documents and Line Art

When dealing with scanned black-and-white documents or drawings, dust specs often appear as tiny black dots on a white background. To clean these effectively:

magick scan.png \
  -threshold 80% \
  -define connected-components:area-threshold=15 \
  -define connected-components:mean-color=true \
  -connected-components 8 \
  cleaned_scan.png
  1. -threshold 80% standardizes the paper texture to pure white and the text/dust to pure black.
  2. The area-threshold=15 removes any isolated mark smaller than 15 pixels while preserving valid punctuation marks like periods or commas (adjust the threshold value to ensure punctuation is not erased).

Removing Dust from Continuous-Tone and Color Photos

For photographic images where pixel values vary smoothly, running CCL directly on the color data may not work directly because adjacent pixels do not share identical values. Instead, extract a mask of high-frequency defects, filter by area, and blend the result.

1. Generate an Artifact Mask

Identify potential dust specs by comparing the original image with a blurred or median-filtered version:

magick photo.jpg \
  \( +clone -statistic Median 3x3 \) \
  -compose Difference -composite \
  -threshold 10% \
  -define connected-components:area-threshold=20 \
  -define connected-components:mean-color=false \
  -connected-components 8 \
  dust_mask.png

2. Apply the Mask to Clean the Original Image

Use the generated dust_mask.png to replace defective areas on the original photo with pixels from a median-filtered base:

magick photo.jpg \
  \( +clone -statistic Median 5x5 \) \
  dust_mask.png \
  -composite \
  repaired_photo.jpg

Tuning Area Thresholds

  • Start Small: Begin with an area-threshold of 5 to 10 pixels to avoid accidentally removing genuine image details like stars in night skies, catchlights in eyes, or thin line intersections.
  • Inspect Component Data: To see a text report of all detected components and their pixel sizes prior to filtering, output the component analysis directly to the terminal:
magick input.png -connected-components 8 -write-mask mask.png null:
