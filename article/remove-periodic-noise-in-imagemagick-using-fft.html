Remove Periodic Noise in ImageMagick Using FFT

Periodic pattern noise, such as halftones, scan lines, or herringbone interference, creates repetitive artifacts across an image that are difficult to remove in the spatial domain without blurring the entire picture. By converting an image into the frequency domain using the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) in ImageMagick, you can isolate these periodic frequencies as distinct bright spikes in the magnitude spectrum. Masking out these anomalous spikes with a notch filter and reconstructing the image with the Inverse Fast Fourier Transform (IFT) completely eliminates the noise pattern while preserving sharp edges and structural details.

Prerequisites

To perform frequency domain editing, ImageMagick must be compiled with the FFTW (Fastest Fourier Transform in the West) delegate and High Dynamic Range Imaging (HDRI) enabled to ensure precision in floating-point calculations.

Step 1: Decompose the Image Using FFT

Transform your source image into its frequency domain representation. This splits the image into two components: the magnitude spectrum (index 0) and the phase spectrum (index 1).

magick input.png -fft spectrum_%d.png
  • spectrum_0.png: The magnitude spectrum, showing the distribution of spatial frequencies.
  • spectrum_1.png: The phase spectrum, containing the spatial location data of the image features.

Step 2: Identify the Noise Frequencies

Open spectrum_0.png in an image viewer. The center represents low frequencies (overall brightness and smooth gradients), while higher frequencies radiate outward.

Periodic noise manifests as sharp, bright, symmetrical dots or star-like spikes located away from the center.

Step 3: Create a Notch Mask

Create a mask to blot out the identified noise spikes without disturbing the bright center point (the DC component) or the broader frequency spectrum:

  1. Create a pure white canvas matching the dimensions of the magnitude image.
  2. Paint small black circles or soft black brushes directly over the bright noise spikes.
  3. Save this mask image as notch_mask.png.

Note: Ensure the black spots cover both symmetrical pairs of each noise frequency point.

Step 4: Apply the Mask to the Magnitude

Multiply the original magnitude spectrum by the notch mask to zero out the frequency components responsible for the periodic pattern:

magick spectrum_0.png notch_mask.png -compose Multiply -composite magnitude_filtered.png

Step 5: Reconstruct the Cleaned Image with IFT

Pass the filtered magnitude spectrum and the original, unmodified phase spectrum into the inverse transform to recreate the spatial image:

magick magnitude_filtered.png spectrum_1.png -ift output_cleaned.png

Consolidated One-Line Processing

If you already have a prepared notch mask for a batch of images sharing the same pattern noise dimensions and frequencies, you can run the entire pipeline in a single command:

magick input.png -fft \( -clone 0 notch_mask.png -compose Multiply -composite \) -swap 0,2 +delete -ift output_cleaned.png
