Remove Salt-and-Pepper Noise in ImageMagick

This guide explains how to effectively eliminate salt-and-pepper noise from images using ImageMagick's built-in median filter. You will learn the exact command-line syntax for both modern ImageMagick v7 and legacy ImageMagick v6, understand how the median filter functions to replace noisy outlier pixels without excessively blurring edges, and discover how to adjust the filter radius for optimal results.

The Median Filter Command

Salt-and-pepper noise manifests as randomly scattered white and black pixels across an image. Because the median filter replaces each pixel with the median value of its surrounding neighborhood, it easily discards these extreme high and low outlier values while preserving image edges better than standard blur or mean filters.

ImageMagick v7 (Current)

In ImageMagick 7, the preferred method is the -statistic median operator using a defined geometry (width x height) for the neighborhood kernel:

magick input.png -statistic median 3x3 output.png

ImageMagick v6 (Legacy)

In ImageMagick 6, you use the convert tool combined with either the -statistic operator or the shorthand -median option:

convert input.png -median 1 output.png

Choosing the Correct Filter Size

The dimensions specified for the median filter determine the size of the pixel neighborhood analyzed:

  • 3x3 (or radius 1 to 1.5): Ideal for fine, single-pixel salt-and-pepper noise. It removes the artifacts while retaining maximum sharpness in image details.
  • 5x5 (or radius 2): Best for heavier noise where corrupted pixels cluster into small groups.
  • Larger Kernels (7x7 and above): Useful only for very large artifacts or high-resolution images. Applying a large kernel to standard-resolution images creates a heavy posterized or watercolor-like smoothing effect.

Advanced Usage: Selective Channel Filtering

By default, the median filter processes all color channels simultaneously. If your noise is confined to luminance or a specific color channel, you can restrict the filter using the -channel flag:

magick input.png -channel RGB -statistic median 3x3 +channel output.png

For general cleanup, a simple 3x3 median statistic applied across the image provides the fastest and cleanest balance between noise reduction and edge retention.
