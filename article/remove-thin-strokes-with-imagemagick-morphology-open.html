Remove Thin Strokes with ImageMagick Morphology Open

The ImageMagick morphology Open operator eliminates thin pen strokes, scratches, and fine noise from scanned documents while leaving larger text bodies intact. It accomplishes this through a two-stage mathematical morphology process: an erosion followed immediately by a dilation, using a user-defined structuring element (kernel). Because features narrower than the kernel disappear entirely during the erosion phase, only thicker components survive to be restored by the dilation phase.

The Two-Stage Mechanism

Mathematical morphology treats pixel neighborhoods based on geometric shapes defined by a kernel (such as Disk, Octagon, or Diamond). The Open operator performs two distinct steps in sequence using the exact same kernel:

  1. Erosion (Erode): The kernel moves across the image, paring away pixels from the boundaries of all foreground shapes (typically black text on a white background, or white on black depending on the color configuration).

    • Thin strokes: If a pen stroke or hairline feature has a width smaller than the diameter of the structuring element, it is eroded away completely.
    • Larger text bodies: Characters with wide stroke widths lose perimeter pixels, but their inner cores remain intact.

  2. Dilation (Dilate): The kernel expands the surviving foreground pixels back outward by the same distance they were eroded.

    • Thin strokes: Because the fine strokes were completely erased during the erosion step, there are no surviving pixels left to dilate. Consequently, they cannot regenerate.
    • Larger text bodies: The surviving cores expand outward, restoring the main body of the characters close to their original shape, size, and boundary.

Kernel Selection and Sizing

The success of the Open operator depends entirely on choosing the right kernel size relative to stroke thickness.

To remove thin pen strokes while keeping larger text bodies readable:

  • Radius: The radius of the kernel must be larger than half the width of the unwanted thin strokes, but smaller than half the width of the thinnest part of the text you wish to preserve.
  • Shape: A rounded shape such as Disk or Octagon provides uniform filtering across all angles, ensuring thin strokes are removed evenly regardless of whether they are horizontal, vertical, or diagonal.

An example ImageMagick command syntax is:

magick input.png -morphology Open Disk:1.5 output.png

By calibrating the radius value, fine lines disappear entirely during erosion, leaving only the primary structural text behind to be restored during dilation.
