Replace Color with Fuzz Tolerance in ImageMagick

This guide explains how to replace a specific color and its nearby color variations in an image using ImageMagick's fuzz tolerance functionality. By combining the -fuzz, -fill, and -opaque operators, you can target a specific shade and automatically substitute all closely matching pixels with a new target color or transparency.

The Basic Command

To replace a color within a specific tolerance, use the following syntax:

ImageMagick v7:

magick input.png -fuzz 15% -fill "#00FF00" -opaque "#FF0000" output.png

ImageMagick v6:

convert input.png -fuzz 15% -fill "#00FF00" -opaque "#FF0000" output.png

How the Parameters Work

  • -fuzz <distance>: Sets the tolerance range. It is most commonly expressed as a percentage (e.g., 10%, 20%). A value of 0% requires an exact color match, while higher values expand the range to include lighter, darker, or slightly different shades of the target color.
  • -fill <new_color>: Defines the replacement color. You can use standard color names (like blue or none), hex codes (like #1A2B3C), or RGB values (like rgb(255,0,0)).
  • -opaque <old_color>: Identifies the base color you want to replace. Any pixel within the defined -fuzz distance of this color will change to the -fill color.

Common Use Cases

1. Replacing a Named Color with Another

To replace variations of red with solid blue:

magick input.jpg -fuzz 12% -fill blue -opaque red output.jpg

2. Making a Background Color Transparent

To turn an off-white or solid background into a transparent alpha channel:

magick input.jpg -fuzz 10% -fill none -opaque white output.png

Note: Ensure your output format supports transparency (such as PNG or WebP).

3. Replacing Hex Colors

To replace a specific hex color shade with another:

magick input.png -fuzz 8% -fill "#336699" -opaque "#CCCCCC" output.png

Finding the Right Fuzz Value

  • Start Low: Begin with a low tolerance, such as 5% to 10%, to prevent overwriting colors in adjacent areas that share similar tones.
  • Gradually Increase: If artifacts or original border pixels remain around the targeted area, increase the percentage in increments of 2% to 5% until the selection covers the entire desired area cleanly.
