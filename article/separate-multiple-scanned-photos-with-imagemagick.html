Separate Multiple Scanned Photos with ImageMagick

Scanning multiple photographic prints in a single pass on a flatbed scanner saves time, but separating them into individual image files manually can be tedious. This guide explains how to automate the detection, cropping, and extraction of multiple photos from a single scanned image using ImageMagick command-line tools.

Requirements for Best Results

For automated separation to work reliably, your scan must meet a few basic conditions:

  • Distinct Background: The scanner lid or background must be a solid, uniform color (typically solid white or black) that contrasts with the photo borders.
  • No Overlap: Photos must not touch or overlap each other on the scanner glass.
  • Spacing: Leave at least a half-inch (1–2 cm) gap between prints and scanner edges.

Method 1: Using Native ImageMagick 7 Connected Components

ImageMagick 7 includes a built-in -connected-components operator that identifies distinct objects on a uniform background and outputs their bounding boxes.

Step 1: Detect and Extract Bounding Boxes

Run the following command to identify distinct objects, filter out noise, and output the bounding box coordinates for each photo:

magick scan.jpg \
  -fuzz 15% \
  -fill white -draw "color 0,0 floodfill" \
  -threshold 95% -negate \
  -define connected-components:verbose=true \
  -define connected-components:area-threshold=50000 \
  -connected-components output_mask.png \
  null:
  • -fuzz 15%: Tolerates minor lighting and color variations in the background.
  • -fill white -draw "color 0,0 floodfill": Identifies the outer background starting from the top-left corner.
  • -threshold 95% -negate: Converts the image to a binary mask where the photos are white and the background is black.
  • area-threshold=50000: Ignores small specks, dust, and artifacts smaller than 50,000 pixels.

The command outputs a list of bounding boxes in the terminal in standard geometry format (e.g., 1200x800+150+200).

Step 2: Crop the Images Automatically

You can parse the generated bounding coordinates to extract each individual photo using a simple shell loop:

magick scan.jpg \
  -fuzz 15% \
  -fill white -draw "color 0,0 floodfill" \
  -threshold 95% -negate \
  -define connected-components:verbose=true \
  -define connected-components:area-threshold=50000 \
  -connected-components /dev/null null: | \
  awk '/^[ ]*[0-9]+:/ && !/0,0/ {print $2}' | while read -r geom; do
    magick scan.jpg -crop "$geom" +repage "photo_${geom}.jpg"
done

This loops through each detected coordinate and saves each extracted photo into a new file named with its geometry offset.

Method 2: Deskewing and Trimming Single Images

If your scan contains photos aligned in distinct rows or if you want to refine extracted images, you can apply automatic deskewing and trimming to straighten angled photos and remove border margins:

magick photo_extracted.jpg -deskew 40% -fuzz 10% -trim +repage photo_final.jpg
  • -deskew 40%: Detects the rotation angle up to 40 degrees and straightens the photo.
  • -fuzz 10% -trim: Trims away remaining uniform background edges.
  • +repage: Resets the virtual canvas geometry to match the new image dimensions.
