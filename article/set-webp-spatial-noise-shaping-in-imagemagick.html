Set WebP Spatial Noise Shaping in ImageMagick

This guide explains how to configure WebP spatial noise shaping (SNS) in ImageMagick to optimize image compression and perceptual quality. Spatial noise shaping controls how encoding distortion is distributed across different areas of an image, balancing noise visibility between flat regions and complex textures. By applying the specific -define option during conversion, you can finely adjust this algorithm to suit your target visual fidelity and file size requirements.

Syntax

In ImageMagick, the syntax for setting WebP spatial noise shaping strength uses the -define command-line argument:

-define webp:sns-strength=<value>

Replace <value> with an integer ranging from 0 to 100.

  • 0: Disables spatial noise shaping entirely.
  • 100: Applies maximum spatial noise shaping strength.
  • Default: Libwebp sets the default value to 50 (or 80 in certain presets).

Command Examples

Modern ImageMagick (v7+)

magick input.jpg -define webp:sns-strength=80 output.webp

Legacy ImageMagick (v6)

convert input.jpg -define webp:sns-strength=80 output.webp

Combining with Quality Settings

Spatial noise shaping is typically used alongside the standard WebP quality setting:

magick input.png -quality 75 -define webp:sns-strength=60 output.webp

How It Works

Spatial noise shaping analyzes macroblocks in the source image to identify areas with high complexity (textures, foliage, grain) versus areas that are flat or smooth (skies, gradients).

  • Higher values (e.g., 70–100): Allow more compression artifacts and noise into complex, textured regions where the human eye cannot easily detect them, reserving more bits to keep smooth areas clean and free of banding.
  • Lower values (e.g., 0–30): Apply quantization more uniformly across the entire image, which may be preferable for graphical content, line art, or screenshots where uniform sharpness is prioritized over hiding artifacts in textures.
