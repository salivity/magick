Sharpen Text and Remove Paper Grain in ImageMagick

This article provides the exact ImageMagick syntax to enhance scanned document readability by sharpening text while simultaneously eliminating background paper grain, texture, and uneven lighting. By combining grayscale conversion, background division normalization, dynamic level adjustments, and selective unsharp masking, you can produce crisp, high-contrast text on a clean, solid background.

The Recommended ImageMagick Syntax

For ImageMagick v7, use the following single-line command:

magick input.jpg -colorspace Gray \( +clone -blur 0x25 \) -compose Divide -composite -level 15%,85% -unsharp 0x1.5+1.5+0.05 output.png

If you are using ImageMagick v6, replace magick with convert:

convert input.jpg -colorspace Gray \( +clone -blur 0x25 \) -compose Divide -composite -level 15%,85% -unsharp 0x1.5+1.5+0.05 output.png

How the Command Works

Each parameter targets a specific aspect of the document degradation:

  1. -colorspace Gray
    Removes color artifacts, chromatic aberration, and colored paper tints, reducing the image to single-channel intensity data.

  2. \( +clone -blur 0x25 \) -compose Divide -composite
    This creates a heavily blurred duplicate of the image to map low-frequency background brightness and paper grain, then divides the original image by this blurred background. This eliminates shadows, paper texture, and uneven lighting while preserving the high-frequency dark text edges.

  3. -level 15%,85%
    Pushes the light gray remnants of paper grain to pure white (values above 85%) and deepens the dark gray ink to pure black (values below 15%). Adjust these percentage values based on the intensity of your original scan.

  4. -unsharp 0x1.5+1.5+0.05
    Applies an unsharp mask with a radius/sigma of 0x1.5, an amplification amount of 1.5, and a threshold of 0.05. The small threshold ensures that tiny variations in the background are ignored while edge contrast on text strokes is significantly enhanced.

Alternative for High-Speed Binarization (Pure Black and White)

If your goal is a lightweight, pure black-and-white (1-bit) document for OCR, use Local Adaptive Thresholding (-lat):

magick input.jpg -colorspace Gray -negate -lat 25x25+10% -negate -unsharp 0x1+1.0+0.05 output.png
  • -lat 25x25+10% evaluates a 25x25 pixel window around each pixel and thresholds it if the pixel is darker than 10% below the local average. This completely removes the background grain while preserving fine text strokes.
