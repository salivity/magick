Simulate Cross-Processed Film in ImageMagick

This guide explains how to simulate the distinctive look of cross-processed film photography using color curves in ImageMagick. Cross-processing—traditionally achieved by developing slide film in chemicals intended for color negative film (E-6 in C-41)—creates high contrast, exaggerated saturation, and dramatic color shifts like cyan/blue shadows and yellow/green highlights. By applying specific non-linear curves to individual RGB color channels, you can reproduce this analog effect completely from the command line.

Understanding the Cross-Process Curve Logic

To achieve an authentic cross-processed look, each color channel requires specific adjustments:

  • Red Channel (S-Curve): Increases highlight warmth while deepening shadows.
  • Green Channel (Slight Boost / S-Curve): Enhances midtone brightness and contrast to push greens and magentas.
  • Blue Channel (Inverted S-Curve / Lifted Shadows): Raises the black point to inject blue into the dark tones and drops the white point to push yellow into the highlights.

Implementing with the ImageMagick Curves Operator

ImageMagick provides the -curves operator to manipulate tone curves using spline interpolation with coordinates mapped from 0.0 (black) to 1.0 (white).

The Complete Command

Run the following command in ImageMagick 7:

magick input.jpg \
  -channel R -curves Spline "0.0,0.0 0.3,0.15 0.7,0.85 1.0,1.0" +channel \
  -channel G -curves Spline "0.0,0.0 0.3,0.25 0.7,0.8 1.0,1.0" +channel \
  -channel B -curves Spline "0.0,0.15 0.4,0.3 0.6,0.7 1.0,0.85" +channel \
  -brightness-contrast 0x10 \
  output_cross_processed.jpg

(For ImageMagick 6, replace magick with convert.)

Parameter Breakdown

  • -channel R -curves Spline "0.0,0.0 0.3,0.15 0.7,0.85 1.0,1.0": Compresses dark reds while boosting bright reds to establish higher chromatic contrast.
  • -channel G -curves Spline "0.0,0.0 0.3,0.25 0.7,0.8 1.0,1.0": Adds a subtle S-curve to maintain vibrancy in midtones without overwhelming the palette.
  • -channel B -curves Spline "0.0,0.15 0.4,0.3 0.6,0.7 1.0,0.85": Lifts the bottom coordinate to 0.15 to tint the shadows blue, and lowers the top coordinate to 0.85 to tint the highlights yellow.
  • +channel: Resets the active channel selection back to all channels before applying the next operation.
  • -brightness-contrast 0x10: Adds a final contrast boost across the entire image to emulate the characteristic harsh roll-off of cross-processed film stock.

Alternative: Using Photoshop ACV Curve Files

If you have an existing Photoshop curve preset (.acv file) tailored for cross-processing, ImageMagick can apply it directly without manual coordinate entry:

magick input.jpg -curves cross_process.acv output.jpg

Adding Optional Grain and Vignetting

To finish the analog aesthetic, combine the color transformation with synthetic film grain and edge falloff:

magick input.jpg \
  -channel R -curves Spline "0.0,0.0 0.3,0.15 0.7,0.85 1.0,1.0" +channel \
  -channel G -curves Spline "0.0,0.0 0.3,0.25 0.7,0.8 1.0,1.0" +channel \
  -channel B -curves Spline "0.0,0.15 0.4,0.3 0.6,0.7 1.0,0.85" +channel \
  \( +clone -attenuate 0.4 +noise Gaussian \) -compose Overlay -composite \
  \( +clone -background black -vignette 0x20 \) \
  output_analog.jpg
