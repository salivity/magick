Simulate Cyanotype Blueprint Prints in ImageMagick

This guide demonstrates how to transform any digital photograph into a vintage cyanotype blueprint print using ImageMagick. You will learn the exact command-line syntax required to strip color, optimize tonal contrast, and apply the signature Prussian blue duotone mapping characteristic of historic cyanotype photography.

The Basic Cyanotype Command

To replicate a traditional cyanotype, the image must be desaturated to grayscale, balanced for contrast, and remapped to a tonal range spanning from deep Prussian blue in the shadows to a faint, paper-tinted white in the highlights.

For ImageMagick v7, execute the following command in your terminal:

magick input.jpg -colorspace Gray -auto-level +level-colors "#0e2d4a","#f0f6fa" cyanotype_output.jpg

For ImageMagick v6, replace magick with convert:

convert input.jpg -colorspace Gray -auto-level +level-colors "#0e2d4a","#f0f6fa" cyanotype_output.jpg

Command Breakdown

  • -colorspace Gray: Strips all existing color data from the input image, converting it to a neutral 8-bit or 16-bit grayscale foundation.
  • -auto-level: Stretches the tonal range across the full spectrum from pure black to pure white. Cyanotypes rely heavily on pronounced midtones and well-defined highlights.
  • +level-colors "#0e2d4a","#f0f6fa": Maps the darkest values (black) to a rich Prussian blue (#0e2d4a) and the brightest values (white) to a subtle, off-white cyan paper tone (#f0f6fa).

Customizing the Effect

1. High-Contrast Blueprint Style

If you want a sharper, architectural blueprint aesthetic with punchy whites and deep ink-blue tones, apply a sigmoidal contrast adjustment:

magick input.jpg -colorspace Gray -auto-level -sigmoidal-contrast 4,50% +level-colors "#081d33","#ffffff" blueprint_style.jpg

2. Aged Cyanotype with Paper Texture Tint

Historical prints often feature slightly warm or faded highlights due to the aging of the paper stock. Adjust the highlight hex code toward a warm cream while keeping the shadows dark:

magick input.jpg -colorspace Gray -auto-level +level-colors "#12304d","#faf5eb" aged_cyanotype.jpg

3. Adding Film Grain

Analog blueprint prints naturally feature slight emulsion grain. You can simulate this texture by introducing a small amount of monochromatic noise:

magick input.jpg -colorspace Gray -auto-level +level-colors "#0e2d4a","#f0f6fa" -attenuate 0.3 +noise Gaussian cyanotype_grained.jpg
