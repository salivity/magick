Simulate Print Dot Gain in ImageMagick

Simulating printing press dot gain allows designers and prepress technicians to accurately preview how physical ink spreads into paper fibers on an offset press. This guide demonstrates how to recreate this effect in ImageMagick using two primary methods: tonal curve manipulation for macroscopic darkening, and half-tone morphological filtering for microscopic ink spread.

Understanding Dot Gain

Dot gain, or tonal value increase (TVI), occurs when ink drops expand on porous paper, making midtones significantly darker than their digital values while leaving the deepest shadows and brightest highlights relatively unaffected.

Method 1: Tonal Shift via Gamma Adjustment

The fastest way to simulate overall dot gain across an entire image is by adjusting the midtone curve. A typical commercial press exhibits a 15% to 25% dot gain, which can be approximated by applying an inverse gamma shift to midtone values.

To simulate standard sheet-fed offset dot gain:

magick input.png -gamma 0.85 output_dotgain.png

For high-gain environments like newsprint (often exceeding 30% dot gain), apply a stronger curve:

magick input.png -gamma 0.70 newsprint_dotgain.png

Method 2: Color Space and ICC Profiles

For standard-compliant press simulation, apply ICC profiles that contain calibrated dot gain curves (such as GRACoL, SWOP, or ISOcoated). This method models the gain accurately across individual CMYK ink channels.

magick input.tif -profile sRGB.icc -profile USWebCoatedSWOP.icc output_cmyk.tif

To view the simulated output on an RGB screen, convert it back using an RGB display profile while preserving the press gamut clipping:

magick input.tif -profile sRGB.icc -profile USWebCoatedSWOP.icc -profile sRGB.icc proof_preview.png

Method 3: Microscopic Mechanical Dot Gain

To simulate the physical enlargement of individual raster dots at a microscopic level, combine ordered dithering with morphological dilation.

  1. Convert the image to grayscale or process individual CMYK channels.
  2. Apply an ordered halftone dither.
  3. Use a morphological filter to expand the printed dots outward.
  4. Slightly blur the edges to emulate ink bleeding into the paper substrate.
magick input.png -colorspace Gray \
  -ordered-dither h8x8a \
  -morphology Dilate Diamond:1 \
  -gaussian-blur 0x0.5 \
  output_halftone_gain.png

For CMYK separations, extract channels, apply the dither and dilation individually, and recombine them to simulate color misalignment and overlapping dot spread.
