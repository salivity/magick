Simulate Tritanopia in ImageMagick

This article provides the exact color transformation matrix and command-line syntax needed to simulate tritanopia (blue-yellow color blindness) using ImageMagick. By applying a 3x3 color matrix to digital images, designers and developers can evaluate accessibility and understand how visual content appears to individuals lacking short-wavelength (S-cone) color receptors.

The Tritanopia Transformation Matrix

To simulate tritanopia accurately, visual research models (such as those by Brettel, Vienot, and Mollon) re-map RGB channels so that blue wavelengths are approximated via red and green luminance.

A widely accepted 3x3 matrix for simulating complete tritanopia is:

\[ \begin{bmatrix} 0.95 & 0.05 & 0.00 \\ 0.00 & 0.43333 & 0.56667 \\ 0.00 & 0.475 & 0.525 \end{bmatrix} \]

In this matrix:

  • Red output (Row 1): Derived from 95% Red and 5% Green.
  • Green output (Row 2): Derived from 43.333% Green and 56.667% Blue.
  • Blue output (Row 3): Derived from 47.5% Green and 52.5% Blue.

ImageMagick Command Syntax

ImageMagick provides the -color-matrix operator to apply linear transformations to image channels. The matrix values are passed as a flattened sequence of row-major numbers.

ImageMagick 7 (Recommended)

magick input.jpg -color-matrix "0.95 0.05 0.00 0.00 0.43333 0.56667 0.00 0.475 0.525" output_tritanopia.jpg

ImageMagick 6 (Legacy)

convert input.jpg -color-matrix "0.95 0.05 0.00 0.00 0.43333 0.56667 0.00 0.475 0.525" output_tritanopia.jpg

Alternative Matrix (Machado Model)

For physiologically calibrated simulations based on the Machado et al. model (100% severity), use the following matrix values:

magick input.jpg -color-matrix "0.972 0.028 0.00 0.00 0.735 0.265 0.00 0.183 0.817" output_tritanopia_machado.jpg

Applying either matrix modifies the image instantly without requiring external lookup tables or plugins, making it ideal for automated accessibility testing scripts.
