Twist Image Center with Borders Fixed in ImageMagick

Twisting the center of an image while keeping its outer boundaries stationary is achieved in ImageMagick using the native -swirl operator. This article explains how the -swirl argument applies a radial deformation that rotates pixels at the center of an image and gradually decreases the rotation angle toward the outer edges, leaving the borders completely untouched.

The Basic Swirl Command

ImageMagick provides the -swirl option, which rotates the pixels around the center of the image. The strength of the twist is determined by an angle parameter specified in degrees. Because the mathematical transformation attenuates to zero as it approaches the image boundary, the borders of the image remain fixed.

ImageMagick 7 Syntax

magick input.jpg -swirl 180 output.jpg

ImageMagick 6 Syntax

convert input.jpg -swirl 180 output.jpg

Controlling Twist Direction and Intensity

The parameter passed to -swirl dictates both the direction and the tightness of the twist:

  • Clockwise Twist: Use a positive degree value (e.g., -swirl 360 creates a full single rotation at the center).
  • Counter-Clockwise Twist: Use a negative degree value (e.g., -swirl -360).
  • Subtle Adjustments: Smaller values like 45 or 90 produce a gentle distortion.
  • Extreme Distortion: Large values such as 720 or 1080 create an intense vortex effect in the middle while maintaining the anchored edges.

Constraining the Twist to a Specific Central Area

If you need the distortion confined strictly to an arbitrary central radius rather than letting it taper off to the full image frame, you can crop the target area, apply the swirl, and composite it back onto the original image.

magick input.jpg \
  \( +clone -crop 400x400+100+100 -swirl 180 \) \
  -geometry +100+100 -composite output.jpg

In this command:

  1. +clone -crop 400x400+100+100 defines the specific central region to be modified.
  2. -swirl 180 twists that cropped section.
  3. -geometry +100+100 -composite layers the twisted region back onto the original canvas, ensuring the surrounding canvas and borders remain completely unaltered.
