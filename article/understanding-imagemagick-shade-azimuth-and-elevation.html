Understanding ImageMagick Shade Azimuth and Elevation

The ImageMagick -shade operator creates a 3D embossed or relief effect by simulating a distant directional light source cast across an image's brightness surface. This lighting is controlled entirely by two spherical coordinate parameters: azimuth and elevation, formatted as -shade <azimuth>x<elevation>. Together, these values position an infinite parallel light source in 3D space, determining the direction and intensity of highlights and shadows across the image.

Azimuth: Horizontal Light Direction

Azimuth configures the horizontal direction of the light source within the 2D plane of the image (the X-Y plane). It is measured in degrees, typically ranging from 0° to 360°, rotating counterclockwise from the positive X-axis:

  • 0° (East / Right): The light originates from the right edge of the image, casting shadows to the left.
  • 90° (North / Top): The light originates from the top edge, casting shadows downward.
  • 180° (West / Left): The light originates from the left edge, casting shadows to the right.
  • 270° (South / Bottom): The light originates from the bottom edge, casting shadows upward.

Intermediate values position the light at diagonal angles. For example, an azimuth of 135° places the light in the top-left quadrant (North-West), which is a common convention in user interface design and cartography.

Elevation: Vertical Light Angle

Elevation defines the vertical angle of the light source relative to the image surface (the Z-axis), extending from the horizon up to the zenith. It is measured in degrees, ranging from 0° to 90°:

  • 0° (Surface / Horizon): The light shines completely parallel to the image surface. This grazing angle exaggerates every surface variation, resulting in extreme contrast and long, harsh shadows.
  • 45° (Mid-Angle): A balanced position providing natural highlights and soft, legible shading suitable for general relief rendering.
  • 90° (Overhead / Zenith): The light source is positioned directly above the image, perpendicular to the surface. At this angle, horizontal directional shading disappears, leaving only flat, uniform illumination.

How the Parameters Work Together

ImageMagick treats the grayscale intensity of the input image as a height map, calculating surface normal vectors for each pixel based on localized brightness gradients. The -shade operator then calculates the dot product between the calculated surface normal and the 3D directional light vector established by the azimuth and elevation settings.

A standard configuration such as -shade 120x45 instructs ImageMagick to shine light from the upper-left (120° azimuth) at a 45° angle above the image plane (45° elevation), generating intuitive depth and defined surface contours.
