Understanding Plus Sign Offset in ImageMagick Crop

In ImageMagick, the -crop geometry argument defines both the dimensions of the cropped area and its placement on the source image using coordinates. This article provides a clear breakdown of what the plus sign (+) offset syntax represents, how it dictates coordinate placement, and how it interacts with reference origins and gravity settings.

The Crop Geometry Syntax

ImageMagick specifies crop operations using the following geometry format:

[width]x[height]+[x_offset]+[y_offset]

In this syntax, width and height designate the size of the output area, while the two offset values dictate where the cropping rectangle begins.

The Meaning of the Plus Sign (+)

The plus sign represents a positive displacement along the Cartesian coordinate grid of the image canvas:

  • +x (Horizontal Offset): Specifies the number of pixels to shift to the right from the reference edge.
  • +y (Vertical Offset): Specifies the number of pixels to shift downward from the reference edge.

By default, ImageMagick sets the origin point (0,0) at the top-left corner of the image. Therefore, an offset formatted as +50+100 instructs ImageMagick to move 50 pixels to the right of the left edge and 100 pixels down from the top edge before starting the crop.

Example

magick input.jpg -crop 400x300+50+100 output.jpg

In this command:

  • 400x300 extracts a box measuring 400 pixels wide and 300 pixels high.
  • +50 places the left boundary of the box 50 pixels in from the left edge.
  • +100 places the top boundary of the box 100 pixels down from the top edge.

Interaction with the -gravity Setting

When the -gravity operator is used, the point of origin shifts away from the default top-left corner, changing the directional behavior of the plus sign offset:

  • -gravity Center -crop 200x200+10+20: The reference point becomes the center of the image. The +10 shifts the crop 10 pixels right of center, and +20 shifts it 20 pixels down from center.
  • -gravity SouthEast -crop 200x200+10+20: The reference point is the bottom-right corner. Here, positive offsets move inward toward the image frame—shifting the crop 10 pixels left from the right edge and 20 pixels up from the bottom edge.

Contrast with the Minus Sign (-)

While the plus sign explicitly designates a positive, inward shift from the reference boundary, a minus sign (-) indicates a negative offset. A negative offset typically positions the crop region outside the visible canvas or counts inward from the opposite boundary, depending on configuration and canvas geometry. Using the plus sign ensures standard, predictable positioning within the image boundaries.
