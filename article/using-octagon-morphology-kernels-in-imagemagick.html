Using Octagon Morphology Kernels in ImageMagick

This article provides a clear guide on utilizing the Octagon morphology kernel in ImageMagick to perform morphological transformations with rounded structural elements. You will learn the syntax of the Octagon kernel, understand how it approximates circular operations to avoid harsh rectilinear artifacts, and see practical command-line examples for common tasks such as dilation, erosion, opening, and closing.

Understanding the Octagon Kernel

Morphological operations modify shapes within an image based on a structural element, known as a kernel. Standard square (Square or Rectangle) kernels create blocky, 90-degree corners, while cross or diamond (Diamond) kernels produce sharp, 45-degree angles.

The Octagon kernel cuts off the corners of a square to form an eight-sided shape. It serves as an efficient discrete approximation of a circle (a Disk kernel), delivering rounded structural processing without the computational overhead or sub-pixel sampling complexities of true circular kernels.

Basic Syntax

In ImageMagick (version 7 uses magick, while version 6 uses convert), morphological operations are invoked using the -morphology operator:

magick input.png -morphology <Method> Octagon[:<radius>] output.png
  • <Method>: The morphological operation, such as Dilate, Erode, Open, Close, or Smooth.
  • Octagon: The built-in kernel shape.
  • [:<radius>]: An optional integer specifying the size of the kernel. A radius of 1 produces a 3×3 octagon (which removes the single corner pixels of a 3×3 square), radius 2 produces a 5×5 octagon, and larger radii expand the octagon outward.

Practical Examples

1. Dilating with Rounded Corners

Dilation expands the bright regions of an image. Using an Octagon kernel ensures that the expansion rounds off sharp exterior corners instead of turning them into squares:

magick mask.png -morphology Dilate Octagon:3 dilated_rounded.png

2. Eroding Features Uniformly

Erosion shrinks white areas and expands dark areas. Applying the Octagon kernel rounds internal corners and reduces thin projections without creating jagged, diamond-like edges:

magick mask.png -morphology Erode Octagon:2 eroded_rounded.png

3. Morphological Opening (Noise Removal)

Opening performs an erosion followed by a dilation. It removes foreground noise, specks, and thin protrusions that are smaller than the kernel size, leaving surviving shapes with smooth, rounded contours:

magick noisy_binary.png -morphology Open Octagon:4 cleaned_open.png

4. Morphological Closing (Hole Filling)

Closing performs a dilation followed by an erosion. It fills small holes and gaps between nearby objects while preserving a natural, curved boundary:

magick broken_shapes.png -morphology Close Octagon:5 closed_shapes.png

5. Shape Smoothing

To clean both internal holes and external roughness on a binary or grayscale mask, you can apply the Smooth method, which executes an Open operation immediately followed by a Close operation:

magick jagged_mask.png -morphology Smooth Octagon:3 smoothed.png

Octagon vs. Other Kernels

  • Square: Faster, but creates noticeable box artifacts and elongates corners diagonally.
  • Diamond: Simple, but strongly biases shapes toward 45-degree points.
  • Disk: Provides the most accurate rotational symmetry, but can be slower or require anti-aliasing thresholds.
  • Octagon: Strikes an optimal balance between processing speed and isotropic (rotation-independent) rounding on pixel grids.
